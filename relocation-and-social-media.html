relocation and social media

feb 1, 2018 · 20 min · with kyle welch · hosted by taylor desseyn

3 takeaways

  • > when relocating for a job, find a recruiter who knows the local market and can advocate for your best interest.
  • > beef up your online resume profiles since you wont have the benefit of local recognition when applying from out of state.
  • > build genuine relationships at meetups and conferences so companies eventually reach out to you instead of only applying cold.

about this episode

on this episode of guidance counselor 2.0, taylor desseyn talks with kyle welch: how to relocate for a tech job without a local network and build visibility through social media and community involvement.

being active on social media and github makes you discoverable to hiring teams and builds your reputation in tech. attending meetups and conferences helps you network with developers who may refer you to opportunities. showing up consistently in your field creates situations where companies approach you rather than you chasing them. being open to feedback and collaboration on code is valued by hiring managers and the dev community. stay active on twitter and github so hiring managers can see your passion and side projects outside of work hours. being open to feedback and able to disagree gracefully with other developers is a quality that helps you grow in your career.

In this episode I catch up with my good friend Kyle Welch. Kyle is a Sr. Engineer here in Nashville with Eventbrite. He and I go over the process of relocating from out of state and the effect social media has on your marketability in your local market.
