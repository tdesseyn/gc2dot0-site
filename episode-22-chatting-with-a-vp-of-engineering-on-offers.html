chatting with a vp of engineering on offers

feb 22, 2021 · 18 min · hosted by taylor desseyn

3 takeaways

  • > give a specific salary number instead of a vague range so hiring managers know where you actually stand.
  • > know your full compensation picture upfront, including benefits, bonus and 401k match, before entering negotiations.
  • > if a candidate asks for much more than expected, treat it as a signal to review your team's pay, not a red flag on the candidate.

about this episode

on this episode of guidance counselor 2.0, hosted by taylor desseyn: a vp of engineering discusses salary negotiation, compensation strategy, and culture fit at small startups.

negotiate your salary actively, candidates know their market value better than hiring managers do. come to negotiations prepared with specifics about benefits, bonuses, and 401k details. small companies have flexibility beyond base salary like equity, schedules, and performance bonuses. how you negotiate signals your communication skills and fit for the role and company culture. hiring managers at small companies view salary pushback as market data, not unreasonableness. come prepared with research and clear expectations, since that preparation signals how you will perform on the job. once you accept an offer, don't renege if something better comes along, since that damages trust with the employer.
