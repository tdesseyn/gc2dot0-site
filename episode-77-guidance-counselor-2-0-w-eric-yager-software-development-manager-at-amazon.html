3 takeaways

  • > put boot camp capstones and side projects on your resume framed as real experience, not hobby work.
  • > pick side projects that show full backend depth plus some frontend, since full stack work leans mostly backend.
  • > expect data structures and algorithms questions in big tech interviews even if you rarely use them day to day.

about this episode

on this episode of guidance counselor 2.0, taylor desseyn talks with eric yager: how to break into tech without a degree and strategies for getting hired at amazon and other major tech companies.

build demonstrable projects and professional experience through bootcamps or side work with startups to stand out on resumes. junior developers need strong data structures and algorithms knowledge plus ability to discuss leadership principles in interviews. practice talking about yourself and your experience repeatedly because interviewing is a skill that requires muscle memory. network with hiring managers on linkedin, get into target company in any role, then transition to software engineering internally. practice talking about yourself out loud since interview skills and keeping an interviewer engaged take real reps. if you cant land your target role at your target company, get in through an adjacent role and transfer internally later.
