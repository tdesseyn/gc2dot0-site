about this episode

on this episode of guidance counselor 2.0, taylor desseyn talks with sarah ray: how volt athletics built strong remote culture with four-day work weeks, autonomy, and personalized benefits instead of forced togetherness.

four-day work weeks increased productivity 97% by letting brains recover like athletes need rest intervals. remote culture thrives on choice and personalization, not forced events or mandated schedules. skip happy hours, give stipends instead so each person buys what actually helps them work better. opt-in activities like donut chats and trivia build connection without obligation or micromanaging. remote work attracted 400 applicants in two days and improved retention by giving people autonomy. let employees choose their schedule and location instead of forcing onsite days, since forced choices breed resistance and autonomy builds buy in.

In this episode, I'm joined by my dear friend Sarah Ray, Head of Business Development at Volt Athletics out of Seattle. We dive into how Volt has pivoted to being purely remote and how they are keeping up their culture!
