3 takeaways

  • > work with a recruiter for your first move from direct hire to contracting so you have someone guiding you through the client relationship.
  • > ask upfront whether a contract role is set at 40 hours a week or could require more.
  • > contracting means you get paid for every hour worked, unlike a direct hire salary where extra hours do not add pay.

about this episode

on this episode of guidance counselor 2.0, hosted by taylor desseyn: moving from direct hire to contracting: what to know about job security, pay, benefits, and working with recruiters.

talk to recruiters before contracting for the first time; they guide you and advocate at the client. contract work through recruiting firms is typically 40 hours per week and you get paid for every hour worked. direct hire jobs where you work over 40 hours reduce your effective hourly rate compared to contracting. leaving direct hire for contracting makes sense if you want to learn new tech and jump between clients. the tech job market is very active right now with many remote opportunities and low switching risk. when a contract is ending, most of the time there is notice in advance so you can plan your next move. the tech job market is strong right now, so if you want to try something new it is a safe time to make a change.
