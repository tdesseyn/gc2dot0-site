how to actually write a resume

aug 9, 2021 · 13 min · hosted by taylor desseyn

3 takeaways

  • > make your resume summary clear and concise with exact years of experience for each major skill so recruiters know what you do immediately.
  • > only bold company name, position, location, and year on your resume since heavy bolding distracts more than it helps.
  • > write professional experience as project based, describing each project at a high level like you would explain it to family.

about this episode

on this episode of guidance counselor 2.0, hosted by taylor desseyn: how to structure a resume with project-based accomplishments, technical skills, and a summary that tells recruiters exactly what you do.

professional experience needs project-based descriptions written at a high level, not long task lists without context. bold only company, position, location, state and year to avoid distracting the reader with too much formatting. technical skills section recaps everything you've touched that you want to be called about, separate from what you did day-to-day. cover letters should quantify accomplishments across your career in a few paragraphs, showing impact with numbers when possible. list the specific tech used to solve each project problem, then add a technologies used section recapping your full tech stack. put a cover letter in your own voice and make it quantitative about projects and results instead of repeating your resume.
