ep 12 · dec 18, 2020

practical tips you can implement to find a job

with taylor desseyn (solo)

  • before applying, bcc a few trusted recruiters on one email asking if they are working the role or know someone who is
  • keep a separate job search email so personal messages never get mixed up with hiring managers
  • track every application in a simple tool or spreadsheet with company, stage, and who submitted you
  • always know which recruiter submitted you where so you are not duplicated or left in the dark
  • use a scheduling link and ask for a short call like five minutes to make it easy for busy people to say yes

questions this episode answers

what does taylor desseyn say about using bcc when applying to jobs?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says before you submit your resume to any job posting, you should blind carbon copy three to five trusted recruiters on one email asking if they are working that role or know someone who is. he emphasizes that good recruiters should always be willing to make introductions and give you leads even if they are not working on something. this keeps you from being duplicated and helps you get insider connections.

what tracking system does taylor desseyn recommend for job applications?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn recommends using a tool or spreadsheet to track every application with columns for company name, interview stage (phone interview, second round, third round, offer, acceptance), and who submitted you. he mentions a tool called huntr that was recommended to him, or asana for simple tracking. he stresses that job seekers need to keep up with which recruiter submitted them where, calling it lazy not to track this information.

how does taylor desseyn suggest asking for networking calls?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn recommends using calendly to set up a scheduling link and asking for very short calls like three and a half to five minutes instead of fifteen minutes. he says asking for oddly specific short timeframes like one minute and forty-five seconds makes you stand out as funny and specific, and busy people are much more likely to say yes to a brief call. he jokes that no one wants to talk for fifteen minutes but he can give three and a half minutes.
