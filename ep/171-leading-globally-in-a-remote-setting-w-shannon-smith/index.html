ep 171 · aug 22, 2022

leading globally in a remote setting w/ shannon smith

guest: shannon smith, vp of engineering at ona

  • keep remote team conversations organized by using dedicated channels for specific squads or groups instead of one big chat
  • build in informal time like a 15 minute coffee chat before standups so remote teammates can bond before diving into work
  • when planning in person meetups, give people advance notice so they can plan around family and other obligations
  • set up a buddy program for new hires so they have a safe person to ask basic questions without feeling judged
  • on your resume or linkedin, dont just list technical skills, explain what makes you uniquely valuable compared to other candidates

questions this episode answers

what does shannon smith say about keeping remote slack conversations organized?

on guidance counselor 2.0, shannon smith says ona keeps slack conversations organized by dedicating channels to specific squads and larger groups like the whole dev team or product team. he mentions being a heavy thread user to keep discussions in the right place. the goal is to avoid killing people with too many channels while still maintaining clear, topic-specific communication spaces.

how does shannon smith build informal time into remote meetings?

on guidance counselor 2.0, shannon smith explains his teams have a 15 minute coffee period before their 15 minute standup. the first 15 minutes is not required, but the video call is open for people to jump in and chat casually about their weekends or whatever is going on before jumping into work. he also mentions monday morning 8:30 kickoffs where the team mostly just talks about non-work topics to start the week.

what does shannon smith recommend about planning in-person meetups for remote teams?

on guidance counselor 2.0, shannon smith says to plan in-person meetups far enough in advance so people can orient their schedules around family and other obligations. he notes that when meetups happen in your hometown it is convenient, but when you add mandatory dinners and events it is hard for people with kids or other commitments. giving advance notice helps people with families or other responsibilities prepare and participate.
