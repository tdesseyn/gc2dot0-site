thoughts around hiring senior & staff engineers w/ dir of eng, harry wolff

guest: harry wolff, director of engineering, identity and access management at mongodb

  • get as clear as possible on what you want from a role before you start a senior or staff search
  • treat job requirements as two buckets, technical skills and non technical traits like independent thinking and collaboration
  • use pragmatic interview questions tied to real day to day work instead of memorization style puzzles
  • always leave time at the end of interviews for candidate questions since that signals real curiosity and interest
  • cold emailing a hiring manager rarely works, a referral or just applying through the recruiting team is the better path

questions this episode answers

what does harry wolff say hiring managers should do before starting a senior or staff engineer search?

on guidance counselor 2.0, harry wolff, director of engineering at mongodb, says hiring managers should get as clear as possible on what they want from the role before starting a senior or staff search. he compares it to knowing what you want in a life partner and says clarity makes everything easier because you have a baseline to evaluate candidates. he recommends collaborating with peers and your team to align on expectations and says clarity should evolve through conversations and iterations.

how does harry wolff structure job requirements for senior and staff engineer roles?

on guidance counselor 2.0, harry wolff, director of engineering at mongodb, says he looks at all roles through two lenses: technical requirements and non-technical requirements. he avoids calling them soft skills because that's too soft a statement. technical requirements include things like five plus years of react or understanding distributed backend systems, while non-technical requirements cover traits like being an independent curious thinker, able to write architecture documents and collaborate successfully across teams.

what interviewing approach does harry wolff recommend for senior and staff engineers?

on guidance counselor 2.0, harry wolff, director of engineering at mongodb, says he uses pragmatic interview questions tied to real day-to-day work instead of memorization-style leetcode puzzles. he prefers pair programming exercises to see how candidates think, collaborate and talk through problems, and he always leaves at least ten minutes at the end for candidate questions. he writes down what candidates ask because curiosity and interest are part of the interview evaluation.
