guidance counselor 2.0 w/eric yager, software development manager at amazon

guest: eric yager, software development manager at amazon

  • put boot camp capstones and side projects on your resume framed as real experience, not hobby work
  • pick side projects that show full backend depth plus some frontend, since full stack work leans mostly backend
  • expect data structures and algorithms questions in big tech interviews even if you rarely use them day to day
  • practice talking about yourself out loud since interview skills and keeping an interviewer engaged take real reps
  • if you cant land your target role at your target company, get in through an adjacent role and transfer internally later

what does eric yager say about putting bootcamp projects on a resume?

on guidance counselor 2.0, eric yager, software development manager at amazon, says bootcamp capstone projects should be noted on your resume as professional experience, not side projects or hobby work. he explains that a lot of developers spend only 20 percent of their time hands-on coding, so bootcamp graduates often code more than that and should treat their capstone work as real professional experience. he also recommends taking actual side projects with startups or anyone with an idea to build a demonstrable portfolio that shows you can write production code.
