how to actually write a resume

with taylor desseyn (solo)

  • make your resume summary clear and concise with exact years of experience for each major skill so recruiters know what you do immediately
  • only bold company name, position, location, and year on your resume since heavy bolding distracts more than it helps
  • write professional experience as project based, describing each project at a high level like you would explain it to family
  • list the specific tech used to solve each project problem, then add a technologies used section recapping your full tech stack
  • put a cover letter in your own voice and make it quantitative about projects and results instead of repeating your resume

questions this episode answers

what does taylor desseyn say should be in a resume summary section?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says the summary section should be clear bullet points listing exactly what you do with years of experience for each major skill. he recommends including years for languages, frameworks and other key technologies plus your degree, so a recruiter immediately sees something like ten years of dot net, four years of angular, two years of azure and a computer science degree. if you are a junior developer with six months of coding bootcamp experience, put that six months in the summary.

how does taylor desseyn recommend formatting the professional experience section on a resume?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says only bold the company name, position, location and year at the top of each job entry, because heavy bolding distracts readers instead of helping them. he says to write experience project by project, starting each project with a high-level summary explaining what you were tasked to do as if talking to family. then list bullet points showing the specific technologies used to solve each part of the problem, and finish with a technologies used section that recaps your entire tech stack for that job.

what does taylor desseyn say a cover letter should include?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says a cover letter should be written in your own voice and be very quantitative about projects and accomplishments you want to highlight. he recommends taking the seven or eight projects from your career and cramming them into a few paragraphs, focusing on things like how many users a project served or specific technical achievements. he says cover letters should not repeat your resume but instead summarize the impact of your work across your career in a bragging way.
