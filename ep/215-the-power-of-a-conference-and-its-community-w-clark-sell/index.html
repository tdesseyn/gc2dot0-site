the power of a conference and it's community w/clark sell, founder & ceo of 'that conference"

guest: clark sell, founder & ceo of that conference

  • showing up is most of the battle, so register, attend sessions, and put yourself in the room
  • volunteer at a conference, especially registration, because it puts you in front of everyone
  • invest a little money in yourself through courses or conference tickets, it pays off over time
  • after meeting someone at an event, wait a few days before reaching out so you stand out from the first-day flood of messages
  • don't be afraid to sit at a different table or approach strangers, almost everyone there wants to connect too

questions this episode answers

what does clark sell recommend for first-time conference attendees who want to network?

on guidance counselor 2.0, clark sell, founder and ceo of that conference, says showing up is 80 percent of the battle because not many people actually attend. he recommends volunteering at registration because you will meet every single attendee. he also suggests sitting at different tables with strangers, not just people you already know, and asking the organizer ahead of time how you can help or get the most out of the event.

how long should you wait before following up with someone you met at a conference, according to clark sell?

on guidance counselor 2.0, clark sell suggests waiting five days before reaching out after meeting someone at a conference. he explains that if you reach out the same day or the next day, your message will likely get lost in the inbox with a thousand other people. waiting a few days helps you stand out and increases the chance your message will be seen.

what does clark sell say about investing money in your own career development?

on guidance counselor 2.0, clark sell tells tech workers to stop being cheap and invest a little cash in themselves, such as spending a hundred bucks on a course or a conference ticket. he points out that tech workers are already in the top five to ten percent of earners in the united states, so investing small amounts can help them grow exponentially and go really far, really fast.
