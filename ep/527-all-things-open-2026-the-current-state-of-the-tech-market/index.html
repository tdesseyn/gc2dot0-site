all things open 2026 - the current state of the tech market

with taylor desseyn (solo)

  • treat ai fluency as a baseline skill now, not a nice-to-have, across tech and non-tech roles alike
  • stop reading from chatgpt scripts in interviews, interviewers can tell and it kills trust and credibility
  • prepare with the star method as a thinking framework, not a memorized script, to keep answers clear under pressure
  • network constantly since a large share of hires trace back to referrals, so build real human connections at events
  • contribute to open source and write about what you build, visible work and thought leadership gets you noticed by hiring managers

questions this episode answers

what does taylor say about networking versus just applying to jobs?

on guidance counselor 2.0, taylor desseyn says only 7 percent of applications are referrals, yet 30 to 50 percent of all hires come from those referrals. this means if 1,000 people apply to a job, 70 are referrals but they account for half the hires. he argues your next opportunity is sitting in the room at meetups and conferences, so you should open linkedin and try to connect with every person before you leave.

what does the panel say about whether ai literacy is now required for tech jobs?

on guidance counselor 2.0, bobby mcdill says many job descriptions now list ai literacy in the requirements section, not just for engineers but for financial analysts, project managers and mechanical engineers across all industries. he says hiring managers want to know how candidates are using ai and are looking for intellectual curiosity, not necessarily expertise. day kersweet from dell says ai fluency is like excel, no longer a differentiator but a baseline workplace skill that's expected now.
