nashville technology council panel-part 2

with taylor desseyn (solo)

  • know your one to three non-negotiables before you start a search so you can judge if an offer is worth taking
  • target two networking meetings every single week to keep your pipeline full like a sales funnel
  • leave past bad job experiences at the door in interviews since coming across bitter is never a good look

questions this episode answers

what does taylor desseyn say about deciding which job offers to accept?

on this guidance counselor 2.0 nashville technology council panel, taylor desseyn says to write down one to three non-negotiables before you start your job search, like distance or pay. if a job offer meets those one to three things, you should take it even if your lower priority wants are not met. right now you need to deploy empathy and recognize the tough market means you may have to accept something that does not check every box.

how often should you network during a job search, according to paige?

on this guidance counselor 2.0 panel, paige (a corporate recruiter) says you should target two networking meetings every single week during your job search. she compares it to a sales funnel where the more opportunities and connections you put in at the top, the more likely the right one will come out at the bottom. it is a numbers game.

what does taylor say junior developers should do to stand out when looking for their first job?

on this guidance counselor 2.0 panel, taylor desseyn says a demo project from coding school is not enough anymore because it only puts you in line with everybody else. to separate yourself, start asking for free work and take on three or more personal projects like building a website for a church, a friend's gardening club, or an anime club. he would call the candidate with three personal projects over someone with only bootcamp experience.
