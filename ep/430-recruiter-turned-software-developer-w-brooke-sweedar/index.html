recruiter turned software developer w/ brooke sweedar

what brooke sweedar covers in this episode

brooke sweedar spent about a decade as an executive recruiter in manufacturing, not tech. after a long stretch out of work, she decided she was done with recruiting and, since she'd be starting from scratch either way, made the drastic switch: a six month full stack web development bootcamp at fullstack academy. she calls it a very positive experience. along the way she went from about 180 linkedin connections in february 2024 to more than 18,000 followers by the time of this episode, and became a content creator and podcast host. this conversation is a tactical breakdown of how she networked her way into tech.

start networking during the bootcamp, not after

brooke started actively networking about three weeks into her bootcamp, because she didn't want to graduate and still not know anyone. she says that's a very common experience for bootcamp grads and career switchers, and her advice to them is simple: don't wait.

her method was specific. she used linkedin search to find local software engineers in baton rouge, louisiana, because sharing a location gave her common ground as a stranger. then she expanded to fullstack academy alumni across the us, since she had the bootcamp in common with them. she sent personalized notes introducing herself as a bootcamp student looking to connect, and after getting linkedin premium, she was sending about 50 invites a day. her first goal was just reaching 500 connections.

post what you're learning, even if it's small

brooke started posting about what she was learning, including what she calls her crappy little calculator app. she felt shy about it, partly because she was watching experienced tech professionals and only seeing their finished product. but the comments came, and so did private messages from experienced people offering encouragement and introductions, without her asking for anything. she says any interviews she's had came from organic networking, not job boards.

how to prep for a coffee chat

her first coffee chat terrified her. her advice: do a little homework so you don't waste someone's time, and have a few questions ready. people like to talk about themselves, so ask what they like about their company, how their experience has been, or what other bootcamp grads would do differently. she learned a lot that way.

pick one language and go deep

on what to learn, brooke says there are so many languages, frameworks and opinions that it's easy to confuse activity with productivity. surface level tutorials on 20 different things won't get you far. pick one language, stick with it until you understand the fundamentals and can build real things, then expand outward. watch trends, but don't spread yourself too thin.

she and taylor also talk about how even so-called entry level roles often ask for three to five years of experience. brooke has leaned away from job boards and further into her network and content, which led to new conversations.

don't get in your own way

her advice to herself before she left recruiting: don't get in your own way. she says, especially for women, betting on yourself and trusting that you're equipped to take the risk is worth it. it's scary, but do it.

questions this episode answers

what does brooke sweedar say about when to start networking during a bootcamp?

on guidance counselor 2.0, brooke sweedar says bootcamp students should start networking three weeks into their program, not after graduation. she started by searching linkedin for local software engineers and bootcamp alumni to build connections while still learning. waiting until after graduation means starting your job search as a stranger with no network, which is what 90 percent of bootcamp grads do.

how did brooke sweedar get her first 500 linkedin connections in tech with no tech background?

on guidance counselor 2.0, brooke sweedar says she sent about 50 personalized connection requests per day after getting linkedin premium. she started by targeting local software engineers because they shared location common ground, then expanded to full stack academy alumni nationwide since they shared the bootcamp experience. her first goal was simply reaching 500 connections, and she went from 180 connections to 18,000 followers in about a year.

what does brooke sweedar recommend preparing before coffee chats with engineers?

on guidance counselor 2.0, brooke sweedar says to do a little homework and prepare genuine questions before coffee chats so you do not waste people's time. she points out that people naturally enjoy talking about themselves, so ask about their experience at their company, their bootcamp journey, or startup versus big tech tradeoffs. she learned more from one-on-one conversations than from any article or youtube video.
