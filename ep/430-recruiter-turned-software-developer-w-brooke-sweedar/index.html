recruiter turned software developer w/ brooke sweedar

guest: brooke sweedar, software engineer and bootcamp graduate, former executive recruiter

  • start networking before you finish your bootcamp so you already have connections by graduation
  • send personalized dms to local software engineers and bootcamp alumni to build common ground fast
  • posting your learning journey publicly, even small projects, can attract support and opportunities from experienced professionals

  • pick one programming language and build real projects before spreading yourself across many frameworks or tools

questions this episode answers

what does brooke sweedar say about when to start networking during a bootcamp?

on guidance counselor 2.0, brooke sweedar says bootcamp students should start networking three weeks into their program, not after graduation. she started by searching linkedin for local software engineers and bootcamp alumni to build connections while still learning. waiting until after graduation means starting your job search as a stranger with no network, which is what 90 percent of bootcamp grads do.

how did brooke sweedar get her first 500 linkedin connections in tech with no tech background?

on guidance counselor 2.0, brooke sweedar says she sent about 50 personalized connection requests per day after getting linkedin premium. she started by targeting local software engineers because they shared location common ground, then expanded to full stack academy alumni nationwide since they shared the bootcamp experience. her first goal was simply reaching 500 connections, and she went from 180 connections to 18,000 followers in about a year.

what does brooke sweedar recommend preparing before coffee chats with engineers?

on guidance counselor 2.0, brooke sweedar says to do a little homework and prepare genuine questions before coffee chats so you do not waste people's time. she points out that people naturally enjoy talking about themselves, so ask about their experience at their company, their bootcamp journey, or startup versus big tech tradeoffs. she learned more from one-on-one conversations than from any article or youtube video.
