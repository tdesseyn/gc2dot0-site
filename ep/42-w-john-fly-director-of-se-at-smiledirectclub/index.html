guest: john fly, director of se at smiledirectclub

  • define your company culture early, waiting until you feel the need for it usually means you are already in trouble
  • avoid silos by making sure every new hire understands the company mission, not just their job role

questions this episode answers

what does john fly say about when to define company culture in a startup?

on guidance counselor 2.0, john fly says it is rarely too early to define company culture, and by the time you realize you need it, you are probably already in a bad place. he explains that not defining what you want your culture to be early and not building it into your hiring practice can lead to chaos. startups need to establish clear cultural boundaries before rapid hiring phases, or they risk bringing in people who optimize for their individual roles instead of the company mission.

how does john fly recommend avoiding silos as a company grows?

on guidance counselor 2.0, john fly recommends making sure every new hire understands the value they bring to the entire organization, not just their job role. he says when people do not understand their value to the organization, they start optimizing their behavior for their job role instead of helping the company succeed. companies should have a clear statement about what type of company they are and make sure hires believe in the company, not just fit the technical requirements.

what does john fly say hiring managers should do instead of using canned job descriptions?

on guidance counselor 2.0, john fly says hiring managers should skip the canned job description and really give thought to who they are hiring and why the position exists. he says even for entry level roles, you should know why you are hiring that person and be able to speak to it in the interview. managers should also build a standardized interview process so that every candidate is vetted through the same steps and evaluated on a level playing field.
