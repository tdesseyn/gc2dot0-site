a letter to recruiters w/head of devrel at snap, tessa kriesel

guest: tessa kriesel, head of devrel at snap

  • developers think differently, so recruiters should do a little research, offer some value, and only then bring up what they're looking for
  • tessa's three steps for reaching developers: find where they hang out online, open with something of value to them, then make your ask clear
  • if you don't know the tech, ask developers what they're building and learning, most want to share what they're passionate about
  • if someone's social media is clearly personal, don't comment on it for a recruiting reason, use what you learn to shape your outreach instead
  • devrel builds the trust and community developers need, so it should feed leads to sales but never be turned into the sales team

questions this episode answers

what is tessa kriesel's three-step approach for recruiters reaching out to developers?

on guidance counselor 2.0, tessa kriesel, head of devrel at snap, says recruiters should first do their research: find where the developer hangs out online and check whether they actually fit the role. next, the outreach should give them something of value, like pointing out something they care about or offering an introduction to someone they should meet. only after that small relationship is built should you come back with the role or the ask, and it does not have to take long, since many developers don't enjoy small talk.

what should recruiters do if they don't understand the tech a developer works on?

tessa kriesel says to ask. developers and engineers like to build cool stuff, so give them the chance to share it by asking what they are building, what they are working on and what they are learning. she says they usually want to talk about what they are passionate about if given the opportunity, so skip random small talk and ask about their work.

why does tessa kriesel say devrel teams should not be used as salespeople?

tessa kriesel says developers are slow to trust, and it usually takes four to five touch points before a developer trusts you. devrel builds that trust and community, and its people become the face of the brand, so turning them into salespeople means they can no longer build trust authentically. she says devrel does contribute to revenue and should pass leads to the sales team, but sales should talk through those leads rather than cold outreach to them.
