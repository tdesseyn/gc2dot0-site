ep 121 · mar 4, 2022

startups and leveraging junior talent w/ haley zapolski

guest: haley zapolski, manager of employer engagement at nashville software school

  • ask detailed questions about work from home policy before accepting an offer since flexible can still mean three days in office
  • nail down how much time youll spend coding versus internal meetings versus client calls before taking a job
  • if you cant think of a question in an interview ask one anyway since having none signals you dont care
  • before accepting an offer get on another call with questions and see how willing the company is to invest in you
  • companies struggling to hire senior developers are increasingly building programs to train junior and bootcamp talent instead

questions this episode answers

what does haley zapolski say about asking questions when you get a job offer?

on guidance counselor 2.0, haley zapolski says candidates should schedule additional calls with questions after receiving an offer and before accepting. she believes a company's willingness to have those extra conversations reveals how much they will invest in you once you become an employee. she personally had two or three more calls after getting her offer before accepting.

what should junior developers ask about before accepting a job according to haley zapolski?

on guidance counselor 2.0, haley zapolski says junior developers must nail down specifics about remote work policy because flexible can still mean three days in the office. they should also clarify what percentage of time they will spend on actual development work versus internal team meetings versus client-facing calls. she says mismanaged expectations on these points lead to unsuccessful tenure because people show up surprised by the reality.

why are companies starting to hire junior developers now according to haley zapolski?

on guidance counselor 2.0, haley zapolski says companies that wanted to hire four senior developers often can only get two because the market is so tight. when they cannot find mid to senior level talent, they are creating programs to hire and train junior developers instead. she notes this is becoming a necessity because four-year computer science programs are not producing talent at the rate the industry needs.
