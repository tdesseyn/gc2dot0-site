ep 130 · apr 4, 2022

the secret sauce for hiring and job seekers w/ david cabal

guest: david cabal, engineering manager at exodus

  • pay candidates upfront for take home code challenges to show you value their time
  • weigh soft skills and humility as heavily as technical skill since attitude affects how someone takes feedback on a team
  • keep hiring moving fast by reviewing code challenges within 24 to 48 hours and letting candidates pick the next interview slot
  • stay laser focused on the delivery date and push non critical bugs and extra features to the backlog instead of chasing perfection
  • as a candidate come with prepared questions for the interviewer since most people skip this and it instantly sets you apart

questions this episode answers

what does david cabal say about paying candidates for take home coding challenges?

on guidance counselor 2.0, david cabal, engineering manager at exodus, says his company pays every candidate upfront for code challenges, sending payment in bitcoin after an initial phone screen. he emphasizes paying upfront shows respect for candidate time, and despite concerns about being scammed, candidates almost never ghost after receiving payment. the practice helps attract talent and signals the company values people.

how does david cabal test for humility during interviews?

on guidance counselor 2.0, david cabal says he intentionally looks for things to criticize in a candidate's code challenge to gauge how they react to critique. he watches whether the person becomes defensive or accepts feedback graciously, because handling criticism well predicts how they will respond to code reviews and team feedback. he values a decent developer who is humble over a brilliant developer with too much pride.

what does david cabal recommend candidates do at the end of an interview?

on guidance counselor 2.0, david cabal says candidates should come prepared with questions for the interviewer, ideally written down on a list. most people skip this step, so having thoughtful questions instantly sets a candidate apart and shows they are excited about the role. if a candidate reaches the end of an interview with no questions, it is not a good look and signals lack of preparation or interest.
