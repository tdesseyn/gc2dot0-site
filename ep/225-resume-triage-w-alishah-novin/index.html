resume triage w/alishah novin

guest: alishah novin, resume reviewer on the episode's resume triage segment

  • put your tech stack and technical experience above personal projects so hiring managers see your strongest skills first
  • always include months and years for every job, not just years, or it looks like you are hiding something
  • write resume bullets around accomplishments and impact, not just daily duties, since recruiters already know the basic job description
  • use numbers and comparisons whenever possible, like how your results stack up against an average or goal, not just raw stats alone
  • keep your resume format clean and scannable since hiring managers skim fast and a dense or dark page gets set aside

questions this episode answers

what does alishah novin say about where to put technical experience versus projects on a resume?

on guidance counselor 2.0, alishah novin says technical experience should be placed above personal projects on your resume because if you have real-world technical experience, that is far more valuable to hiring managers than projects. projects are still important and should be included, but professional experience takes priority in the order of your resume sections.

why do taylor desseyn and alishah novin say you should always include months and years for every job?

on guidance counselor 2.0, taylor desseyn and alishah novin agree you must include month and year for every position, not just years, because if they do not see the months, hiring managers will assume you are hiding something like job hopping or short tenure. both say this transparency is critical because the absence of months raises immediate red flags during the resume screening process.

what does taylor desseyn say about writing resume bullets around accomplishments instead of duties?

on guidance counselor 2.0, taylor desseyn says your resume bullets should focus on accomplishments and impact, not just daily duties, because recruiters and hiring managers already know what the basic job description entails. he explains that every bullet should answer the question of what specific impact you made or what you accomplished, not simply list tasks like collaborated or utilized without explaining the outcome or purpose.
