job search 101 + solo show

with taylor desseyn (solo)

  • if you have to take a customer service job to pay the bills, keep coding or building on the side so you have something to show later
  • attention to detail matters more than ever, a typo or small mistake can get you rejected right now
  • if you cannot land a technical role, look at customer success, marketing, or support roles inside tech companies as a way in
  • before an interview, actually use the company's product or tool so you can speak to it instead of just talking about your own background

questions this episode answers

what does taylor desseyn say about taking a non-technical job while searching for a developer role?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says if you need to take a customer service or other non-technical job to pay bills, you should accept it but keep coding on the side. he emphasizes you must have something to show later, because he's currently counseling someone who hasn't coded in two years and that creates a real problem. taking care of yourself and your family comes first, but don't stop building.

how does taylor desseyn recommend following up with recruiters during a job search?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says you need to follow up persistently and not worry about being annoying. he shares that after his own interview, the ceo told him to check back in a month, but taylor checked back in a week instead and got the offer because things move too fast in business. he tells job seekers to message recruiters three or four times if needed, being kind but acting like a vulture when it comes to follow-up.

what does taylor desseyn say about using a company's product before interviewing?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says you should always try to use the tool or product of the company before your interview. he explains that if a software developer built something specifically to help his content business and showed it in the interview, he would immediately focus on that candidate. he warns that just talking about what you've done in the past without connecting it to the company's needs doesn't work as well.
