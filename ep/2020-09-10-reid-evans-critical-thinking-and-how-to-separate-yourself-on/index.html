reid evans + critical thinking and how to separate yourself on the job search

guest: reid evans, principal consultant at results stack

  • position yourself as someone who solves problems instead of chasing a specific job title or w2 role
  • in interviews, focus on communicating clearly and asking good questions rather than proving you are the best coder in the room
  • recruiters should ask what projects a company has going on instead of just asking about open reqs for a specific tech stack
  • a resume should tell a hiring manager what you do in 15 seconds, skip fancy fonts, columns, and headshots
  • your most recent job or two should have real detail since that is what hiring managers focus on most

questions this episode answers

what does reid evans say about how to position yourself in the job market right now?

on guidance counselor 2.0, reid evans says people should position themselves as someone who solves problems instead of chasing a specific w2 job title. he explains that many companies aren't hiring full-time right now but still have business problems that need solving, and consultants who can provide value are turning down work because there's so much demand. evans emphasizes that once you solve a problem for someone, they'll bring you more problems to solve.

what does reid evans recommend developers focus on during interviews instead of proving technical ability?

on guidance counselor 2.0, reid evans says he doesn't ask candidates to write code or solve fizzbuzz in interviews because he values communication and problem-solving over being the best coder. evans explains that as a consultant, being able to talk to business people and ask good questions about why they want something is more important than technical depth. he would rather hire someone from a customer-service role like a mobile phone store who is technically inclined but has great communication skills than someone who only knows how to code.

what does reid evans say recruiters should ask hiring managers instead of asking about specific tech stack openings?

on guidance counselor 2.0, reid evans and taylor desseyn agree that recruiters should ask what projects a company has going on instead of only asking if they have openings for a specific technology. evans confirms this approach makes more sense because a company might have six busy projects in javascript even if they don't have a dotnet opening, and candidates from similar projects could be a better fit than someone with the exact tech stack.
