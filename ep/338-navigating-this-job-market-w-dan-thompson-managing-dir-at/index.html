what dan thompson covers in this episode

dan thompson is a managing director at vaco in tampa, where he's worked for about ten and a half years, after getting into recruiting about twelve years before this episode. he helps companies find talent, mostly senior to executive level it roles, along with staff and project based work. this conversation, which took place as 2023 was wrapping up, is a recruiter's read on the tech job market and what candidates should do differently in it.

the market: flat salaries, fewer middle managers

dan calls 2023 a rough year for tech: lots of layoffs, little growth, and consolidation inside it departments. what stood out to him was a middle manager and senior level recession, with managers, leads and directors getting let go, not just juniors going unhired. he did say the three months before this episode had been stronger.

on pay, he doesn't expect a drop so much as a flat line. salaries saw a large jump from 2021 to 2022 and another decent jump into 2023, then flattened. he didn't see much wage growth over the next year, probably two, and noticed companies holding back performance bonuses. his view: it couldn't be a rocket ship forever.

if you're at the top of the range, explain why

taylor asks about people who've been out of work for months without adjusting their expectations. dan's main point is that if you're asking for the upper end of a salary range, you have to be able to explain why. what you were paid before isn't enough. plenty of developers went from around $75,000 or $80,000 to $130,000 in a few years when companies were desperate to hire, and that changes how you're judged. you're now evaluated as a $130,000 developer, not as someone with five years of experience.

he also walks through why hiring is expensive. someone paid $100,000 might cost a company about $130,000 after benefits, plus the time other people spend training and ramping them up. in a tougher economy, companies want people who can fit in and help right away. so before an interview, really learn what the company does, its products and services, not just its website, and connect your experience to how you could help now.

advice for junior developers

dan's advice to juniors is to get real experience outside the classroom or bootcamp. look for startup incubators, hackathons and local meetups. many startups need help, can't pay much, and don't always need deeply technical work, so offering to help on a small project can be a real start. committing to github counts too.

his other tip is about who to ask for advice. many juniors email an application development manager, who may not have time to meet every junior for 30 minutes. other juniors, mid-level and senior developers rarely get asked for advice, because they can't buy anything and aren't recruiters. ask them how they got their foot in the door. the barrier is much lower.

be purposeful, and enjoy the journey

dan's wrap-up: be purposeful with your job search. know what kinds of companies you want, and when you land an interview, dig into what they do and where your experience fits. his advice to his younger self was to enjoy the journey. it's fine to want the next promotion, but if you're laser focused on it you miss relationships and experience, and it's okay to fail and ask what you learned.

questions this episode answers

what does dan thompson say about salary growth in 2024?

on guidance counselor 2.0, dan thompson, managing director at vaco, says he does not see much wage growth over the next year or probably the next two years. salaries saw large jumps from 2021 to 2022 and again from 2022 to 2023, but 2023 has shown flatlining. companies are also withholding performance bonuses this year based on company performance.

how does dan thompson recommend justifying a high salary request?

on guidance counselor 2.0, dan thompson, managing director at vaco, says if you are negotiating near the top of a salary range, you need to explain why you are worth it, not just point to what you were paid before. you need to show what you bring to the table and how you can help the company, especially since hiring a new employee costs about 130 percent of their salary after benefits and training time.

what is dan thompson's advice for junior developers trying to break in?

on guidance counselor 2.0, dan thompson, managing director at vaco, tells junior developers to build real experience through startup incubators, hackathons, local meetups, or by helping startups that need technical work but cannot afford much. he also recommends reaching out to other junior, mid, and senior developers for advice instead of only targeting managers and directors, since those people get asked less often and have more time to help.
