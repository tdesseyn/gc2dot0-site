ep 443 · apr 18, 2025

advice from a hiring manager w/ eng manager dan digangi

guest: dan digangi, engineering manager at active campaign

  • treat freelance work like a real job on your resume, with specific projects and details, not vague summaries
  • going from leadership back to an ic role is a lateral move, not a step down, if it fits your skills and goals
  • long employment gaps matter less if you can explain them clearly, like taking time off intentionally
  • use ai tools to research companies and competitors before interviews, not just to write for you
  • read your resume or important writing out loud before sending it, it helps you catch what doesn't flow

questions this episode answers

what does dan digangi say about moving from a leadership role back to an individual contributor position?

on guidance counselor 2.0, dan digangi says moving from leadership to an individual contributor role is not a step down but a lateral move. he explains that at the senior level, you're deciding which path to take—management or ic—and then moving forward from there. he notes there's nothing wrong with switching paths, though it can be a reset in some ways and you need to understand the time component.

how should freelance work be written on a resume according to dan digangi?

on guidance counselor 2.0, dan digangi and taylor desseyn say you need to write each freelance project and client like it's an actual full-time job. they note that many developers summarize freelance work vaguely instead of detailing specific projects with the same level of detail as regular employment. dan adds that freelance work is real experience and should be formatted that way on your resume, regardless of whether you were paid.

what does dan digangi recommend about using ai tools for job searching?

on guidance counselor 2.0, dan digangi recommends using ai tools as a research tool when job searching, specifically to learn about companies, their competitors, and the space they work in. he emphasizes using ai for productivity and learning, not to shortcut your work or cheat. dan personally uses claude across his work and life and says it has been the best productivity boost he's had in the past few years.
