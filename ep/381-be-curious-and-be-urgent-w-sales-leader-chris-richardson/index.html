questions this episode answers

what does chris richardson say about practicing your elevator pitch?

on guidance counselor 2.0, chris richardson says you should sit down with your camera on and practice different versions of your elevator pitch until you can vary it depending on the audience and setting. he emphasizes that you need to be clear and concise about who you are and what you want people to know about you in 30 seconds. practice helps you pivot at the drop of a dime whether you're talking to someone casual at a networking event or doing a formal interview.

how does chris richardson recommend reaching out to people on linkedin?

on guidance counselor 2.0, chris richardson says you should never send lazy, generic messages asking for help. instead, research the person's profile, their talks, or their background and find a commonality or something specific that resonates with you. he suggests mentioning a ted talk they gave or a shared connection to show you've done your homework. people will quickly spot a chat gpt template, so make each message unique and germane to that specific person.

what does chris richardson say about job descriptions and requirements?

on guidance counselor 2.0, chris richardson says job descriptions are like dating profiles with unrealistic requirements, such as ten years of experience in a technology that has only existed for five years. he tells people not to pay too much attention to the listed requirements and instead talk to people who have actually done the hiring at those companies to learn what really sets candidates apart. the job description is simply a wish list, not a barrier.
