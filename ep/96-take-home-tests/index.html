ep 96 · oct 27, 2021

take home tests

with taylor desseyn (solo)

  • long take home tests hurt retention more than they help, especially when candidates already have jobs and families
  • ask better interview questions instead of leaning on hours of take home code tests to evaluate someone
  • live coding or screen share problem solving together is a fair way to assess skills without a take home burden
  • keep the process tight, aim for around three rounds within two weeks with fast turnaround on scheduling and feedback
  • build experience by volunteering a few hours a week on a real project and adding that work to your resume

questions this episode answers

what does taylor desseyn say about take-home coding tests in interviews?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn argues that take-home coding tests hurt retention more than they help because candidates already have jobs and families and cannot spend fifteen hours on multiple two to three hour tests. he says companies that skip take-home tests and instead have organic technical conversations during interviews have better success keeping and retaining talent. taylor is fine with live coding or screen-sharing problem solving during an interview call, but opposes separate take-home assignments.

how many interview rounds does taylor desseyn recommend for hiring?

on guidance counselor 2.0, taylor desseyn recommends three rounds within two weeks total. he suggests hiring managers schedule the first round within forty-eight hours of resume submission, schedule a group round within seventy-two hours after the first round, then provide feedback and schedule one final round within twenty-four hours if needed. he criticizes companies with seven rounds as having too many people who feel their opinion must be heard.

how should career changers build experience according to taylor desseyn?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn recommends volunteering one to five hours per week on a real project for a nonprofit or small business that needs development work, then adding that experience to your resume. he gives an example of a junior developer he worked with who took on building a website for a coffee shop for free so he could add angular and node experience to his resume.
