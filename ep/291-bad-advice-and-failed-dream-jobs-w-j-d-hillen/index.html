ep 291 · nov 8, 2023

bad advice and failed dream jobs w/j.d. hillen

guest: j.d. hillen, developer and speaker at garmin

  • get good at explaining short job stints, especially in volatile industries like advertising where staffing up and down for clients is normal
  • before quitting a job ask if the problem is industry-wide or specific to your company, since you will likely face industry problems again elsewhere
  • landing your dream job does not guarantee happiness, so check in with yourself or a trusted person on whether you actually feel proud going to work
  • build real connections by reaching out directly to people at conferences or companies you want to join, even if you start as a total stranger to them
  • keep working toward a goal even after early rejection, since skills built over years can make you qualified for a role later

questions this episode answers

what does j.d. hillen say about explaining short job stints on your resume?

on guidance counselor 2.0, j.d. hillen says he got very good at explaining nine-month to one-and-a-half-year stints in advertising by clarifying whether each was a contract gig or tied to a specific client. the advertising industry is volatile, with companies staffing up for clients and laying off workers when contracts end, so hiring managers in that industry understand the pattern. he learned to frame his history so it did not look like he was just jumping ship.

how does j.d. hillen recommend deciding whether to leave a job?

on guidance counselor 2.0, j.d. hillen and taylor desseyn agree you should ask yourself whether the problem is industry-wide or specific to your current company. if it is an industry problem, you will likely face it again at the next job, so staying may make more sense. if it is a manager-specific or company-specific issue, moving could solve it. hillen learned this after leaving jobs in advertising and realized some challenges were part of the industry itself.

what does j.d. hillen say about landing your dream job?

on guidance counselor 2.0, j.d. hillen says landing his dream job at boulevard brewing did not bring the happiness he expected. it took his wife asking whether he felt proud going to work each morning for him to realize he had not been happy there. he now believes there is no perfect job because every role has ups and downs, so chasing a dream job does not guarantee fulfillment.
