contracting vs. direct hire

guest: daniel oliver, software developer at radio systems corporation

  • expect recruiters to ask about contracting early since contract to hire is now a normal part of the process
  • going independent cuts out the middleman but means handling your own taxes, benefits and paid time off
  • w2 through a staffing company is usually easier since they handle taxes and paperwork for you
  • being a career contractor is not a red flag by itself, but talking about your next move during an interview is
  • no job is fully guaranteed so dont avoid a contract role if its the kind of work you actually want to do

questions this episode answers

what does daniel oliver say about w2 versus 1099 contracting for developers?

on guidance counselor 2.0, daniel oliver, a software developer at radio systems corporation, says going independent as a 1099 contractor cuts out the middleman but means you handle your own taxes, benefits and paid time off yourself. he explains that w2 through a staffing company is easier because they handle taxes and paperwork for you, and it's more wrapped up in a bow. he notes that while 1099 gives you more money, it comes with more mental overhead and self-employment tax.

why do recruiters ask about contracting early in the hiring process?

on guidance counselor 2.0, daniel oliver says that over the past couple of years, every time he gets on the phone with any recruiter, one of the first questions is always how he feels about contracting. he explains that contract to hire is now a normal part of the process, so recruiters need to know your preference upfront. taylor desseyn adds that companies often use contract to hire to get people onboard quicker because the recruiting company acts as an extension of hr and handles background checks and drug tests.

is being a career contractor a red flag to hiring managers?

on guidance counselor 2.0, daniel oliver says that just based on the surface of being a career contractor, there is nothing wrong with that and he would not pass on someone because of it. however, he explains that what he would personally pass on them for is if they come in and are already talking about the next thing, making it obvious this is just something to do for three or six months. taylor desseyn notes that no job is fully guaranteed, so you should not avoid a contract role if it is the kind of work you actually want to do.
