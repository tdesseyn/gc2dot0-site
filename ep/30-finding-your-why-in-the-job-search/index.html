finding your why in the job search

with taylor desseyn (solo)

  • figure out your actual why before starting a job search instead of applying just to see what happens
  • its fine if your why is simply chasing more money, just be honest about it and own that reason
  • write down and rank your top priorities like money, title, work life balance, and commute before you search
  • know your full compensation package including benefits and 401k costs before you start looking, not after
  • ask about benefits earlier in the process instead of waiting until the offer stage to find out they are bad

questions this episode answers

what does taylor desseyn say about chasing money as your reason for a job search?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says there is no shame if money is your why for looking for a new job. he advises that if you are chasing money, that is okay and you should just own it and go chase it. he shares an example of working with someone who kept bringing up salary, and when the person finally admitted they were chasing money, taylor told them to go get it.

how does taylor desseyn recommend job seekers prioritize what matters in a job search?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn recommends writing down your top three to five priorities before you start your job search. he suggests listing things like money, title, work life balance, commute, and culture, then ranking them to guide your decision making. he emphasizes that you need to know your why before starting instead of just casually applying to see what happens, because people who skip this step often end up unhappy in their new job three months later.

when should you ask about benefits during the interview process according to taylor desseyn?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says you should ask about benefits on the front end of the interview process instead of waiting until the offer stage. he explains that many people start their job search without knowing their current total compensation including benefit costs and 401k, then get a job offer only to discover the benefits are terrible. he advises knowing your full compensation package before you start looking so you can compare properly.
