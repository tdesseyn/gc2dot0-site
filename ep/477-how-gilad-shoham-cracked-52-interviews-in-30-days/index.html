how gilad shoham cracked 52 interviews in 30 days

guest: gilad shoham, leads the development team at bit.dev (bit.cloud)

  • when running many interview processes at once, keep a daily log of questions asked, how it went, and how the company felt to you
  • build a weighted scoring spreadsheet of what matters to you (tech stack, company size, product, fundraising) before you start job hunting, not during
  • space out interviews with each company even if you have time, so you dont get a contract deadline forcing you to rush everything else
  • before an interview, explore the product, find bugs, and prepare specific questions to show you did your homework
  • when hiring top talent, screen for ego during interviews since even one ego-driven hire can hurt team productivity

questions this episode answers

what does gilad shoham recommend doing after each interview during a job search?

on guidance counselor 2.0, gilad shoham says to write down what questions were asked, what went well or poorly, and most importantly how you felt about the company and the vibes. when running many processes at once, he kept a facebook group with close friends where he posted after each interview so he could remember details after doing 12 more interviews. he also maintained a detailed spreadsheet with categories like company size, location, fundraising, product, and tech stack, then processed everything each evening and again weekly.

how does gilad shoham recommend prioritizing what matters in a job search?

on guidance counselor 2.0, gilad shoham says to sit down before starting any interview process and write down everything important to you, then assign weights to show what matters more. he recommends doing this before talking to companies because once you start processes and have concrete offers, you become emotionally invested and will convince yourself a company is good just because you already put effort into it. his categories included tech stack, company size, product considerations, and technical considerations, with different weights so he could grade each company on a non-average weighted scale.

how does gilad shoham screen for ego when hiring senior engineers?

on guidance counselor 2.0, gilad shoham says he puts special effort during interviews to check for ego and has declined really famous, well-known candidates when he suspected they might cause ego wars. he explains that even one ego-driven person can kill the entire team productivity, so he cannot allow himself even one of these hires. he also sets clear boundaries by hiring the best person for very specific skills, like having the number one dependency management expert handle only dependency work and the top angular expert handle angular integration.
