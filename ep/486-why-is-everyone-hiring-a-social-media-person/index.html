why is everyone hiring a social media person

guest: julia, tech marketing and personal branding agency founder, taylor's friend

  • more companies are hiring social media leads and chief content roles because brand perception is built on social, not traditional marketing
  • if you hire someone to run your personal brand, treat it like hiring a clone of you, so screen carefully and build trust over time through freelance or trial work first
  • give content creators a clear brief like make good videos instead of forcing scripted product pitches, then trust them to know the audience
  • don't be scared of direct hot takes on linkedin, buzzwords and fluff make it harder for people to understand what you actually do
  • a real community means people engaging with each other, not just a support channel or sales funnel disguised as community

questions this episode answers

what does julia say about hiring someone to run your personal brand on social media?

on guidance counselor 2.0, julia, a tech marketing and personal branding agency founder, says you should treat hiring someone for your personal brand like hiring a clone of yourself, so screen carefully and build trust over time. she recommends starting with freelance or content creation projects first to see how you work together before giving them full control. part of their job is helping you define your own brand clearly if you haven't already, especially for executives focused on other things.

what does julia say about giving briefs to content creators?

on guidance counselor 2.0, julia says the best brief is simple like make good videos, not forcing scripted product pitches. she references tldb where the creator was told to make good videos about communication problems in tech companies without being told how or what keywords to use, then trusted to pitch the product in a way his audience would respond to because he knew them better than anyone else.

what does julia say about using buzzwords on linkedin?

on guidance counselor 2.0, julia says she is allergic to buzzwords because they say nothing and make it harder for people to understand what you actually do. she says people in tech love to fluff things up with buzzwords when they should be direct, and that cutting through the noise means talking to people in corporate like you are talking to your friends without sugarcoating everything.
