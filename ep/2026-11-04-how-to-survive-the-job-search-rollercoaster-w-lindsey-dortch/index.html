how to survive the job search rollercoaster w/ lindsey dortch

guest: lindsey dortch, marketer turned software engineer

  • stay positive without faking it: the job search sucks, so be honest about it and build a small community to get through the week together
  • measure progress by one question: did i do at least one thing today that moved the needle, like an outreach, a build or 10 minutes of interview prep
  • make your linkedin headline stop the scroll, not just software developer: hers is software engineer at the intersection of growth, product and user experience
  • coffee chats take time: comment on someone's posts for 15 minutes a day, follow them for months, then ask with something real to talk about
  • become known for something: ask what makes your eyes light up, her marketing background is what landed her new role

questions this episode answers

how did lindsey dortch stay positive during an eight-month job search?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 episode, lindsey dortch says fake corporate positivity doesn't work because the job search sucks, but if you show up with an everything-sucks mindset, that's how you show up in interviews. she took the people she met through coffee chats and started a small discord server called the offer letter club, where they talk each week about how they'll stay positive, what's getting them down and help each other draft messages. it has a frustrated channel where messages sit behind a spoiler tag, so only people with the capacity to help open them. her advice is to keep your community small, even two to five people.

how did lindsey dortch use ai in her job search?

on guidance counselor 2.0, lindsey dortch says she did about 10 minutes of interview prep a day with claude, giving it an answer one star piece at a time (situation, task, action, result) and having it tighten each part, and used voice mode to go back and forth. she found claude's resume and cold outreach templates didn't work for her, and she didn't use ai auto-apply apps. near the end of her search she used granola to transcribe her interviews and asked what she did well and what to improve, which showed her she was strong behaviorally but stumbling technically.

how should you write your linkedin headline so recruiters notice you?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 episode, lindsey dortch says recruiters scroll past headline after headline that just says software developer, so design yours to stop the scroll. hers reads software engineer at the intersection of growth, product and user experience. her recruiter told her she found lindsey through a niche linkedin recruiter search for an engineer with digital marketing experience, and by the end of her search lindsey was getting three to four real recruiter messages a day without applying. every engineering job she has gotten came from a recruiter reaching out on linkedin.
