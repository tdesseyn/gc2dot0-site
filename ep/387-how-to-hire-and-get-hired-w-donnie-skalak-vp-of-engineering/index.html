how to hire and get hired w/ donnie skalak, vp of engineering

guest: donnie skalak, vp of engineering

  • target roles you actually want and tailor your resume and cover letter instead of mass applying to everything
  • reach out directly to a recruiter or hiring manager on linkedin since a thoughtful dm can get your resume a second look
  • local onsite candidates often compete against smaller applicant pools than fully remote roles so going onsite can set you apart
  • if a company gives you a canned rejection or no feedback at all after an onsite interview thats a red flag about their culture
  • write your own job descriptions as the hiring manager instead of leaving vague generic language to hr

questions this episode answers

what does donnie skalak say about applying for jobs: volume or targeting specific roles?

on guidance counselor 2.0, donnie skalak says he sides on intentionality over volume, especially for more senior positions. he recommends targeting roles you're really interested in and tailoring your resume and cover letter to that specific role instead of mass applying. he suggests spending time digging into the company, sending direct messages to the recruiter or hiring manager, and tapping into your network for introductions.

does reaching out directly to a hiring manager on linkedin help you stand out, according to donnie skalak?

on guidance counselor 2.0, donnie skalak says he really appreciates when candidates reach out directly on linkedin or guess his email to introduce themselves. if someone sends him a resume and it interests him, he forwards it to the recruiter and asks them to give the person a screen, which helps the candidate jump through the process and go around an overwhelmed recruiter.

what does donnie skalak say about applying to local onsite roles versus remote roles?

on guidance counselor 2.0, donnie skalak says local talent has a big advantage because applying for a remote role means competing against a much larger talent pool. he estimates a remote senior manager engineering role might have a thousand applicants, while the same local austin role might have only a hundred. hiring managers are probably less picky if they can get local talent who can come into an office, so being willing to go onsite sets you up for success.
