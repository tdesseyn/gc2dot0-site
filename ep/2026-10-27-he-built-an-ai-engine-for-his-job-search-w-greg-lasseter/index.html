what greg lasseter covers in this episode

greg lasseter has 20-plus years in tech. he started as a developer and grew up in production operations, the work of keeping a business-critical system reliable and observable when real money is at stake. after time in the tax software space, he's now a senior director of site reliability engineering at a small healthcare company focused on elderly patients. his previous moves came after long stretches, including 14 years at one company, so this search dropped him into a market with ai on both sides. this episode walks through how he used claude as a working partner through his whole job search.

start with what you want

before applying anywhere, greg wrote down what kind of role he actually wanted. then he spent about a week on a top to bottom resume overhaul with ai as a guide. he typed in pages of context, facts and the emotional stuff too, and had claude interview him with more questions. what came out was an entirely new resume, with older, redundant and less relevant material cleaned out.

flip the fit question

when scoring job descriptions with ai, greg flipped the usual question. instead of am i a fit for this role, he asked is this role a fit for me. it kept him selective. he also built an agentic setup that started from his downloaded linkedin contacts, targeted their companies, and searched for new openings that matched his ideal role.

taylor adds that ai can help you send the message you're too scared to send. he used it to draft an invite to a guest with a huge following, and the guest said yes.

measure movement, not volume

greg shared his numbers. across 34 total engagements, 20 ended in silence and 12 reached a screen. he wasn't sending 80 or 100 applications. on nearly everything he applied to, he found one, two or three people to reach out to, and still often heard nothing, even for postings that looked tailor made for him. so he stopped reading silence as feedback, measured progress by how many applications moved to a screening, and put his energy into the conversations that were engaging. taylor says that was a point in his own talk too: more applications doesn't help, the right ones do.

his follow-up rule: after a cold reach out with no reply, follow up once a week later, then move on. ai helped him track what was still in the loop and when to follow up, so he didn't come across as desperate.

practice like it's live

for interview drills, greg had ai ask him questions and timed his answers, with no backspace allowed, because once something is said in an interview, it's out there. he didn't use voice mode and says he probably should have, which taylor recommends. he also learned to resist filling awkward silence. his other interview tips: find out who is interviewing you, feed their linkedin experience into claude to prep, take a few seconds before answering, and give your verdict first before explaining.

his current role came from a recruiter who reached out. he thought one early interview went poorly and almost wrote it off, a reminder that your own read on an interview isn't always right.

questions this episode answers

how does greg lasseter measure progress in a job search?

on guidance counselor 2.0, greg lasseter says to measure progress by how many applications move to a screen, not by how many you send. volume alone tells you nothing, so track the rate at which applications turn into conversations.

how does greg lasseter use claude to prep for interviews?

on guidance counselor 2.0, greg lasseter explains that he finds out who is interviewing him, grabs their linkedin experience and feeds it into claude to prep. he also flips the fit question from am i a fit for this role to is this role a fit for me.
