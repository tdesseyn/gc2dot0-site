questions this episode answers

what's the difference between text-based and app-based personal ai assistants?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says he isn't talking about claude or chatgpt but about personal ai assistants you reach by text, like instinct, pally (which he and his wife loop into a group text) and tab, versus app-based ones like muse and wabi that live on your desktop with a main chat and side chats you spin off. if you want one single place, one thread, he'd go text-based; if you like separation of concerns, like a filing cabinet, look at the app-based ones. he says try as many as you want, but have at least two going, because each one picks up on different things.

how does taylor desseyn use a personal ai assistant in his personal life?

on guidance counselor 2.0, taylor desseyn says he gives each thing its own side chat in muse or wabi: comparing randstad's open enrollment benefits against last year's spend, dropping in christmas gift links to buy later, getting car insurance quotes, photographing bills so it can flag duplicates and what to pay first, project managing an insurance claim after his office flooded, and summarizing any email marked newsletter in three bullets. he connects it read-only to his mortgage, insurance and investments and prompts it to track abnormalities and upcoming changes without touching anything.

how does taylor desseyn say to use ai assistants in a job search instead of auto-apply tools?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says you don't need to pay for ai auto-apply software because you can do it free with an assistant like pally, instinct, tab, muse or wabi. set up a watcher that scans your linkedin and x feeds every morning for roles that fit you, then have it find first-degree connections or mutuals at that company and who posted the role. instead of applying, send that person a dm about your background as it relates to the role, drafted from a career md doc that has your background in it. if they get back to you, they'll still ask you to apply, but they may attach a referral or put in a good word.
