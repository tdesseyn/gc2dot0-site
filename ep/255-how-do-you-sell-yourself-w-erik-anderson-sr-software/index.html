how do you sell yourself w/erik anderson, sr. software engineer at yum! brands

guest: erik anderson, sr. software engineer at yum! brands

  • build experience by volunteering for nonprofits or small shops instead of waiting for someone to hand you a job
  • log hours honestly on your resume so part time volunteer work does not read like a full time role
  • optimize your linkedin headline, about section and skills with the specific keywords recruiters actually search like react or node
  • spend time daily posting, commenting and engaging on linkedin since activity signals to the algorithm that you are responsive
  • be intentional about telling your company what you want to work on so you do not get stuck as the expert in old tech you dont enjoy

questions this episode answers

what does erik anderson recommend for getting experience as a junior developer without a job?

on guidance counselor 2.0, erik anderson recommends volunteering with nonprofits like code for america, donate code, and ragtag.org to build work experience. he explains that if you put in eight to twenty hours a week helping them in your free time, you can list that as work experience on your resume and speak to those projects in interviews. he also advises being clear about hours worked so part-time volunteer work does not appear as full-time employment.

how does erik anderson say to optimize linkedin for recruiters searching for developers?

on guidance counselor 2.0, erik anderson says to put your top three technical skills in your linkedin headline separated by pipe symbols, like react pipe node. he recommends repeating those keywords high in your about section so recruiters do not have to click see more, listing them in your featured section, and turning on creator mode with hashtags for those skills. he also advises completing skill assessments for your primary languages and asking bootcamp cohort members to endorse you.

what does erik anderson warn about becoming an expert in old technology at work?

on guidance counselor 2.0, erik anderson warns that companies often need legacy systems maintained or have projects using technology that is not very fun. he says you can quickly become an expert in old technology or something you do not enjoy, making it difficult later to find jobs doing the exciting things you actually want to work on. he advises being intentional with your company about what you enjoy working on and developing those skills in your free time if you are not getting them at work.
