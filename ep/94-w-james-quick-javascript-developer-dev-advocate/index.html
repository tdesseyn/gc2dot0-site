questions this episode answers

what does james quick say about learning to code in college versus building real projects?

on guidance counselor 2.0, james quick says he got good grades in college but treated programming like a memorization test and couldn't answer basic computer science questions when interviewing. he only learned to program by building things himself after graduation, which is when the concepts actually clicked. he recommends making programming personal by building something you enjoy rather than just completing homework assignments.

what does james quick say about networking for developers?

on guidance counselor 2.0, james quick says networking is not walking up to strangers and pitching yourself transactionally. it is genuinely investing in people and relationships in the community, whether on twitter or at meetups. he says when he applied for his job at auth0, he had two or three people who could vouch for him because he had built real relationships over time, and that networking gives you control over your career trajectory.

how does james quick say communication skills help developers advance to senior roles?

on guidance counselor 2.0, james quick says moving from developer to senior or principal level comes from much more than programming skills, especially your ability to communicate. companies need experienced developers who are articulate enough to translate technical concepts into things that non-technical people can understand. he says communication played a huge role in his double promotion to architect at fedex, where he became probably the youngest architect at the company.
