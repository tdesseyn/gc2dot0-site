ep 278 · sep 27, 2023

the power of content w/jason lengstorf

guest: jason lengstorf, freelance developer-focused media and marketing consultant, former vp of developer experience at netlify, host of learn with jason

  • focus content on the story of who someone becomes by using a tool, not a list of features or specs
  • pick one clear narrative instead of burying people in technical specs they do not care about
  • watch out for building brand identity so tightly that changing your rules later feels like betraying your audience
  • stop obsessing over siloed metrics like blog views and instead track awareness, signups, activation, and paid conversion together
  • treat your career like an experiment and try many paths early instead of specializing too soon

questions this episode answers

what does jason lengstorf say about how companies should talk about their products?

on guidance counselor 2.0, jason lengstorf says companies should focus on telling the story of who someone becomes by using a tool, not explaining features or specs. he points to apple as an example: they picked privacy as one story and said stop letting companies touch your stuff, rather than listing encryption specs. people who never cared about privacy suddenly chose apple because the story showed how their world improves.

what does jason lengstorf say about the problem with marketing metrics?

on guidance counselor 2.0, jason lengstorf says companies break metrics into smaller, individually ownable pieces like blog views, but those metrics do not matter on their own. he explains you could drive millions of hits without a single sale but still hit your metric. instead, marketing, dev rel, product and engineering should all be responsible for the same big metrics: awareness, signups, activation and paid conversion.

what does jason lengstorf say companies should do instead of conferences?

on guidance counselor 2.0, jason lengstorf says companies should invest in episodic tv-style content instead of expensive conferences that no longer drive roi. he suggests making a reality show or documentary series with weekly drops, like the great british bake off for developers, which would cost around 400,000 to 500,000 dollars for eight episodes and can be offset with sponsors. weekly episodes keep people talking and engaged, unlike a one-day conference drop that gets watched once and forgotten.
