importance of conflict w/ liz kislik

guest: liz kislik, management consultant and executive coach

  • irrational anger usually signals a boundary violation, not a character flaw
  • calm your body first by noticing your feet, surroundings, or what you can hear to stop a stress spiral
  • rebuild routines gradually, like meeting a colleague for coffee before jumping back into full social schedules
  • talk to yourself in second person to create distance from harsh self judgment
  • look back at what you have already accomplished before assuming you are failing or an imposter

questions this episode answers

what does liz kislik say irrational anger means at work?

on guidance counselor 2.0, liz kislik says irrational anger is an absolute indication of some kind of boundary violation. she explains that it does not matter what the other person meant; something has come across at you in a way that triggers earlier bad times. the anger signals that you do not feel safe, not that you have a character flaw.

how does liz kislik recommend calming down during a tense conversation?

on guidance counselor 2.0, liz kislik recommends anchoring yourself in your body to persuade your brain that you are safe. feel your feet pressing into the floor in your shoes, notice four things you can see around you, and listen for several sounds in the environment. she also suggests looking over each shoulder to check that nothing is physically coming at you, which helps your brain realize there is no actual danger.

what does liz kislik say about talking to yourself in second person?

on guidance counselor 2.0, liz kislik says referring to yourself in the second person is very helpful because it creates a little bit of distance from harsh self-judgment. most people attack themselves with critical voices, but when you talk to yourself as if speaking to someone else, you might be a little kinder. this distance gives you an opportunity to use a voice of grace instead of the harsh judgmental voice.
