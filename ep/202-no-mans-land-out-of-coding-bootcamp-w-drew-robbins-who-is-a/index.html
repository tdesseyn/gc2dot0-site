questions this episode answers

what does drew robbins say about choosing a coding bootcamp?

on guidance counselor 2.0, drew robbins says not to go the cheap route when picking a bootcamp. he warns that cheaper options often lack structure and accountability, leaving students more on their own. drew chose general assembly because it was accredited and offered intense structure with 8 to 12 hour days, six days a week for three to four months, which worked better for him than self-taught or college pacing.

how did drew robbins network on linkedin after bootcamp?

on guidance counselor 2.0, drew robbins says he maximized linkedin's 100 connection requests per week limit by sending templated messages with a personalized detail at the end. he would reference something specific from each person's profile, like a shared bootcamp, location or interest, which he says nearly doubled his response rate compared to cold outreach. drew also committed to commenting every time he liked a post to boost visibility and spent six to seven hours daily on linkedin, deleting all other social media apps to stay focused.

what does drew robbins recommend doing when recruiters reach out on linkedin?

on guidance counselor 2.0, drew robbins says always reply to recruiters even if the role is not a fit, because responding returns their inmails token and builds goodwill. he suggests taking 15 seconds to say no thank you, refer a friend who might be a better match, or ask if they have other types of roles. drew points out that recruiters are gatekeepers to jobs and staying in their network can lead to future opportunities.
