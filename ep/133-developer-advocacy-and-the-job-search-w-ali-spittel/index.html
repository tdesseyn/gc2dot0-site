developer advocacy and the job search w/ ali spittel

guest: ali spittel, leads developer advocacy at aws amplify

  • build a portfolio site instead of relying only on linkedin, since you control the story and can show real projects
  • skip the generic to-do list or restaurant app and build something you would actually use yourself
  • teaching a concept through a blog post, video, or talk proves you understand it and builds communication skills employers want

questions this episode answers

what does ali spittel say about building a portfolio for a developer job search?

on guidance counselor 2.0, ali spittel from aws amplify says a portfolio site is the primary thing she looked for when hiring software engineers. you have complete control over it, unlike linkedin, and can show exactly what you want including projects you're proud of. even if it's not visual or you don't have a thousand projects, just showing what you're interested in and what you're doing sets people apart.

what does ali spittel recommend bootcamp grads build instead of a to-do list app?

on guidance counselor 2.0, ali spittel advises bootcamp graduates to build something they would actually use themselves rather than a to-do list or restaurant app. she saw those two types most frequently as a bootcamp instructor, and building something you care about will keep you motivated to work on it during the time between bootcamp and getting a job. adding some sort of spin or making it personal makes your portfolio so much more interesting to hiring managers.

why does ali spittel say teaching a concept helps your job search?

on guidance counselor 2.0, ali spittel says if you can teach something to somebody else through a blog post, youtube video, or conference talk, it shows you really understand that topic. as a hiring manager, seeing a strong blog post on a subject showed her the person understood it and could distill information for teammates, proving they're strong communicators. she looks for team communication skills as one of the first things when hiring, not just individual genius coders.
