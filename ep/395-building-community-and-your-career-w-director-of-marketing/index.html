ep 395 · aug 7, 2024

building community and your career w/director of marketing of renderatl

guest: brianna holmes, marketing director at renderatl

  • build community before you chase revenue, people can tell when a brand only wants their money right now
  • your most authentic, unpolished content often converts better than polished ads because it reminds people you are human
  • apply to jobs even if you do not feel qualified, sometimes a rejection turns into a role created just for you
  • stay in touch with recruiters or hiring managers after a rejection, continued connection can lead to an opportunity later
  • pick passion over profit early in your career, chasing security over what excites you can cost you time and impact

questions this episode answers

what does brianna holmes say about applying to jobs when you don't meet all the qualifications?

on guidance counselor 2.0, brianna holmes says to apply even if you don't think you're qualified. she applied to jobs she wasn't qualified for and got rejection letters, but stayed in contact with the recruiters. two months after one rejection, the company reached out and created a new role specifically for her because she had shown her strengths and what she could contribute.

what does brianna holmes recommend for changing careers or finding a new job?

on guidance counselor 2.0, brianna holmes recommends three things: first, network constantly and meet as many people as possible who have the career path you admire. second, pay attention to resume trends, especially how applicant tracking systems process resumes, because creative designs can hurt you if you're not in design or marketing. third, apply to jobs even if you don't feel qualified and stay in touch with recruiters after rejections, because that connection can lead to new opportunities later.

what does brianna holmes say about choosing passion over profit in your career?

on guidance counselor 2.0, brianna holmes says if she could give young brianna one piece of career advice, it would be to focus on passion instead of profit. she chose accounting in high school because it promised financial security, but her real passion was working with people. she believes focusing on passion earlier would have allowed her to impact more people and saved her from spending money on a degree she didn't need.
