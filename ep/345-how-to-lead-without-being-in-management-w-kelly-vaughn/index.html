how to lead without being in management w/ kelly vaughn, director of eng

guest: kelly vaughn, director of engineering

  • think beyond your assigned task and consider the impact your work has on the wider organization
  • amplify and support your teammates instead of only promoting your own work to build real influence
  • ask for feedback often and be willing to accept it even when it is delivered poorly or bluntly
  • work on genuine weaknesses like being defensive or abrasive instead of writing them off as just your personality
  • build emotional intelligence by managing your own reactions and stepping away before responding in heated moments

questions this episode answers

what does kelly vaughn say about showing leadership before having a title?

on guidance counselor 2.0, kelly vaughn says you have to demonstrate leadership tendencies before you get an official leadership title. when she is looking at who might become an engineering manager for the first time, she looks at how they have already exemplified leadership skills based on things like thinking about bigger-picture impact and amplifying others. she does not believe in promoting someone just because they are a great engineer.

how does kelly vaughn recommend handling poorly delivered feedback?

on guidance counselor 2.0, kelly vaughn says that not many people are good at delivering constructive feedback, so you need to learn to parse feedback and understand the intent behind it. even when someone gives you feedback in a way that is insulting or abrasive, the underlying point may still be valid and worth receiving. if you get feedback you do not agree with, just graciously say thank you and that you will take it into consideration, then set it aside.

what does kelly vaughn mean by amplifying others on your team?

on guidance counselor 2.0, kelly vaughn says amplifying others means recognizing that nobody works in a silo and you get help from other people. instead of taking credit for all your work as if you did it alone, you should highlight the voices and contributions of teammates who reviewed your work or provided input. taylor desseyn adds that being a cheerleader for others can open unexpected doors in your career, because when you champion someone like a director, they will advocate for you when you want to move up.
