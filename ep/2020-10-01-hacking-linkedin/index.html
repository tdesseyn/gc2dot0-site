hacking linkedin

with taylor desseyn (solo)

  • use your linkedin cover photo to share contact info or a quick summary of what you do
  • pick a profile picture with good lighting and a pop of color so you stand out in a sea of tiny thumbnails
  • make your title specific with the exact tech or skill you use since recruiters search by keyword and skip vague titles like software developer
  • keep your about section to two or three lines since linkedin only previews a short snippet before cutting it off
  • spend time every day liking and commenting on posts since engagement through shared connections can get your profile and posts seen by hiring managers

questions this episode answers

what does taylor desseyn say about using your linkedin cover photo?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says to use your linkedin cover photo to immediately share your contact information with visitors. he points to examples like dan thompson who lists his email, twitter handle, and what he does (advocate, relationship builder, connector) in his cover image. taylor mentions canva as a tool that provides pre-sized templates for linkedin headers.

why does taylor desseyn say to put specific tech in your linkedin title?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says recruiters search linkedin recruiter by specific keywords like dot net or javascript, and if your title just says vague things like software developer or senior engineer they will skip your profile entirely. he explains that lazy recruiters won't even click through to read your full profile if the visible title doesn't match their search terms. he recommends titles like dot net developer or react developer instead of generic ones.

what does taylor desseyn say about linkedin engagement to get noticed by hiring managers?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says to spend an hour every day liking and commenting on posts you find interesting because linkedin shows your activity to your connections. when you like a post, all your connections see it in their feed, which means hiring managers in your network can discover your profile through that engagement. he shares that developer danny morris got a job interview because a manager saw his linkedin post after someone liked it.
