what anthony mays covers in this episode

anthony mays is a former googler and the founder of morgan latimer consulting, and at the time of this episode he'd just become a bootcamp instructor, after being asked to build a curriculum that packed a lot of computer science and practical training into ten weeks. it was his third time on guidance counselor 2.0. the theme, it's not 2019 anymore, is about how breaking into tech has changed, especially for juniors, and what to do instead of what used to work.

the leetcode grind isn't the answer

anthony jokes that his tombstone will probably read don't grind leetcode. when he first worked with bootcamp students, some needed more time with their tools, but the bigger gap was problem solving: how you think and which questions you ask. he splits technical interviewing into two parts, the tools and the process. if you can't describe how you solve problems in 60 seconds, he says, you'll struggle in coding interviews.

build with one or two people and show the proof

if he had to start his career over, anthony would find one or two people to build something with and focus on what you'd do on the job: good commit messages, good pull requests, good documentation. then, when talking to a hiring manager or recruiter, point them to a link that shows you did exactly what the role needs, with a team. he also tells students to bring tangible assets and numbers, to learn ai tools instead of running scared of them, and to write well, whether it's a short linkedin post or a full article.

self-taught vs bootcamp

a listener asked who communicates their work better, bootcamp grads or self-taught juniors. anthony picks self-taught, on average. being self-taught means filtering through a lot of bad documentation and information and having the courage to ask people for help, which builds the ability to explain what works and what doesn't. he also notes that some bootcamp marketing attracts people looking for a shortcut, and when the fast job doesn't come, they have to buckle up. his advice to bootcamp grads is to become self-taught too.

networking that isn't asking for a job

taylor shares how his own advice has shifted. hiring managers are buried in resumes, so instead of asking for a meeting, he points to kelly vaughn's advice: ask direct, good questions in the dms, and when you apply, mention it. a dm that just says find me a job doesn't work.

on a listener question about a hiring manager viewing their profile after they applied, anthony says reach out, but don't be creepy about it, and ask something specific, like what three problems the team is facing right now. taylor's caveat: if you're already working with a recruiter, go through them, because some strict hiring managers will disqualify candidates who go around the recruiter. either way, ask to talk to someone who works there during every interview loop.

the new meetups

taylor and anthony both say live streams and audio rooms are the new meetups. anthony's point is to pick the format that fits how you like to communicate, and be intentional about building the skill, because nobody wakes up knowing how to network.

his advice to his younger self: don't be scared. times are scarier now, but hard work, honesty about where you need to grow, and bringing people along still work.

questions this episode answers

what does anthony mays say bootcamp grads should do instead of grinding leetcode?

on guidance counselor 2.0, anthony mays says bootcamp grads should stop grinding leetcode alone and instead build real projects with one or two people. he tells students to focus on writing good commit messages, pull requests and documentation that show they can work on a team. when talking to hiring managers they can then point to these tangible projects as proof of their skills.

according to anthony mays who explains their work better, bootcamp juniors or self-taught juniors?

on guidance counselor 2.0, anthony mays says self-taught juniors communicate their work better than bootcamp grads on average. he explains that self-taught developers had to filter through a lot of bad information and documentation to learn, which forced them to develop the ability to hold conversations and explain what they are doing. they also tend to have more tenacity because they did not expect a shortcut to success.

what does anthony mays say about messaging someone when they view your linkedin profile?

on guidance counselor 2.0, anthony mays says if a hiring manager looks at your profile you should message them with specific questions, not generic ones. he recommends asking things like what three problems their team is facing right now or other specific details about the role. however taylor and anthony agree that if you are already working with a recruiter you should go through that recruiter instead of messaging the hiring manager directly.
