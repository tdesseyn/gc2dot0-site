the power of community w/ will johnson

guest: will johnson, developer advocate at auth0

  • document what you are currently learning publicly instead of waiting until you feel like an expert
  • meetups are a way to learn the local industry, find mentors, and meet people who can vouch for you later
  • building relationships before you need them turns a cold application into a warm referral
  • speaking at meetups or conferences makes people come to you instead of you chasing connections
  • approach every interaction by asking how you can help the other person, not what you can get from them

questions this episode answers

what does will johnson say about documenting your learning publicly?

on guidance counselor 2.0, will johnson says documenting what you are learning on social media proves to people that you are serious and progressing. he explains that when he met people at meetups six months after first connecting, they could see all the work he had been doing on twitter and took him more seriously. he did not create extra work but simply shared what he was already learning through tutorials, books, and courses.

how does will johnson recommend starting relationships at meetups and conferences?

on guidance counselor 2.0, will johnson says you should approach every interaction by asking how you can help the other person, not what you can get from them. when he wanted to book his dance group for events years ago, he would contact promoters and ask how he could help them promote their upcoming shows rather than immediately pitching his own group. he explains that starting a relationship by being helpful is the best foundation and makes hiring managers see you as less risky.

should you spend more time networking than cold applying to jobs?

on guidance counselor 2.0, will johnson and taylor desseyn agree you should spend far more time networking than cold applying. will says when you network and build relationships you can take five hundred applications down to ten and have a much higher success rate. he explains that having someone who has worked at a company for five years vouch for you makes you look less risky to hiring managers than blindly uploading your resume to indeed.
