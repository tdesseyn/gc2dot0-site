the importance of value in your career w/ staff dev advocate, adewale abati

guest: adewale abati, staff developer advocate at block

  • focus on the value you create in a role, not just the code you write or tools you use
  • document what you're doing for yourself first, then sharing it becomes less scary over time
  • sharing your struggles and lessons publicly often resonates more than sharing polished wins
  • getting involved in open source or community slacks solving problems can open doors you never expected
  • in interviews, frame your projects around the value and impact they created, not just the tech stack used

questions this episode answers

what does adewale abati say about overcoming fear of sharing content publicly?

on guidance counselor 2.0, adewale abati says he uses three mental tricks to reduce pressure when posting. first, he tells himself he is just documenting something for himself, not for an audience. second, he frames it as helping someone who struggled like he did. third, he imagines sharing only for a small set of people he knows are interested, rather than thinking about millions of potential viewers.

how did adewale abati get his first speaking opportunity?

on guidance counselor 2.0, adewale abati explains he was active in a slack group called dev career, helping people solve problems on stack overflow without expecting anything in return. someone in the community noticed his helpful answers and reached out asking if he would speak at a meetup. he had no idea that was possible but said yes, and that first talk nine years ago is still being referenced today and opened many doors for him.

what does adewale abati recommend focusing on instead of just tech stacks in your work?

on guidance counselor 2.0, adewale abati says developers should focus on the value they create rather than just the technology they use. he suggests always asking why you are building something and what benefit it will bring to users or the business. in interviews, he talks about projects from a value perspective first, not the libraries or frameworks, because that shows he thinks about impact and relevance rather than just staying busy with code.
