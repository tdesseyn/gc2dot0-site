ep 186 · nov 28, 2022

i ain't got time...how can recruiters help w/daniel norton, cto of neural payments

guest: daniel norton, cto of neural payments

  • treat recruiting partners like a time-saving resource since most hiring managers cannot spend real hours sourcing candidates themselves
  • agency recruiters talk to huge numbers of engineers every year, giving them a live pulse on market pay and availability that job boards cannot match
  • push back on gotcha-style technical interviews and favor a vetting process candidates actually respect
  • consider contract to hire so the staffing partner handles onboarding paperwork and logistics for you
  • bring in junior developers from coding bootcamps since they arrive with practical skills, strong motivation, and let senior engineers move into bigger roles

questions this episode answers

what does daniel norton say about why ctos should work with agency recruiters?

on guidance counselor 2.0, daniel norton says ctos rarely have time to recruit because they are busy keeping systems live and shipping product. agency recruiters meet hundreds of engineers every year and have a real-time pulse on the market that salary surveys cannot match. norton also values that recruiters can handle vetting, reference checks and onboarding logistics so he can focus on building product.

how does daniel norton recommend hiring managers incorporate junior developers?

on guidance counselor 2.0, daniel norton says hiring managers should hire senior engineers who can mentor and hand off work to junior developers. as mid-level engineers grow and take on bigger challenges, they create a vacuum below them where junior developers can handle bug fixes, documentation and smaller features without pressure. norton also says bootcamp graduates come with immediately applicable skills, professional experience from prior careers, hunger to prove themselves, and lower salary requirements than senior hires.

what does taylor desseyn say recruiters offer that salary surveys do not?

on guidance counselor 2.0, taylor desseyn says agency recruiters talk to roughly 520 to 800 engineers per year per recruiter, giving them a daily pulse on market pay and candidate availability. he argues that salary surveys from sites like dice are outdated because they do not involve daily conversations with hiring managers and engineers across the country. taylor also notes that most people recruiters talk to are not actively looking, so recruiters surface passive candidates.
