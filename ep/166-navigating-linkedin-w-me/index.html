ep 166 · aug 10, 2022

navigating linkedin w/ me :)

guest: matthew, vp of software architecture and development, vault systems

  • make your linkedin banner and headline explain what you actually do, not just a job title or company name
  • write your profile so someone reading it thinks wow tell me more, same rule applies to resumes
  • add a featured section on linkedin to point people to your github, portfolio, or side projects
  • network inside your own company now, so if a colleague leaves for a new job they may bring you with them
  • when dming someone for advice, give a specific short time ask like six or seven minutes so they know what they are agreeing to

questions this episode answers

what does taylor desseyn say about linkedin banner photos and headlines?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says your linkedin banner photo needs to explain what you actually do, not just show a company name. he tells matthew that a banner saying vault systems tells him nothing and doesn't draw people in. your headline or title section should say what you currently do or what you want to do, not just generic labels, because recruiters search line by line through those titles.

what does taylor desseyn recommend for writing linkedin profiles and resumes?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says to write every sentence so someone reading it thinks wow tell me more. he advises matthew to streamline his about section to three bullet points because people glance at profiles and want the most important information quickly. your linkedin profile does not need to be a book, just short applicable bullet points with technologies at the top and one or two main projects.

how does taylor desseyn suggest using linkedin for networking within your own company?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says to network with people internally at your organization immediately, even when you are not job searching. he explains that startups and big companies fail and lay people off, but people like to work with people they like. if you network with colleagues now and they leave for another company, they will probably take you with them.
