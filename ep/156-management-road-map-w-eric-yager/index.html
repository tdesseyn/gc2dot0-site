ep 156 · jul 18, 2022

management road map w/ eric yager

guest: eric yager, site tech lead, amazon air science technology organization

  • expect competition for talent not just from other companies but from other teams inside the same large organization
  • offer flexibility on remote work and location since engineers now have many options and recruiters reach out constantly
  • write down team tenants so decisions can still happen smoothly when the manager is not in the room
  • run one on ones every week with direct reports and plan specific topics ahead of time instead of winging it
  • if you lack the exact skills for an entry level tech role, take an adjacent role to get in the door and then work toward the role you want

questions this episode answers

what does eric yager say about competing for talent as a hiring manager at amazon?

on guidance counselor 2.0, eric yager says that as a hiring manager at amazon, you compete not just against external companies but also against other teams within amazon itself. he explains that amazon is structured more like a conglomerate of startups with centralized funding, so different organizations internally compete for the same candidates. he sometimes finds that candidates he sources have already been contacted by other amazon teams.

what does eric yager recommend if you lack skills for an entry level tech role?

on guidance counselor 2.0, eric yager says you should either skill up to match the entry level position or find an adjacent role you are qualified for at your target company. he suggests coming in as a program manager, business analyst, support engineer, or quality assurance engineer, then automating tasks and contributing code to demonstrate your ability. once inside, talk to hiring managers and work toward shifting into the role you want, and look for internal apprenticeship programs that let non-technical employees transition into software development roles.
