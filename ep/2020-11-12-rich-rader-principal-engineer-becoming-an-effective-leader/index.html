rich rader principal engineer + becoming an effective leader

guest: rich rader, principal engineer

  • influencing people matters more than being technically right, so learn to understand what others actually want before pushing your point
  • read how to win friends and influence people and the 21 laws of leadership to build real influence instead of just telling people they are wrong
  • give team members slightly more responsibility than they would ask for themselves, framed as growth and backed by support, not added stress
  • protecting your team sometimes costs you personally, so decide early whether you will defend your people even if it affects how you look to bosses
  • pair juniors and seniors as mentors for each other so growth keeps moving up and down the ladder, not just top down

questions this episode answers

what does rich rader say about learning to influence people as a developer?

on guidance counselor 2.0, rich rader says he was a terrible communicator early in his career, bluntly telling people their work was stupid or bad. his manager recommended two books, how to win friends and influence people and the 21 laws of leadership, which taught him to understand what others want instead of just being technically correct. he learned that curating relationships matters as much as writing good code, and that perspective shift unlocked real leadership ability.

how does rich rader challenge his team members to grow?

on guidance counselor 2.0, rich rader says he gives each team member slightly more responsibility than they would ask for themselves, framing it as healthy growth rather than added stress. he explains up front that the pressure is intentional and that he has their back if it becomes too much. he also pairs juniors with mid-level developers as mentors so everyone in the chain is teaching and learning, not just receiving top-down instruction.

what does rich rader say about protecting your team as a leader?

on guidance counselor 2.0, rich rader says he has been demoted more than once because he refused to sacrifice his team to look good to his bosses. he faced moments where he had to choose between making his team work eighty-hour weeks or pushing back and getting in trouble himself, and he chose to defend his people. that willingness to take a personal hit is one reason his team members have followed him from company to company.
