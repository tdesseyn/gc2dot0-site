the importance of the follow up w/ liz schwerd, sr. recruiter

guest: liz schwerd, sr. recruiter, founder of a recruiting consulting firm

  • apply to fewer jobs but follow up on each one, aiming for a higher hit rate instead of just blasting applications
  • after applying, message the recruiter directly and include the job or rec id number so they can route you to the right person fast
  • if you get no response after a cold application, give it a week or two before reaching out to the likely hiring manager, not before
  • once a recruiter has actually contacted you, never jump over their head to the hiring manager, that move backfires
  • when networking feels awkward, pick learning or industry events over pure networking mixers so conversation happens naturally around shared interest

questions this episode answers

what does liz schwerd say about following up after applying to a job?

on guidance counselor 2.0, liz schwerd says you should apply to fewer jobs but follow up on each one with a direct message to the recruiter. include the job or rec id number in your message so the recruiter can quickly route it to the right person or forward it internally. she advises sending the follow-up message right after you apply rather than waiting, because some markets get flooded with applications and early is best.

what does liz schwerd recommend about messaging hiring managers after applying?

on guidance counselor 2.0, liz schwerd says if you get no response after a cold application, wait a week or two before reaching out to the likely hiring manager, not before. she stresses that once a recruiter has actually contacted you, never jump over their head to the hiring manager because that move will backfire. cold applications are the only time it is appropriate to message the hiring manager after giving the recruiter time.

how does liz schwerd recommend networking for introverts?

on guidance counselor 2.0, liz schwerd says introverts should skip pure networking events and instead attend learning or industry events where conversation happens naturally around shared topics. she says if an event is titled networking she will not go, but she attends conferences and meetups where people discuss specific skills or trends. that way you have common ground to talk about without forcing small talk, and the networking happens as a side benefit.
