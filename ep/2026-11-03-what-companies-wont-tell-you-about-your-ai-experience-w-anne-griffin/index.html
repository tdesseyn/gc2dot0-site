what anne griffin covers in this episode

anne t. griffin is an agentic ai product leader. she started using ai in her own product work about two and a half years before this conversation, back when coworkers wondered how she was getting through so much so fast. in this episode she and taylor dig into what hiring teams are really looking for when they ask about your ai experience, and why the answer is less about tools than about outcomes and judgment.

learn it on your own if you have to

anne's view is that most people will be using an agent as part of their work, so if your company has ai locked down, learn on your own time. and not just to say you've used it. understand how it works: what examples an agent needs to follow your directions, or to sound like you. taylor shares his hot take that not experimenting with agentic ai on your own is a red flag on a resume today, with the caveat that a company that bans it is a different conversation.

come to the interview with an opinion

anne says you have to be able to talk about ai in an interview, and you often need an opinion. if she were a software engineer, she'd think through where she doesn't use ai coding tools, and why. when a leader asked her what to look for in ai product talent, she said yes, ai experience, but shown on top of the fundamentals, because the fundamentals haven't changed. it's hard to explain how you use a tool without giving up your own thinking if you've never used the tools.

for a developer or pm with real ai experience, her advice on making it land is to talk about what you actually did: what you built, which tools you tried, what you liked and didn't, and why you'd pick one over another. back in 2017 there was no ai pm bootcamp, so her first step was writing, including an article on how ai could make a waiting room feel more human.

ai shows who is checking boxes

taylor says ai has made it obvious who is cosplaying a role versus doing what needs to be done. anne frames it as outcomes. if you're not working toward an outcome, people can tell. that's why roles look like they're blending together: product, design, engineering and sales are all pushed toward the same outcome, with and without ai. it's also why communication skills matter more than ever. she also says that because ai promotes sameness, you have to think differently to compete with it.

customers buy outcomes, not technology

when she talks about value, anne pushes past saving time. why do people care about the time saved? what do they get back, and what's the task they hate most? name that, and people get it right away. she also points to a palantir executive's comment that using ai just to move faster isn't ambitious enough. be specific about what better means.

it's okay to be frustrated

on the market, anne says you can be an a player and still miss out on opportunities. she uses serena williams as the example: after a loss, it's about what to improve next. being frustrated is fine. the job is to channel it into the next thing, whether that's work skills, ai skills or interviewing, and keep upgrading.
