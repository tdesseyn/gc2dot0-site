ep 142 · may 20, 2022

from startups to corporate w/ brian dougherty

guest: brian dougherty, chief operations officer and chief technical officer at jumpstart health investors

  • avoid making big changes in your first 90 days at a new organization so you can see what truly motivates each person
  • get a clear read on team culture before trying to fix anything, then build a new mission and values with the team, not for them
  • watch for self regulating culture, like teammates calling each other out on values, as a sign the culture is actually working
  • be careful when narrowing a strong performer's scope of responsibility since it can feel demoralizing if not handled thoughtfully
  • help each person grow by figuring out if they want to be a generalist, a subject matter expert, or move into management, then assign work that fits

questions this episode answers

what does brian dougherty say about making changes in your first 90 days at a new job?

on guidance counselor 2.0, brian dougherty says not to make any big moves in your first 90 days at a new organization. he explains that by day 45 you might think someone is ineffective, but by day 90 you understand what truly motivates each person and where they can have real impact. this waiting period helps you see strengths you would have missed if you acted too quickly.

how does brian dougherty recommend building culture with a new team?

on guidance counselor 2.0, brian dougherty recommends working with the team to define mission and values together rather than dictating them from the top. he had every team member sign a letter of commitment to the mission and values they created together. he says culture is working when it becomes self-regulating, meaning teammates call each other out when someone violates a shared value like empathy or positivity.

what three career paths does brian dougherty use for employee development?

on guidance counselor 2.0, brian dougherty asks each person if they want to be a generalist with wide technical breadth, a subject matter expert with deep technical depth in one area, or move into management and leadership to build teams. he then assigns work that helps them grow toward their chosen path, so someone wanting management gets stories to lead while someone wanting deep expertise gets time to research frameworks or solve complex technical problems.
