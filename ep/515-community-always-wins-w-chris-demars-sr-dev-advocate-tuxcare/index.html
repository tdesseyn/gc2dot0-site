community always wins w/ chris demars, sr. dev advocate @ tuxcare

guest: chris demars, sr. dev advocate at tuxcare

  • when you get laid off, treat the job search as your full time job and start applying and telling your network immediately
  • before interviewing, research whether a company actually understands the role they are hiring for or is just copying a competitor
  • use interview conversations to disqualify roles that dont match what you need, dont just chase any offer
  • after a good interview, follow up with a thank you plus extra materials like a portfolio, talks, and work samples so they can pass you along
  • spread yourself across multiple communities, not just your own niche, since those connections can lead to referrals and job leads

questions this episode answers

what does chris demars say about taking time off after getting laid off?

on guidance counselor 2.0, chris demars says your job is to find a job, not to take two or three weeks to decompress after a layoff. he started applying immediately and told everyone he knew across slack channels, bluesky and linkedin the second he was laid off from digital ocean. time is money, especially if you have single income, so you need to hit the ground running right away.

how does chris demars say to research whether a company understands the role they're hiring for?

on guidance counselor 2.0, chris demars says you should ask questions in interviews to understand where the investment comes from and whose idea it was to create the role. he found many companies in 2024 were hiring developer advocates just because a competitor had them, without understanding what developer relations actually means. you need to break down conversations to see if leadership truly gets the value or if they are just throwing things at the wall.

what does chris demars recommend doing after a good first interview?

on guidance counselor 2.0, chris demars says if the first conversation went well, follow up with a thank you and send all your additional materials so they can pass you along. send a portfolio, a list of speaking engagements, certifications, awards, youtube videos of conference talks and blog posts. he learned from eric bouchard to create a one-stop portfolio on dev.to with everything, then track views using a short link from bitly or rebrandly.
