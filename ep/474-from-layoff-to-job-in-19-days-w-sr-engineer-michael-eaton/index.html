from layoff to job in 19 days w/ sr. engineer michael eaton

guest: michael eaton, senior engineer

  • turn on the open to work signal immediately after a layoff instead of waiting around
  • keep a simple tracker of every lead, referral source, recruiter, and interview stage so nothing slips through the cracks
  • always follow up with recruiters and interviewers, even the ones who did not move forward, to leave a good impression
  • build your network over years by staying in touch with former coworkers, speaking at events, and being active in online communities
  • share what you know publicly so people come to you, rather than just consuming content and staying invisible

questions this episode answers

what does michael eaton say about turning on the open to work banner after a layoff?

on guidance counselor 2.0, michael eaton says he turned on the linkedin open to work banner immediately after getting laid off, even while still in the car answering his wife's questions. that same evening he got his first solid lead from someone who had seen him speak at a conference the year before. the recruiter reached out specifically because the banner made michael's availability visible.

how did michael eaton track his job search leads and interviews?

on guidance counselor 2.0, michael eaton says he kept a simple whiteboard behind his desk where he wrote down each company, who referred him, the recruiter's name, what interview phase he was in, and any salary discussions. he updated it throughout the process so he wouldn't forget details or embarrass himself by forgetting what he had told someone. this physical tracker helped him manage about five solid leads without letting anything slip through the cracks.

what does michael eaton recommend about following up with recruiters who reject you?

on guidance counselor 2.0, michael eaton says he sent thank you messages to every recruiter and team that didn't move forward with him, making it clear he appreciated their time. when one company went a different direction after multiple interviews, he told the recruiter to let the team know he enjoyed the process even though it was stressful. he believes following up professionally leaves a good impression and maintains relationships even when you don't get the job.
