career transition into software engineering w/ chris nowicki, eng lead

guest: chris nowicki, engineering lead at this dot labs

  • treat a coding bootcamp as the start, not the finish, since landing a job takes extra skills beyond the technical training
  • pick one stack like react and next.js and go deep instead of spreading thin across many frameworks
  • attend conferences and talk to people in person since real connections there can lead directly to job opportunities
  • offer to help someone on a real project for free to build experience that goes beyond side projects
  • build in public by writing about what you are learning, since those articles can stand in for job experience on a resume

questions this episode answers

what does chris nowicki say about bootcamp graduates and job readiness?

on guidance counselor 2.0, chris nowicki, engineering lead at this dot labs, says bootcamps provide technical knowledge but not all the other skills needed for a job. many graduates think they are job-ready after finishing, but they are not. you need to develop additional skills like networking, contributing to real projects, and learning to work in teams before you can land a role.

how does chris nowicki recommend choosing which tech stack to learn?

on guidance counselor 2.0, chris nowicki says to pick one stack and go deep instead of spreading yourself thin across multiple frameworks. he chose react and next.js and stuck with them to build full understanding. knowing a lot about one stack is more valuable than knowing a little about many, especially when you do not have tons of projects to show for each one.

what does chris nowicki say about building in public for job seekers?

on guidance counselor 2.0, chris nowicki says to write blog posts about what you are learning, even if others have already covered the same topic. writing helps you retain information better, and those articles can be attached when employers ask for work samples. building in public does not mean becoming a content creator or influencer, just documenting your learning process so you have something to show on your resume when you lack job experience.
