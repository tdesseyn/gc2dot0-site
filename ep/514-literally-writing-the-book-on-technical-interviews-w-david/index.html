ep 514 · may 1, 2026

literally writing the book on technical interviews w/ david mccarter

guest: david mccarter, software engineer, author, and microsoft mvp

  • prepare for interviews beyond reading the job description, since most candidates barely glance at it before showing up
  • ask the interviewer questions too, since interviews are a two way conversation and you need to know if you actually want the job
  • before accepting an offer, try to poke holes in it and even use ai to pressure test the details
  • use ai to generate likely interview questions from a job description, but don't let it write your resume or code from scratch for you
  • get into the habit of writing and releasing your own small projects regularly, since that builds the practical skills that separate you from ai

questions this episode answers

what does david mccarter say is the biggest mistake candidates make in technical interviews?

on guidance counselor 2.0, david mccarter says the biggest mistake is lack of preparation. too many software engineers just read the job description or talk to a few people, but if you really want the job you have to prepare a lot more. he started interviewing engineers in his very first job and has interviewed hundreds since, and poor preparation is the consistent problem he sees.

how can ai help with interview prep according to david mccarter?

on guidance counselor 2.0, david mccarter says you can give ai the job description and ask it what questions you'll likely be asked in the interview. he tested this approach when helping a young woman prep for an alaska airlines interview and was impressed with the questions ai generated. he includes this technique in the seventh edition of his technical interview book.

what does david mccarter say about using ai to write code or resumes?

on guidance counselor 2.0, david mccarter warns against letting ai do everything from scratch. he says you should write your resume or code first, then use ai to help make it sound better or fix things. he gave ai a chance to write a program from scratch without him writing any code and it didn't go well, which he details in an upcoming article.
