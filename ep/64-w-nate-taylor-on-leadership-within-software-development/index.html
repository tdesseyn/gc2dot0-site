w/nate taylor on leadership within software development

guest: nate taylor, director of software development at pluralsight

  • leadership does not require a title, leading an epic or owning a deliverable counts as real leadership experience
  • before promoting someone into management, sit down and ask if they actually want to manage people instead of assuming
  • companies often promote their best coders into leadership without checking if they have the aptitude or desire for it
  • build dual tracks so engineers can grow into deep technical expert roles without being forced into management
  • to break into software development, pick a real problem you care about and build something, then research issues as they come up

questions this episode answers

what does nate taylor say about leadership without a management title?

on guidance counselor 2.0, nate taylor says leadership does not require a formal title or management role. you can lead by owning an epic, being the point person for a major product feature, or taking responsibility for a deliverable while still coding or designing. these are real leadership experiences that involve talking to customers, coordinating with the team, and owning outcomes.

why do companies promote good engineers into bad managers according to nate taylor?

on guidance counselor 2.0, nate taylor explains that companies take phenomenal coders and promote them into leadership without checking if they want to manage people or have the aptitude for it. these engineers spent years honing technical skills, not people skills, and they often gravitate back to what they are good at like architecture. companies rarely sit down and ask if the person actually wants to manage before promoting them.
