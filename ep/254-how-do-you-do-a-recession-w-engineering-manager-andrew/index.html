ep 254 · aug 2, 2023

how do you 'do' a recession w/engineering manager, andrew wyborski

guest: andrew wyborski, engineering manager, kroger

  • treat your career the same in a boom or a bust, keep building your network and skills regardless of the economy
  • an intentional email signature and salutation make you more human in a remote first environment
  • if you get laid off, give yourself a day or two to breathe before jumping into a job search
  • job searching after a layoff should run like an eight to five job, mornings for outreach, afternoons for networking calls
  • you dont need one perfect mentor, stitch together advice from a few trusted peers who each know different things

questions this episode answers

what does andrew wyborski say to do immediately after getting laid off?

on guidance counselor 2.0, andrew wyborski says to take a day or two to breathe and let things settle before activating your network. he suggests taking time to assess your financial situation and avoid making panicked decisions. the key is to be intentional rather than immediately posting on linkedin or firing off job applications within hours of the layoff.

how should you structure your day during a job search according to taylor desseyn?

on guidance counselor 2.0, taylor desseyn says your job search should be an eight to five job with a structured routine. spend mornings from seven to eleven sending emails, texts and linkedin messages to get on people's radar before they start work. take a lunch break from eleven to one, then use afternoons from one to five for networking calls and conversations.

what does andrew wyborski say about whether to change your career approach during a recession?

on guidance counselor 2.0, andrew wyborski says you should treat your career the same in a boom or bust and not dramatically change your approach based on economic conditions. if you are intentionally building your network and growing your skills on a daily basis, the macroeconomic climate should not matter. the same practices that work in a strong economy work in a recession, so consistency matters more than reacting to what the fed says each week.
