ep 241 · apr 11, 2023

the shift to remote work and the power of content w/vp of software engineering, andrew moore

guest: andrew moore, vp of software engineering at nations lending

  • apply within the first few hours of a job posting since high volume roles fill up fast with applicants
  • skip generic easy apply templates and send a direct message explaining why you fit the role
  • when networking or applying, open with something personal or a shared connection to the work to stand out
  • if you lead remote teams, be intentional about one on ones and check ins since casual hallway contact is gone
  • build your online presence consistently on linkedin since an active profile gets noticed faster than a silent one

questions this episode answers

what does andrew moore say about applying to jobs on linkedin?

on guidance counselor 2.0, andrew moore, vp of software engineering at nations lending, says job seekers should apply within the first few hours of a posting because he received 80 applicants in just three or four hours for one role. he emphasizes that early applicants stand out, especially when there are hundreds or thousands of total applicants. candidates who reach out directly with a message explaining their fit get pushed to the front of his attention even if they are not the most technically skilled.

how does andrew moore recommend personalizing outreach to hiring managers?

on guidance counselor 2.0, andrew moore says he appreciates direct messages that show a candidate took time to understand the role, the company, and the specific value they can bring. he likes conversational writing that shows there is a real person behind the message, not just the generic linkedin template. starting with something you have in common or explaining how you can help is huge and changes the entire conversation.

what does andrew moore say about building an online presence for job seekers?

on guidance counselor 2.0, andrew moore says that when two candidates with identical skill sets and experience reach out, he talks to the person with an active linkedin presence first. he explains that having an online brand means your personality is represented before you even apply, so when you need support from your network they already know who you are and what you can do. he recommends posting consistently without overthinking it, even once to three times per week.
