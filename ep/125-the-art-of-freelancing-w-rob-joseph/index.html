ep 125 · mar 14, 2022

the art of freelancing w/ rob joseph

guest: rob joseph, android app developer and host of coffee and coding podcast

  • build a portfolio early since it proves you can do the work before you have real experience to point to
  • save enough money to cover a few months of expenses before going freelance so fear of drying up work doesn't drive bad decisions
  • expect contracting to mean set hours like a job while freelancing means delivering a result by a deadline, however long it takes you
  • referrals from other developers often become your best source of new gigs once you have done good work for someone
  • put your work where people can find it, like an app store listing or simple webpage, so your name backs up what you say you can do

questions this episode answers

what does rob joseph say about building a portfolio before freelancing?

on guidance counselor 2.0, rob joseph says a portfolio is crucial when you start freelancing because you have no experience to point to, but you might still be a better developer than someone with years of gigs. he released four personal apps before landing his first freelance client, and when he applied he showed those apps plus articles written about them. once you have a few real contracts, your resume speaks for itself and the early portfolio matters less.

how much savings does rob joseph recommend before going freelance?

on guidance counselor 2.0, rob joseph says he saved enough money to cover six months of expenses before quitting his job to freelance, giving himself six months to earn money from android work or go back to a regular job. he stresses that having a buffer removes the fear and prevents you from taking bad gigs just because you need the money. even three months of savings is plenty to get started.

what does rob joseph say is the difference between contracting and freelancing?

on guidance counselor 2.0, rob joseph explains that contracting typically means working set hours like eight hour days five days a week, similar to a job, while freelancing means you deliver a specific result by a deadline regardless of how long it takes you. with contracting you cannot bill for a full month if you finish the work in a day, but with freelancing if a month-long project takes you one day you still get paid the full amount.
