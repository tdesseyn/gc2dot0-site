ep 84 · sep 29, 2021

linkedin overview

with taylor desseyn (solo)

  • make your banner photo count since its the first thing viewers see on your linkedin profile
  • at minimum include your email in your banner since linkedin hides contact info behind a click
  • skip putting your resume in the featured section so people dm you directly instead
  • put specific keywords like exact languages or frameworks in your title since recruiters search by title not by scrolling profiles
  • add real numbers and comparisons to your accomplishments instead of vague claims like increased user retention

questions this episode answers

why does taylor desseyn say to put your email in your linkedin banner photo?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says linkedin makes it hard to find contact information because you have to click through to contact info to see someone's email. he recommends putting at minimum your email in the banner photo so recruiters can reach you immediately. if you don't know what else to include in the banner, just put your email as the bare minimum.

what does taylor desseyn say about putting your resume in the linkedin featured section?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says not to put your resume in the featured section because you want people to message you directly. if a recruiter sees your resume and it's not a fit based on how you wrote it at that time, they'll just move on. but if the resume isn't there, they have to dm you to ask for it, which opens a dialogue even if you're not a fit for that specific role.

why does taylor desseyn say to put specific programming languages in your linkedin title?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says recruiters search by title keywords because they don't have time to click through hundreds of profiles. when he searches for dot net developer in nashville and gets 1200 results, he only looks at titles showing the exact language. he recommends putting specific languages like react, node, or dot net in your title instead of just software engineer, even if you consider yourself a generalist, because your linkedin is for recruiters to find you.
