how to navigate an interview (solo show)

with taylor desseyn (solo)

  • research the company and tie your own experiences or personal connection into why you care about their mission
  • research your interviewers on linkedin and google so you can find common ground and shift the conversation
  • bring a one page sizzle sheet with three to four bullet points connecting your background to the role
  • keep a pen and paper in front of you to take notes instead of typing, since hiring managers find typing more distracting and suspicious
  • print your resume, close extra tabs, and silence your phone so you stay focused and avoid blanking during the interview

questions this episode answers

what does taylor desseyn say about researching companies before an interview?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says job seekers should research what the company does and then tie in their own experiences or personal connections to show genuine passion for the mission. he shares that he won a client project by mentioning his own foot problems when pitching to a podiatry center, demonstrating why their product mattered to him personally. people want to work with candidates who are passionate about their business, not just those who did surface-level research.

how does taylor desseyn recommend researching interviewers on linkedin?

on guidance counselor 2.0, taylor desseyn says to research the people interviewing you on linkedin and google, then bring up similarities to change the entire trajectory of the interview. he mentions that a guest named julian got his job because he researched his interviewers and tied in his similarities. if someone doesn't have much linkedin content, taylor recommends googling their name plus their company and city to find things like triathlon results or other public information you can use to build rapport.

why does taylor desseyn tell job seekers to use pen and paper instead of typing during interviews?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says every hiring manager he has talked to finds typing more distracting than handwritten notes because they worry the candidate is using chatgpt during the interview. he recommends keeping a pen and paper in front of you with three to four bullet points connecting your background to the role, plus your printed resume, so you stay focused without blanking out. writing things down also prevents you from interrupting when you lose your train of thought.
