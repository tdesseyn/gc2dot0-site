ep 261 · aug 18, 2023

scaling an engineering organization of 300+ developers

guest: ryan and yule, staff software engineer and director of engineering at volvo cars

  • use a rotating roster for technical assessments instead of asking for volunteers so reviews don't stall out
  • give take-home assignments a time cap and ask candidates to prioritize what they solve so you see judgment not just free time
  • submit interviewer feedback separately before comparing notes so the panel doesn't bias each other
  • have recruiters lead candidate-to-team matching once they know the tech space well enough to spot the right fit
  • set expectations early that candidates may not know their exact team until later in a fast-growing org

questions this episode answers

what does ryan from volvo say about rotating technical reviewers instead of asking for volunteers?

on guidance counselor 2.0, ryan, a staff software engineer at volvo cars, says moving from a volunteer pool to a mandatory rotating roster cut assignment turnaround from two weeks to 48 hours. recruiters assign the next person in the roster and give them 48 hours to complete the review unless they are on leave or traveling. he credits the recruiting team for identifying the bottleneck and implementing the roster.

how does volvo structure take-home coding tests to avoid bias toward candidates with free time?

on guidance counselor 2.0, ryan from volvo cars says they give candidates a four-hour time cap and explicitly ask them to prioritize what they solve. the test is a tax calculation problem similar to volvo's work, and reviewers evaluate not just what was completed but also what was left undone and why. this way someone who completes everything is not automatically scored higher because they may have spent extra time.

what does yule from volvo say about submitting interviewer feedback separately before comparing notes?

on guidance counselor 2.0, yule, director of engineering at volvo cars, says technical interviewers and shadowers write their feedback completely separately so they cannot see each other's assessments before submitting. this prevents one interviewer from biasing the other. the same practice applies in culture interviews, where both the lead and shadow submit independent feedback to the hiring manager.
