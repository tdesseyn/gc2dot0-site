ep 261 · aug 18, 2023

scaling an engineering organization of 300+ developers

guest: ryan and yule, staff software engineer and director of engineering at volvo cars

  • use a rotating roster for technical assessments instead of asking for volunteers so reviews don't stall out
  • give take-home assignments a time cap and ask candidates to prioritize what they solve so you see judgment not just free time
  • submit interviewer feedback separately before comparing notes so the panel doesn't bias each other
  • have recruiters lead candidate-to-team matching once they know the tech space well enough to spot the right fit
  • set expectations early that candidates may not know their exact team until later in a fast-growing org

what ryan and yule cover in this episode

ryan is a staff software engineer at volvo cars, working with three teams on pricing: price calculations, taxes and discounts, so his systems sit behind almost any volvo price you see online or at a dealership. yule is director of engineering for the same area, after years at tech startups in gothenburg, sweden. their part of volvo hired more than 300 developers in a year, and this episode is a behind the scenes look at how they kept hiring fast without letting quality or fairness slip.

fix the bottleneck with a rotating roster

early on, technical assessments went to a pool of engineers in a single slack channel, and recruiters would ask who was free to review one. nobody raised their hand. reviews took about two weeks, and they lost strong applicants because they were too slow. the fix came from the recruiters: put every reviewer on a rotating roster, assign the next person, and give them 48 hours. you can pass if you're out or traveling, and it moves to the next name. ryan says most assessments now come back within 48 hours of submission.

a take-home with a time cap

taylor's view is that you should meet candidates where they are, since some people have kids and no time for a take-home. ryan agrees the bias is real: a take-home favors people with a stable home life and spare time, and more hours usually means a better submission. so they cap it at four hours and ask candidates to prioritize what they solve, because there's more than four hours of work in it. what you leave undone is part of the evaluation, and so is what you chose to focus on. the problem itself is a tax calculation, kept close to the team's real work.

keep interviewers from biasing each other

after the take-home, ryan leads a technical interview: walking through the assessment with some pair programming and system design questions. a second engineer shadows, and both submit feedback completely separately, so neither can see the other's take first.

the culture interview, which yule covers, works the same way: always one lead and one shadow, on a rotation, so he isn't interviewing candidates for his own team at that stage. they follow a script to keep it standardized but stay flexible enough to dig into things. it's only an hour, so they spend at least five minutes setting expectations at the start and leave time for questions at the end. the script covers volvo cars' core competencies.

let recruiters do the matching

at first, a panel helped decide which team each candidate should meet. over time the recruiters got so good at knowing the hiring managers, the teams and the tech space that the panel wasn't needed. ryan says recruiters now come to him excited about a candidate and push for a fast review, and the team relies on them heavily.

be honest that the team will change

yule says they're transparent that in an org growing by around 20 percent, the team you'd join may look different by your first day, so they can't always promise you'll meet it. they want people who join because the challenge interests them, and he says retention hasn't been a problem. the harder question, from a listener, was how to keep hiring juniors while things change so fast. yule calls onboarding junior people in that environment one of their biggest challenges.

questions this episode answers

what does ryan from volvo say about rotating technical reviewers instead of asking for volunteers?

on guidance counselor 2.0, ryan, a staff software engineer at volvo cars, says moving from a volunteer pool to a mandatory rotating roster cut assignment turnaround from two weeks to 48 hours. recruiters assign the next person in the roster and give them 48 hours to complete the review unless they are on leave or traveling. he credits the recruiting team for identifying the bottleneck and implementing the roster.

how does volvo structure take-home coding tests to avoid bias toward candidates with free time?

on guidance counselor 2.0, ryan from volvo cars says they give candidates a four-hour time cap and explicitly ask them to prioritize what they solve. the test is a tax calculation problem similar to volvo's work, and reviewers evaluate not just what was completed but also what was left undone and why. this way someone who completes everything is not automatically scored higher because they may have spent extra time.

what does yule from volvo say about submitting interviewer feedback separately before comparing notes?

on guidance counselor 2.0, yule, director of engineering at volvo cars, says technical interviewers and shadowers write their feedback completely separately so they cannot see each other's assessments before submitting. this prevents one interviewer from biasing the other. the same practice applies in culture interviews, where both the lead and shadow submit independent feedback to the hiring manager.
