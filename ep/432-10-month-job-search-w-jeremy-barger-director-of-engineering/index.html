10 month job search w/ jeremy barger, director of engineering

guest: jeremy barger, director of engineering

  • rewrite a resume that has not changed in years, since old formats stop getting callbacks even with strong experience
  • do not be afraid to let a resume run two or three pages if it is tailored and highlights specific tech and accomplishments
  • clean up your linkedin headline and attach skills to each job so recruiter searches for your title actually surface your profile
  • get involved in online communities and groups even when not job hunting, since those relationships can lead to referrals and tips later
  • follow up after every interview and rejection with a thank you, since hiring managers keep track of who to call first for the next opening

questions this episode answers

what does jeremy barger say about updating a resume that hasn't gotten callbacks in years?

on guidance counselor 2.0, jeremy barger says he used the same resume format for 15 years and suddenly got zero callbacks after his 2023 layoff. after working with taylor to change the format, add a professional summary highlighting specialties and years of experience, and expand from one page to two or three pages with detailed accomplishments and technologies, he immediately started getting responses. he emphasizes that old resume formats stop working even when your experience is strong, so ask for help and tailor the content for the current market.

how does jeremy barger recommend fixing linkedin to get recruiter attention?

on guidance counselor 2.0, jeremy barger says he updated his linkedin headline and started adding skills to specific jobs, listing the technologies and leadership work for each position. a recruiter who eventually hired him mentioned that the skills showing on his profile aligned perfectly with the role. once he made these changes, recruiters started reaching out constantly, whereas before the updates his profile was invisible in searches.

what does jeremy barger do with candidates who send thank you notes after rejection?

on guidance counselor 2.0, jeremy barger says he keeps an email folder for every person who sends a followup or thank you after the interview process, even if they were rejected. if he ever needs someone for a future role, those are the people he will go to first. he recommends following up after every interview and rejection because hiring managers remember and track who was kind and professional.
