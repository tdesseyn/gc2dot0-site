ep 124 · mar 11, 2022

creativity in hiring especially around junior devs w/ andrew wyborski

guest: andrew wyborski, engineering manager at kroger

  • when hiring junior devs, look past resumes for stories of them automating or improving a tedious part of their old job
  • use coding exams as a conversation starter, not a pass-fail gate, so interviews focus on problem solving not syntax
  • if you cannot find time to teach new hires basic skills, that is a sign you are not investing in your current team either
  • build your network before you need it, join local dev meetups and keep a master resume with every skill and project listed
  • hiring managers should push junior talent into uncomfortable stretch goals since that discomfort is what drives real growth

questions this episode answers

what does andrew wyborski look for when hiring junior developers?

on guidance counselor 2.0, andrew wyborski, engineering manager at kroger, says he looks for candidates who automated or improved tedious processes in their previous non-tech jobs. he wants to see stories of people solving problems creatively, like someone who wrote macros to automate manual reporting work after environmental impact studies. these nuggets show someone who wants to eliminate unimportant tasks to focus on what matters, which is the developer mindset he values.

how should hiring managers use coding exams according to andrew wyborski?

on guidance counselor 2.0, andrew wyborski says coding exams should be a conversation starter, not a pass-fail gate. he uses them to gather information upfront so that when he talks to candidates, they discuss the problem and the thought process from idea to working software, rather than writing code on the fly or worrying about syntax. this approach uses the tool to build a full profile of the candidate instead of making it the end of the conversation.

what does andrew wyborski say about teaching new hires basic skills?

on guidance counselor 2.0, andrew wyborski says if a hiring manager claims they do not have time to teach new hires basic skills, that is a signal they are not teaching their current team either. he argues this should make you question whether you want to work there, because if they cannot invest in teaching newcomers, they are probably not investing in anyone's growth. he tells hiring managers that if they truly cannot teach, they might as well close up shop because someone will overtake them.
