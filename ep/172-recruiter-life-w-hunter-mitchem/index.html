recruiter life w/ hunter mitchem

guest: hunter mitchem, agency recruiter focused on software development

  • ask about company financials and funding before accepting a job to gauge stability
  • find a recruiter with tenure who knows the local market and specific tech stacks
  • keep your linkedin profile updated since recruiters rely on it to match you to roles
  • check in with recruiters every week or two so you stay top of mind for new roles
  • give recruiters a salary range and trust their read on where you stack up in the market

questions this episode answers

what does hunter mitchem say to ask companies before accepting a job offer?

on guidance counselor 2.0, hunter mitchem says candidates should ask about company financials, funding sources, how much funding they received, and what project they'll be working on. he explains that understanding whether a project is critical to the organization or just in maintenance mode affects job security during layoffs. only about 30 percent of candidates he talks to ask these questions, but they should.

how does hunter mitchem recommend finding the right agency recruiter?

on guidance counselor 2.0, hunter mitchem says to look for recruiters in your city who have tenure at reputable companies, because they know which local companies use which tech stacks and can informally recommend places even if they don't work with them. he suggests reaching out for a 15 to 30 minute call or meeting for coffee or lunch, because candidates he meets in person become personal friends and stay top of mind when new roles come in.

what does hunter mitchem say about keeping in touch with recruiters during a job search?

on guidance counselor 2.0, hunter mitchem says candidates should check in with recruiters every week or two because recruiters talk to so many people that they can forget about you within a week, even if the initial conversation went well. he explains that staying in touch keeps you top of mind, and a recruiter might have just gotten a perfect role ten minutes ago that you'd be ideal for.
