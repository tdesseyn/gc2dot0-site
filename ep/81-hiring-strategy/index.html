ep 81 · sep 22, 2021

hiring strategy

with taylor desseyn (solo)

  • if your hiring process takes longer than two weeks, something in it is broken
  • let recruiters handle scheduling and logistics so interviews move fast, not just sourcing candidates
  • give candidates specific feedback like three bullet points on strengths and gaps instead of vague rejections
  • if a great candidate is not right for the role you interviewed them for, push to find them another fit in the company
  • as a candidate, ask directly at the end of an interview how to follow up and what the next steps are

questions this episode answers

what does taylor desseyn say about how long a hiring process should take?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says that if a company cannot hire within two weeks, the process is broken. he shares an example from channel advisor where they hired 15 people in 60 days by doing a one-hour phone call followed by an immediate on-site interview. the number of interview steps does not matter as long as the company completes them all within two weeks and stays incredibly efficient.

what does taylor desseyn say recruiters should handle beyond just finding candidates?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says recruiters can and should handle administrative tasks like booking flights, arranging travel, and managing logistics to keep the interview process moving quickly. he explains that companies often do not realize recruiters can do more than sourcing, and letting recruiters organize scheduling and travel helps eliminate delays. he also emphasizes that recruiters need to ask better questions upfront to understand why a position is open and what kind of personality fits the team.

what does taylor desseyn say hiring managers should do when they love a candidate but not for the specific role?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says that if he loves a candidate but not for the specific role, he would immediately go to hr and ask where else in the organization that person could fit. he explains that companies should hire for overall talent within an organization instead of only for the specific position, and he would not move on to interview another candidate until he figured out where to use that person because people are the most important asset.
