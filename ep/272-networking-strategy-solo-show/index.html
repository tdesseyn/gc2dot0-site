networking strategy (solo show)

with taylor desseyn (solo)

  • treat networking like a meetup where showing up and saying hey is the first step, then engage further in comments
  • find a common denominator with someone, like a shared interest, to start a real connection
  • keep conference conversations short, five minutes covering what someone does and whether they like it is plenty
  • recruiters focus on the body of your resume, not links to portfolios or github, so make that content count
  • after meeting people at a conference the follow up afterward is where the real relationship and opportunity happens

questions this episode answers

what does taylor desseyn say about how long conference conversations should last?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says conference conversations should be about five minutes, not thirty. he recommends a simple exchange: ask what someone does, whether they like it, get their linkedin, then move on. he believes people put way too much pressure on themselves when networking at events.

what does taylor desseyn recommend about following up after a conference?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says the follow up after a conference is probably the most important part. he emphasizes that meeting people at a conference is just the first step, and the follow up is where the magic happens. he advises sending a message after the event to reconnect and continue the conversation.

what does taylor desseyn say about whether recruiters look at portfolio links in resumes?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says recruiters do not click on links to portfolios or github in resumes. he explains that recruiters only care about what is in the body of the resume itself. he suggests putting portfolio and github information in a cover letter or something you can pass over separately instead.
