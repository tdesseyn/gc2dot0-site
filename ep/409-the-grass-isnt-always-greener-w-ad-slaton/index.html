the grass isn't always greener w/ ad slaton

guest: ad slaton, senior director of software engineering, wbd

  • staying long term at one company can come down to people, meaningful work, and real work-life balance
  • keep an internal checklist to separate industry-wide problems from company-specific problems before deciding to leave

  • treat documentation as a core skill, writing things down helps new hires and global remote teams ramp up faster

questions this episode answers

what does ad slaton say about why he stayed at warner brothers for 13 years?

on guidance counselor 2.0, ad slaton, senior director of software engineering at wbd, says three things kept him at warner brothers for 13 years: the people and relationships he built, the compelling work at scale that millions of people use, and work-life balance that lets him be present for his three kids. he also values that the company invested in culture and allowed him to grow from a contract software engineer to senior director.

what does ad slaton recommend before deciding to leave a job?

on guidance counselor 2.0, ad slaton says to have frank, honest conversations with your manager before deciding to leave. he moved internally multiple times by telling his manager he was unhappy or thinking about leaving, which opened up other opportunities within the organization. he also suggests checking whether issues are company-specific or industry-wide problems you would face anywhere.

why does ad slaton say documentation matters for software engineers?

on guidance counselor 2.0, ad slaton says documentation is an underrated skill that helps new hires and global remote teams get up to speed without constant meetings. he gave an intern documentation on day one, and within five days the intern understood the system and could articulate what they were trying to accomplish. for global teams, writing things down means people in different time zones can learn without him being on every zoom call.
