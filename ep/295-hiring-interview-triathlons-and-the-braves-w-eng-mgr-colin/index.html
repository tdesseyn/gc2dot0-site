hiring, interview, triathlons, and the braves w/eng mgr colin lord

guest: colin lord, engineering manager at smile direct club

  • when hiring, look for people who stay calm and solve problems when production goes down instead of panicking or blaming others
  • identifying the problem is the hardest part of any outage, fixing it is usually the easy part once you know what broke
  • you do not need to match every bullet on a job posting, be honest about gaps and show willingness to learn
  • good communication and humility set candidates apart more than technical skills, since technical skills are easier to teach
  • during take home assessments, write comments explaining your thought process to show humility and communication even without live interaction

questions this episode answers

what does colin lord look for when hiring engineers during interviews?

on guidance counselor 2.0, colin lord, engineering manager at smile direct club, says he tries to answer one question: would i go into battle with you when production is down and we don't know why. he looks for people who stay calm under pressure, approach problems analytically, and don't freeze up or blame others when things break. the worst thing someone can do when production is down is start saying it's not their fault and blame somebody else.

what does colin lord say about soft skills versus technical skills when hiring developers?

on guidance counselor 2.0, colin lord says there are a lot more people who know react than know how to communicate and have humility. he would hire somebody with weaker coding skills but stronger soft skills because he can teach react much easier than he can teach someone how to be a good communicator. communication and humility set candidates apart more than technical abilities because technical skills are easier to teach.

how can someone show soft skills during a take home coding assessment according to colin lord?

on guidance counselor 2.0, colin lord says you can demonstrate humility and soft skills in a take home assessment by writing comments in your code. explain what you're doing and explain your thought process through comments, helping the interviewer learn who you are both through your code and through your comments. this lets you show communication ability even without live interaction.
