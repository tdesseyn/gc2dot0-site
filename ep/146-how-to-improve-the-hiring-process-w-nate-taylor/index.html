how to improve the hiring process w/ nate taylor

guest: nate taylor, software engineering director at pluralsight

  • write resume bullets that explain the problem you solved, not just the tech stack or process you used
  • a resume with a few sentences of context can tell a hiring manager more than ten generic bullet points
  • employment gaps are not a dealbreaker if your total experience still shows you can do the job
  • long interview processes with too many touch points can cause good candidates to drop out on their own
  • before hiring, weigh the time to find a perfect fit against the time it takes to ramp up someone less experienced

questions this episode answers

what does nate taylor say makes a resume stand out to hiring managers?

on guidance counselor 2.0, nate taylor says he wants to see the problems candidates solved, not just lists of technologies or process buzzwords like agile sprints. a few sentences explaining what you built, for whom, and why it mattered can tell him more than ten bullet points. he suggests weaving your tech stack into a short story about the user problem you addressed.

does nate taylor care about employment gaps on a resume?

on guidance counselor 2.0, nate taylor says gaps in employment do not bother him and will not eliminate a candidate. he looks at total experience instead, understanding that gaps can happen for education, visa sponsorship, or other reasons. if someone has five years of solid experience before a gap, he considers them still qualified even if they need to catch up on new frameworks.

what does nate taylor say about hiring for ramp-up time versus waiting for a perfect fit?

on guidance counselor 2.0, nate taylor says hiring managers should weigh the time it takes to find a perfect candidate against the time needed to train someone less experienced. if it takes six months to find an ideal hire plus two to three months to onboard them, that is eight or nine months total, versus finding someone in three months and spending the next months ramping them up. he personally leans toward bringing people in and developing them, though the decision depends on the team's maturity and mentoring capacity.
