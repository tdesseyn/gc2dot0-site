ep 26 · mar 3, 2021

recruiter specialization w/jonah dimeo

guest: jonah dimeo, cyber security recruiter at vaco (morgan franklin), charlotte

  • ask a recruiter directly if they specialize in your field before working with them on your search
  • interview the recruiter as much as they interview you to see if they actually understand your tech stack
  • check a recruiter's linkedin connections to see if most are in your field, that signals real specialization
  • if you're a junior developer, ask recruiters what skills show up on placed candidates' resumes that you're missing
  • aim to work with a recruiter who has been at the same company and in your local market for at least two years

questions this episode answers

what does jonah dimeo say about how to find a specialized recruiter?

on guidance counselor 2.0, jonah dimeo says to look at a recruiter's linkedin connections to see if most are in your field, which signals real specialization. he explains that if you look at taylor's connections they are mostly app dev people, while his own connections are mostly cybersecurity. jonah also recommends simply asking the recruiter directly if they specialize in your area during your first conversation.

what should a junior developer ask a recruiter according to jonah dimeo?

on guidance counselor 2.0, jonah dimeo says junior developers should ask recruiters what skills appear on the resumes of candidates who are actually getting placed that they themselves are missing. he recommends asking for constructive criticism about your resume and interview practices. jonah emphasizes that junior developers should work with a senior recruiter who can provide career advice rather than another junior person.

how long should a recruiter have been in the market according to taylor desseyn?

on guidance counselor 2.0, taylor desseyn recommends working with a recruiter who has been at the same company for at least two years and in your local market for at least two years. he calls this the two and two rule and explains that after two years in a market, recruiters start to really understand what is happening locally.
