ep 506 · dec 8, 2025

navigating recruiters - solo show

with taylor desseyn (solo)

  • treat recruiters as one of five pillars in your job search alongside networking, cold dms, job boards, and applying directly
  • with internal recruiters at your target companies, connect on linkedin, engage with their posts, and build rapport before ever asking for help
  • when an agency recruiter has no immediate role, ask how your resume stacks up, what to fix, how your salary expectations compare, and how often to follow up
  • before trusting a recruiter who messages you about a job, ask how long they have worked the role, if a contract is in place, who the hiring manager is, and if they have placed people there before
  • if multiple recruiters pitch you the same role, getting double submitted can get you rejected, so pick the one who can answer your questions fastest and ask about a right to represent

questions this episode answers

what five questions does taylor desseyn say to ask a recruiter who messages you about a job?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says to ask how long they have been recruiting on the role, whether they have a contract in place, who the hiring manager is, whether they have had an intake call with notes, and how many people they have placed with that manager or company. these questions help you cut through spam and understand if the recruiter truly knows the role.

what does taylor desseyn say to ask an agency recruiter when they have no immediate role for you?

on guidance counselor 2.0, taylor desseyn says to ask where your resume or skill set stacks up compared to others they see in your industry, what one thing you should improve on your resume and linkedin, where your salary expectations compare to the market, and what a good follow up cadence looks like. agency recruiters look at resumes every day and can give you real market insight even when they do not have a job for you right now.

what does taylor desseyn say about getting double submitted by recruiters for the same job?

on guidance counselor 2.0, taylor desseyn says if two recruiters submit you to the same job, you can get rejected because it makes you look unorganized and bad with details on the hiring side. he recommends asking the five screening questions to each recruiter and going with whichever one can answer fastest, or asking to sign a right to represent so only one firm submits you.
