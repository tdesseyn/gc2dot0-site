landing an interview (solo show)

with taylor desseyn (solo)

  • mass applying to jobs right now rarely works because recruiters get flooded with applicants for a single role
  • treat every linkedin visit like walking into a networking event and actually engage with people's posts
  • rewrite your resume's professional summary to match the specific skills listed in each job posting
  • when linkedin limits your dms, send a connection request with a short note instead to reach the top of their inbox
  • if you're a new developer with no experience, offer to build something for a small business or creator to prove your skills

questions this episode answers

what does taylor desseyn say about mass applying to jobs right now?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says mass applying your resume is not going to work right now because recruiters are getting flooded with applicants. he mentions kelly vaughn, a director of engineering, received over a thousand applicants for a single mid-level engineer role. he recommends abandoning the strategy of just submitting your resume to big corporate environments.

how does taylor desseyn recommend networking on linkedin?

on guidance counselor 2.0, taylor desseyn says every time you open linkedin, it is like going to a networking event, but most people just scroll and don't engage. he recommends consistently engaging with people's posts by commenting, not just scrolling. he gives examples of people who are always present and engaged, and says if they ever need help, he will do his best to help them because they showed up consistently.

what does taylor recommend for bootcamp grads with no experience?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn recommends going to every small business, friend, and family member to ask if they need something built. he shares a story of a two-year react engineer who volunteered his time for two years to gain experience without pay. he emphasizes that just having a capstone project or boot camp project is not enough anymore, and hiring managers need to see you built something for somebody.
