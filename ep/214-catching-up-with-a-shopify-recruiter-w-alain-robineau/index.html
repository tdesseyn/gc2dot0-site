ep 214 · feb 1, 2023

catching up with a shopify recruiter w/alain robineau

guest: alain robineau, recruiter at shopify focused on data science

  • when giving rejection feedback, offer candidates a quick call instead of just an email so they understand where they fell short
  • most candidates offered a feedback call will not book it, so dont expect high uptake even when you offer
  • before messaging someone at a company, check their profile so you ask about the right specialty instead of a generic question
  • stand out by sending a pointed message that connects your background to the specific role instead of a generic pitch
  • in a tough market be intentional with outreach, tailor your resume to the job description, and get active in slack or discord communities instead of lurking

what alain robineau covers in this episode

alain robineau is a recruiter at shopify, based in ottawa, who recruits only for data science. like many recruiters, he didn't start there. he spent about eight years at a canadian university helping students land internships with tech companies, then moved in-house to a company whose hiring team he already knew. at the time of this episode he'd been at shopify for 11 months. taylor, an agency recruiter, brings him on to talk through the corporate side: feedback, how to stand out, and what a tough market means for job seekers.

rejection feedback, face to face

taylor asks how companies can give feedback without worrying about hr consequences. alain says you never stop being careful, and you have to read the person you're talking to. but when he sends a rejection email, he immediately includes a link to book time with him, then walks the candidate through where they fell short. he says it's been well received so far, and he hears from many candidates that it's the first time anyone has taken the time to explain. he also points out that people often reapply several times before getting in, so feedback helps them grow.

there's a values angle too. shopify wants people who take feedback and act on it, so when a candidate sits down receptive and starts building a plan, alain notes it in their profile for next time. taylor adds that he once polled his audience: would you send friends to a company that gave you feedback, even after rejecting you? the answer was a resounding yes.

find the right person before you message

alain's first tip on standing out is to find the right person. he once got a nice message from a software engineer asking what shopify looks for in software engineers, but his profile clearly says he only recruits for data science. he could talk about mission and values, but not what those hiring teams want. check someone's profile before you reach out.

what works better is a pointed message. something like: i want to be a data scientist, here's what in my background is relevant to shopify, how does that line up? if that hooks him, he'll look at your resume and keep the conversation going. effort matters too. his linkedin summary invites people to ask how his fiddle lessons are going, and one person wrote that they were taking guitar lessons and suggested they jam. all they did was read his summary.

what a tough market means

alain says there are still people hiring, but you have to be intentional with outreach and tailor your resume to every job description, or you're just throwing in paper. when the market gets scared, companies tend to hire more senior people first. he suggests looking at startups that are growing fast, where there may be room for people who aren't senior yet, as long as you can show what you bring.

he and taylor both push communities. taylor is in several slack groups and discords, and alain says engagement matters more than lurking. ask questions, give back, and share a different take. it's scary at first, but people won't jump down your throat.

his tip for hiring managers is short: know what you want. his advice to his younger self: don't be afraid to make mistakes. believe in yourself, take chances and put yourself out there.

questions this episode answers

what does alain robineau say about offering rejection feedback to candidates?

on guidance counselor 2.0, alain robineau says he gives candidates a link to book time with him immediately after sending a rejection email so they can discuss where they fell short. he finds that more than 50 percent of candidates offered this feedback call do not actually book it. he believes offering face-to-face feedback helps candidates understand what they need to improve and shows alignment with company values around receiving and acting on feedback.

how does alain robineau suggest job seekers message recruiters on linkedin?

on guidance counselor 2.0, alain robineau says candidates must find the right person and check their profile before reaching out. he received a message from a software engineer asking for advice even though his profile clearly states he only recruits for data science. he recommends sending a pointed message that highlights what in your background is relevant for that specific company and role, rather than a generic pitch asking what they look for.

what does alain robineau recommend for job seekers in a tough market?

on guidance counselor 2.0, alain robineau says job seekers need to be intentional in their outreach and craft a resume that addresses each specific job description rather than sending generic applications. he also emphasizes getting active in slack or discord communities rather than just lurking, saying engagement helps people notice you and allows you to ask questions and share different viewpoints.
