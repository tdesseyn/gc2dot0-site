ep 260 · aug 16, 2023

handling an uncertain market w/james quick

guest: james quick, developer, speaker, and content creator

  • when you get laid off, resist posting a generic update and instead lean on relationships you already built in your community
  • share your work consistently on twitter, discord, or a blog so people can verify your skills before you ever apply
  • treat content as proof for your resume claims, since anyone can watch your videos or read your posts and see if the skill is real
  • if a recruiter or contact does not respond, follow up with empathy a couple more times instead of sending canned repeated messages
  • ask your employer for time and resources to learn new things rather than assuming all growth has to happen on unpaid personal time

questions this episode answers

what does james quick say about posting on social media after getting laid off?

on guidance counselor 2.0, james quick says that when he was laid off from planetscale, he posted a simple message on twitter saying he had been let go and asked if anyone was hiring. overnight he received 100 direct messages from people offering job opportunities or saying they would love to hire him. he warns this may sound insensitive because most people do not have that network, but it shows the power of building relationships and creating content before you need them.

how does james quick recommend developers start building a network?

on guidance counselor 2.0, james quick recommends just being active somewhere like twitter, discord, or a blog. he says you do not even need to create full posts at first; responding to others and participating in conversations counts. share your work consistently so people can see what you know, because content validates the words on your resume in a way employers can actually verify before an interview.

how many times should you follow up with someone who does not respond, according to james quick?

on guidance counselor 2.0, james quick says three messages is the maximum before it becomes spam. he recommends not repeating the same message; instead, show empathy by acknowledging the person is busy and explaining why talking to them matters to you. he also warns against canned sequences or staged messages because they are impersonal and annoying.
