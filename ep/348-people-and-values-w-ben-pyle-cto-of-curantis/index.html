ep 348 · mar 29, 2024

people and values w/ ben pyle, cto of curantis

guest: ben pyle, cto of curantis

  • hire on attitude and aptitude, not just technical skill, since both can be taught but good character is harder to find
  • reaching out directly to someone with hiring authority can pay off, even casually, if you show genuine skill and care
  • executive coaching can create real change if you find a coach you connect with and you do the introspective work
  • learn what makes your people's eyes light up, then put them in roles and projects that match those interests
  • invest in yourself consistently through content and networking, since showing growth in public makes hiring easier

questions this episode answers

what does ben pyle say about hiring developers without technical interviews?

on guidance counselor 2.0, ben pyle, cto of curantis, says he hires almost specifically on attitude and aptitude rather than technical skill alone. he spent an hour reviewing a candidate's personal serverless project and hired him without a formal technical interview because he had already seen the candidate's care, detail, and capability. he emphasizes asking whether someone is a good human who can work in a team.

how does ben pyle recommend finding out what motivates your team members?

on guidance counselor 2.0, ben pyle, cto of curantis, recommends learning what makes each person's eyes light up by observing which projects and assignments generate follow-up questions. when someone asks more questions, they are growing and engaged; when they do not, they either already know it or hate the work. once you identify what delights them, put them in roles and projects aligned with those interests so they naturally want to go there.

what does ben pyle say about defining your personal values for your career?

on guidance counselor 2.0, ben pyle, cto of curantis, says he initially thought defining personal values was hokey but discovered they are powerful after working with an executive coach. values act as a filter for decisions, relationships, and interactions; when you feel icky about a situation, it is usually because it does not match your values. he lists his own as selflessness, authenticity, creativity, empathy, and pragmatism, and recommends finding a coach you connect with to do the introspective work.
