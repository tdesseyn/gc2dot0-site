mindset in the job search w/ jasmine vo

guest: jasmine vo

  • after getting laid off, take time to actually process it instead of jumping straight back into work the next week
  • treat a layoff as a transition, not a personal failure, since company funding and budget issues are rarely about you
  • if you like structure, college can help, but self-paced routes like 100devs can get you into tech in a few months
  • block off set hours each day to network and learn, just like you would treat a 9 to 5 job
  • find a mentor early so you learn about career paths you don't know exist instead of just following the path others expect

questions this episode answers

what does jasmine vo say about processing a layoff before jumping back into work?

on guidance counselor 2.0, jasmine vo says that after her first layoff she jumped into work a week later without processing it, which was a mistake. she felt like a failure because she never took time to realize the layoff was about company funding issues, not her performance. when her startup ran out of funding, she finally took a step back to ground herself and do soul-searching through habits like 75 hard, reading, working out and walks in nature.

how does jasmine vo recommend structuring your day during a job search?

on guidance counselor 2.0, jasmine vo explains that she treated her job search like a 9 to 5 schedule before she landed her first tech role. she blocked off 9 to 5 hours every day to learn and network, mimicking the behavior of someone already working in tech. this alignment helped her prepare mentally and practically for when the job actually happened.

what does jasmine vo say about choosing between college and self-paced learning for tech?

on guidance counselor 2.0, jasmine vo says the choice depends on who you are as a person. if you like structure and in-person support, college might be the route for you. if you are self-driven and can keep yourself on a schedule, the self-paced or bootcamp route like 100devs is ideal because it saves money and still gives you access to a big online community. she mentions that nuanced fields like ai or machine learning may actually require college.
