recruiter extravaganza w/adam walters, global head of talent acquisition at polygon.io.

guest: adam walters, global head of talent acquisition at polygon.io

  • agency recruiting teaches full cycle sourcing while in house recruiting means thinking about the whole business and team growth over time
  • treat onboarding like you are earning candidate trust, not just filling a seat, since candidates are taking a chance on you too
  • set clear expectations on interview process length and stages so candidates do not panic if rounds change
  • give direct specific feedback like naming the exact skill gap instead of a vague rejection, it helps candidates and builds goodwill
  • network with recruiters before you need a job, have a real conversation, stay in touch, and let timing work in your favor later

questions this episode answers

what does adam walters say about the difference between agency and in-house recruiting?

on guidance counselor 2.0, adam walters, global head of talent acquisition at polygon.io, says that agency recruiting taught him full-cycle sourcing but he focused only on filling seats without thinking about the client's business a year down the road. in-house recruiting requires thinking about how hires affect the team in six months or a year, considering funding rounds, onboarding bandwidth, and whether the company can properly train new hires instead of just slinging offers. he wishes he had sat in the in-house role before becoming an agency recruiter because the strategic perspective would have made him better at agency work.

what does adam walters say about giving feedback to rejected candidates?

on guidance counselor 2.0, adam walters says he believes in giving direct, specific feedback instead of vague rejections, such as naming the exact skill gap like telling a candidate they lacked kubernetes experience. he records some technical assessments to provide better feedback and will tell candidates to work on specific areas and reconnect in six months for reassessment. his goal is never to just say no but to say no with a path forward, sometimes redirecting candidates to other roles that might be a better fit either at his company or elsewhere.
