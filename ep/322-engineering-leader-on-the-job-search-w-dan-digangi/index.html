engineering leader on the job search w/ dan digangi, engineering manager

guest: dan digangi, senior engineering manager

  • expect the application process across different job sites and logins to be frustrating, and plan extra time for it
  • if your usual network isn't producing leads right now, consider expanding it or approaching it differently instead of just mass applying
  • avoid blasting your resume out hundreds of times, focus instead on real conversations with connections and connections of connections
  • when emailing people to schedule time, give two to three specific days with clear time blocks so they can pick easily
  • in interviews, ask what success looks like in the first 30, 60, and 90 days to show you're focused on outcomes

questions this episode answers

what does dan digangi say about applying to jobs on different job sites?

on guidance counselor 2.0, dan digangi says the disparate application process across workday, greenhouse, lever and other sites with separate logins is heinous and frustrating. he says it eats up hours even though you want to move fast, and the endless hurdles make you want to slow down. he suggests this problem needs to be solved but notes that ziprecruiter and indeed aren't fixing it.

what does dan digangi recommend instead of blasting your resume to hundreds of jobs?

on guidance counselor 2.0, dan digangi says you should not blast your resume out five thousand times and be sad about getting two hits. instead, he recommends having real conversations with people in your network, reaching out to connections and connections of connections, and doing some content creation. he says five hundred applications won't yield much success and that networking conversations are the real way to get a job.

how does dan digangi suggest scheduling calls when reaching out to people?

on guidance counselor 2.0, dan digangi recommends being very concrete when emailing people to schedule time. he says you should give them two to three specific days with a couple of hours or a single hour block, making it easy for them to pick what works. if the times don't work, they'll suggest alternatives, but providing clear options makes coordination easier without endless back and forth.
