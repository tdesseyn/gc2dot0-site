questions this episode answers

what does rob zelt say about how most people find jobs?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 networking episode, rob zelt from unc chapel hill says that with one exception, every role in his career came through a friend recommending he apply, not cold applying. he explains that when somebody knows you or somebody knows somebody, you rise to the top immediately because of the personal connection. he advises to never stop meeting people because the more people you know, the easier it is to find a new role even when you are not looking.

what does colin say about having a blog or code portfolio as a developer?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 networking episode, colin, a senior front end engineer at smile direct, says having a blog and code pen account has unlocked more in his career than anything except maybe speaking at conferences. he wrote one medium blog post on horizontal scrolling containers that has been the number one ranking result for two years, pulling over one hundred thousand views. he says having something publicly available is useful even if you think it is not going anywhere, because one day google might index it and somebody will find it, and at worst you have a repository to reference yourself.

how should junior developers network on linkedin according to taylor desseyn and colin?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 networking episode, taylor desseyn and colin agree that the best way to get a response is to check in genuinely on how someone is doing instead of immediately asking for a job. colin says if somebody messages him just checking in to see how he is doing, he is more likely to respond than if they open with asking for a job and sending their resume. taylor advises having one intentional conversation per day and connecting with people to start real conversations instead of cold pitches.
