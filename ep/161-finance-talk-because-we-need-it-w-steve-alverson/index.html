finance talk...because we need it w/ steve alverson

guest: steve alverson, certified financial planner

  • ask if your compensation package includes an opportunity for ownership before diving into details on shares
  • know the difference between restricted shares, which are given to you, and options, which let you buy stock at a set price
  • understand your vesting schedule since most shares vest over three to five years and you forfeit unvested shares if you leave early

questions this episode answers

what does steve alverson say is the difference between restricted shares and stock options?

on guidance counselor 2.0, steve alverson explains that restricted shares are given to you by the company, while stock options give you the opportunity to buy shares at a set price that is usually substantially lower than the current market price. options let you purchase stock at a favorable strike price if the shares are in the money. with restricted shares you receive the stock outright as part of your compensation package.

what does steve alverson recommend asking a private company about equity during the interview process?

on guidance counselor 2.0, steve alverson recommends that job seekers first ask whether the compensation package includes an opportunity for ownership. once the company confirms they offer equity, candidates should ask about the vesting schedule and how long shares take to become fully theirs, since most vest over three to five years. alverson also advises asking about the company's cash reserves and financial viability, especially after experiencing how many small businesses failed in 2020 without adequate nest eggs.

what does steve alverson warn about company stock becoming too much of your net worth?

on guidance counselor 2.0, steve alverson cautions that employees should not let company stock become a huge piece of their overall nest egg, especially at smaller companies where prices can fluctuate seventy percent in a single day. he recommends considering an internal selling schedule to liquidate some shares and invest in other things so you are not putting all your eggs in one basket. this diversification protects you from the high volatility common in smaller publicly traded companies.
