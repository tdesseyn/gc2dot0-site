managing workload and delegation w/kelly vaughn, director of engineering at spotai

guest: kelly vaughn, director of engineering at spotai

  • pick one must-do task for the week, two backup tasks, and a few small tasks to use as brain breaks
  • put your most intensive deep-thinking work at the beginning of the day when you have the most energy
  • schedule brain breaks and lunch on your calendar so you actually take them instead of skipping them
  • communicate clearly what you are not going to get done so nobody is surprised later
  • delegation starts with trust, so let people do the work their own way instead of dictating your method

questions this episode answers

what does kelly vaughn recommend for structuring your weekly workload?

on guidance counselor 2.0, kelly vaughn recommends picking one must-do task for the week that would make it successful, two backup tasks to pursue after finishing the first, and three small tasks to use as brain breaks. this one-two-three strategy helps with both productivity and mental health by giving structure without overwhelming yourself. the small tasks let you shift your brain off intensive thinking work throughout the day.

when should you schedule your most intensive work according to kelly vaughn?

on guidance counselor 2.0, kelly vaughn says to put your most intensive deep-thinking work at the beginning of the day when your energy is highest. she does her deepest thinking work in the morning and saves afternoons for one-on-ones and busy work. she also recommends actually blocking lunch and brain breaks on your calendar so you take them instead of skipping them.

what does kelly vaughn say about delegation and letting people work their own way?

on guidance counselor 2.0, kelly vaughn says delegation starts with trust, and you cannot dictate how someone completes a task. when you hand something off but expect them to do it exactly your way, they do not learn and you end up thinking you should have just done it yourself. letting people execute their own way means you will either learn a more efficient method from them or they will make a mistake and learn from it.
