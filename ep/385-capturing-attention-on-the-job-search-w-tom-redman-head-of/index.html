capturing attention on the job search w/tom redman, head of dev dx

guest: tom redman, head of developer experience at convex

  • before applying, dig deep into a company's founders, history, and culture so your outreach shows genuine understanding, not a generic pitch
  • quality beats quantity in a job search, a handful of tailored, researched applications outperform mass applying
  • when reaching out to a hiring manager, skip generic resumes and instead write specific bullet points on how you see the role and the opportunities
  • if imposter syndrome hits in a new role, reach out directly to people you admire in that space and ask for their help, many will respond
  • most people are self limiting in their job search, pushing past what you think is possible often reveals you can go much further

questions this episode answers

what does tom redman say about researching a company before applying?

on guidance counselor 2.0, tom redman says quality beats quantity in a job search, and the key is diving deep into a company's founders, history, and culture before reaching out. he researched convex's founders, read the cto's phd thesis from 2012, and even built a working implementation of the system described in that thesis. this research let him email the cto directly with a demo and specific bullet points about how he saw the role, which helped him skip the normal application process entirely.

how does tom redman recommend reaching out to a hiring manager?

on guidance counselor 2.0, tom redman says skip sending a generic resume and instead write specific bullet points about how you see the role and the opportunities. he sent the cto at convex a short intro paragraph followed by 15 bullet points explaining what he thought the company was, what the opportunities were, and how he understood the role. the goal is to guarantee that the right person gives your application a real human look by showing genuine excitement and understanding.

what does tom redman say about dealing with imposter syndrome in a new role?

on guidance counselor 2.0, tom redman says reach out directly to people you admire in that space and ask for their help. when he became a product manager, he emailed a dozen product leaders and about half responded, including the head of product at instagram who called him on the phone. he says many people who are great at their jobs want to help others who also want to be great, so you can lean into that by doing research and putting yourself out there.
