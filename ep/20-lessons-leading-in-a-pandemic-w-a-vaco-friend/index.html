lessons leading in a pandemic w/a vaco friend

guest: taylor roberts, leads a team in raleigh doing sales and client delivery for technology

  • pay attention to what people say with their hands and their hearts, not just their words
  • start meetings by checking in on the person before jumping into tasks
  • let go of controlling every detail and trust your team at a deeper level
  • notice when team members start proactively checking in on each other and build that into a habit
  • as a leader, talk to your people about where they want to go and align that with business strategy instead of just dictating direction

questions this episode answers

what does taylor roberts recommend leaders do at the start of meetings during remote work?

on guidance counselor 2.0, taylor roberts says leaders should spend the first five to ten minutes of meetings checking in on the person, not just jumping straight into tasks. he explains that people need more than task-focused conversations because of everything else happening outside of work. roberts emphasizes paying attention to what people say with their hearts, not just their words, and listening for tone or signs that something is off.

how does taylor roberts say leaders should balance control and trust with their teams?

on guidance counselor 2.0, taylor roberts says he learned to control things less and trust his team at a deeper level during the pandemic. he explains that while organization is an asset, the dark side is controlling everything too much. roberts adds that letting go doesn't mean being irresponsible, but it does require knowing team members better and playing to their strengths, which means using his ears more and listening.

what does taylor roberts say about aligning individual goals with business strategy?

on guidance counselor 2.0, taylor roberts says leaders shouldn't just dictate where the team is going without input. he recommends talking to people to understand where they are capable of going and where they want to go, then aligning that with the business strategy the leader is responsible for. roberts emphasizes that leaders need a vision for where they're taking people, but they also need to know each person individually and consider where each team member can go.
