giving yourself permission w/ matt dolson, manager at deloitte

guest: matt dolson, manager, human capital at deloitte

  • look for ways to apprentice others or learn from someone with different experience, regardless of title or tenure
  • if your company lacks an apprenticeship or mentorship program, give yourself permission to start something small instead of waiting for approval
  • build in regular moments to celebrate wins on your team, no win is too small to acknowledge
  • leadership is about actions and skills anyone can show up with daily, not a title you wait to receive
  • before chasing a new role, get clear on your own values and who you are so you can tell if a culture fits

questions this episode answers

what does matt dolson say about starting an apprenticeship program if your company doesn't have one?

on guidance counselor 2.0, matt dolson, manager in human capital at deloitte, says you should give yourself permission to start something without waiting for approval. he started a weekly leadership email called matt's monday motivation for over 400,000 employees at his organization without asking anyone first. if you see a need for apprenticeship or mentorship and your company lacks a formal program, he believes you should be the person who shows up and makes something happen within reasonable boundaries.

what does matt dolson recommend about celebrating wins on your team?

on guidance counselor 2.0, matt dolson, manager in human capital at deloitte, says there is no win too small to celebrate and teams should build in regular moments for recognition. he runs a friday wellness meeting with his team where they reflect on the week and do a team start stop continue exercise to celebrate what they learned. taking time to celebrate in meetings creates a ripple effect because people notice those meetings feel better than others.

what does matt dolson say about leadership versus having a leader title?

on guidance counselor 2.0, matt dolson, manager in human capital at deloitte, distinguishes between a leader title and leadership as a practice. a leader has positional power because of their role, but leadership is skills and actions anyone can show up with daily regardless of title or tenure. he says someone with two years of experience can apprentice a new team member, and you can always demonstrate leadership by creating space for people, setting expectations, delegating, and leading by example.
