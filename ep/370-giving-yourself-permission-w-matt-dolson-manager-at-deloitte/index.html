ep 370 · may 22, 2024

giving yourself permission w/ matt dolson, manager at deloitte

guest: matt dolson, manager, human capital at deloitte

  • look for ways to apprentice others or learn from someone with different experience, regardless of title or tenure
  • if your company lacks an apprenticeship or mentorship program, give yourself permission to start something small instead of waiting for approval
  • build in regular moments to celebrate wins on your team, no win is too small to acknowledge
  • leadership is about actions and skills anyone can show up with daily, not a title you wait to receive
  • before chasing a new role, get clear on your own values and who you are so you can tell if a culture fits

what matt dolson covers in this episode

matt dolson is a manager in the human capital practice at deloitte. his work is the people side of large tech implementations and process redesigns: helping people understand what's changing and why, getting buy-in, and building the skills they need for what comes next. he describes himself as a lifelong learner who counts a day as a success if he helped someone get closer to their potential. the conversation runs through three ideas, apprenticeship, permission and celebration, plus what matt sees in his career coaching.

anyone can apprentice someone

matt uses apprenticeship to mean passing along your knowledge and real life experience so someone doesn't have to learn it the hard way. it isn't tied to title or tenure. someone two years in can apprentice a new hire, and someone ten years in can learn from a newer person who's now above them. his example: when a teammate volunteers to present, offer dry runs and real time feedback, like counting their filler words. he says people right now are hungry for that kind of insight.

give yourself permission

when listeners ask what to do if their company has no apprenticeship program, matt's answer is his second theme. we default to waiting for permission. if what you want to do is reasonable, moral and ethical, feel empowered to just start. he did exactly that with matt's monday motivation, a weekly leadership email that turns something from his week, like finishing trim in the bathroom, into a lesson about not skipping the final details. it reaches an organization of more than 400,000 people, and he started it because he saw a need, not because someone told him he could. if your company doesn't have something in place, he says, good. you get to be the one who makes it happen.

no win is too small to celebrate

matt says people yearn for celebration, and when one person pauses a meeting to recognize something, it ripples out. his team has a 30 minute wellness block every friday where they don't talk about work at all: weekend plans, what they're excited about, how they took care of their well-being. only after that human moment do they get into a 30 minute work meeting with a start, stop, continue exercise. culture, he says, is the sum of individual actions, and he shifts it one person at a time through one-on-ones where he asks what each person learned that week.

leader vs leadership

taylor's view is you don't need a title to lead. matt, who studied this in grad school, puts it this way: a leader has positional power because of their role, but leadership is a set of skills and actions anyone can show up with every day. creating space, setting expectations, delegating and letting people own something all count. at his organization, he says, you're expected to perform at the next level consistently for about a year before a promotion, so you don't get promoted into burnout.

know who you are first

from his coaching, matt says many people can't say who they are or what they value, which makes it hard to find the right role. he suggests tools like the enneagram or a forced rank of your top five values, so you can tell whether a culture will fit. his advice to his younger self: don't get caught up in perfection. there's rarely one right answer at work, so bring a point of view, fail fast and keep moving.

questions this episode answers

what does matt dolson say about starting an apprenticeship program if your company doesn't have one?

on guidance counselor 2.0, matt dolson, manager in human capital at deloitte, says you should give yourself permission to start something without waiting for approval. he started a weekly leadership email called matt's monday motivation for over 400,000 employees at his organization without asking anyone first. if you see a need for apprenticeship or mentorship and your company lacks a formal program, he believes you should be the person who shows up and makes something happen within reasonable boundaries.

what does matt dolson recommend about celebrating wins on your team?

on guidance counselor 2.0, matt dolson, manager in human capital at deloitte, says there is no win too small to celebrate and teams should build in regular moments for recognition. he runs a friday wellness meeting with his team where they reflect on the week and do a team start stop continue exercise to celebrate what they learned. taking time to celebrate in meetings creates a ripple effect because people notice those meetings feel better than others.

what does matt dolson say about leadership versus having a leader title?

on guidance counselor 2.0, matt dolson, manager in human capital at deloitte, distinguishes between a leader title and leadership as a practice. a leader has positional power because of their role, but leadership is skills and actions anyone can show up with daily regardless of title or tenure. he says someone with two years of experience can apprentice a new team member, and you can always demonstrate leadership by creating space for people, setting expectations, delegating, and leading by example.
