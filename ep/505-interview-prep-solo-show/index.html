interview prep - solo show

with taylor desseyn (solo)

  • research the company and be ready to explain why you are genuinely excited about that specific company, not just what they do
  • find out exactly who is interviewing you and research them, and tie in any shared background like schools or interests
  • before the interview ask why the position is open, what will be discussed, and how quickly they are looking to hire
  • bring a notebook to interviews, tell the interviewer you are taking notes, and do not feel pressure to answer instantly
  • after the interview ask about next steps, follow up in about 48 hours if they agree, and send a personalized thank you note on linkedin

questions this episode answers

what does taylor desseyn say to ask a recruiter or manager before an interview?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says to ask three questions before the interview: why is this position open (did someone leave, is it growth, or new budget), what will be discussed during the interview, and how quickly the company is looking to move on hiring for the role. asking these questions helps you frame your responses and understand the urgency of the hire.

how does taylor desseyn recommend using glassdoor reviews when preparing for interviews?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn advises not to directly ask the hiring manager about negative glassdoor reviews because it can be off-putting. instead, use glassdoor to craft thoughtful questions for the manager, like asking about working hours or culture. he also suggests checking the dates of reviews and who posted them, noting that reviews from different departments may not reflect the role you're interviewing for.

what does taylor desseyn say about pausing before answering interview questions?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says you do not need to answer interview questions immediately and that taking time to think actually shows you are a good thinker. silence is a powerful tool that lets you collect your thoughts instead of rambling, and hiring managers value seeing how candidates process information. he recalls telling an interviewer to give him a second to think when asked a question, even though the silence felt uncomfortable.
