how to break into tech (solo show)

with taylor desseyn (solo)

  • consider coding bootcamps over self-paced learning for the built-in alumni community and networking access
  • if self-paced, run your job search like a full-time job from eight to five every day
  • spend mornings from eight to noon networking on linkedin and twitter before studying code in the afternoon
  • when messaging someone, lead with genuine admiration, ask a specific question, then request a specific small amount of time like seven minutes
  • build in public by posting what you are learning and building so people can follow your journey and eventually reach out

questions this episode answers

what does taylor desseyn say about bootcamps versus self-paced learning for breaking into tech?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says he recommends bootcamps over self-paced learning primarily because of the built-in alumni community. he notes that when he searches linkedin for national software school alumni in nashville, over 1,000 people appear, giving bootcamp graduates an instant network to tap into. he explains that people are more likely to respond to messages from those who went through the same experiences, making the alumni network a critical advantage for job seekers.

how does taylor desseyn recommend structuring your day when learning to code self-paced?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says you should treat your job search like a full-time job from eight to five every day. he recommends spending eight to noon networking on linkedin and twitter, commenting and engaging with people, then taking a lunch break from noon to one. from one to five, you should study code and build projects, dedicating the morning hours to networking because those are the most active times on social media.

what three-step dm formula does taylor desseyn recommend for reaching out to people?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says your message should have three parts: first, lead with admiration or flattery about what the person is doing. second, ask a specific question about something you want to learn from them. third, request a specific amount of time like seven minutes, not five or ten, because seven feels more genuine and people associate five minutes with a much longer conversation.
