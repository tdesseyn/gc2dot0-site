how to survive an interview w/eddie hinkle, engineering manager at glassdoor

guest: eddie hinkle, engineering manager at glassdoor

  • treat a technical interview like pairing with a peer instead of performing for a proctor to calm your nerves
  • if youre too nervous to show your skills live, ask to switch to a take home and follow up interview
  • mark an x on a calendar every day you hate your job, and if over half the month is x days start job searching
  • document unusual situations and keep copies of your reviews since managers and companies can change without warning
  • know what you want from a role, like skill building or a big name company, before deciding whether to take a step down to get there

questions this episode answers

what does eddie hinkle say about handling nerves in technical interviews?

on guidance counselor 2.0, eddie hinkle, engineering manager at glassdoor, says to treat a technical interview like pairing with a peer coworker instead of thinking of the interviewer as a proctor watching you take a test. he tells candidates to imagine he's one of their coworkers so they can get out of their heads and communicate better. if someone is too nervous to show their skills during live coding, he offers them the option to do a take home assignment and follow up interview instead.

what does eddie hinkle recommend for knowing when to leave your job?

on guidance counselor 2.0, eddie hinkle, engineering manager at glassdoor, suggests using a calendar to track bad days at work. take a pen and mark an x on every day you think you hate working there, then at the end of the month look to see if over half the month is covered in x marks. if more than half your days are x days, it's time to start job searching before you reach emergency mode and take the wrong job out of desperation.

what does eddie hinkle say about documenting work situations?

on guidance counselor 2.0, eddie hinkle, engineering manager at glassdoor, says you should document unusual situations at work and keep copies of your performance reviews because managers and companies can change without warning. he explains that your manager can get promoted or leave and a new manager can come in, so having written records of who said what and what your reviews contained can protect you. he personally had documentation that saved him from serious trouble in past situations.
