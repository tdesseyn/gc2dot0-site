questions this episode answers

what does tim cheadle say about career ladders and setting expectations?

on guidance counselor 2.0, tim cheadle, vp of engineering at binti, says a career ladder only works when both the manager and employee clearly understand what success looks like at each level. he believes bad managers avoid setting expectations because they genuinely do not know what they want from the role. when he joined binti, he worked with staff developers to revise a vague, abstract career ladder into concrete day-to-day expectations so engineers could see exactly how they were performing.

what does tim cheadle say about promotion speed and title inflation?

on guidance counselor 2.0, tim cheadle says what matters for promotion is your impact and how much value you bring to the company, not just time in a title. he has seen engineers go from junior to senior in under a year because their impact was far ahead of expectations, but he has also worked with staff developers who were not ready for that level. he worries that promoting someone before they can handle new expectations sets them up to fail, even if they make more money.

what does tim cheadle look for in a developer's educational background?

on guidance counselor 2.0, tim cheadle says some of the best people he has worked with went to schools nobody has heard of and got degrees unrelated to tech, like history or literature. he cares most about how programmers think about problems and whether they can see patterns, not which school issued the degree. real world experience matters far more than a prestigious degree, and while a stanford or caltech on a resume is a signal, it is absolutely not a deal breaker if someone does not have that.
