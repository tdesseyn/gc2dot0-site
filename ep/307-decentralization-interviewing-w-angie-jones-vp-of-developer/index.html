questions this episode answers

what does angie jones say about referrals when applying for jobs?

on guidance counselor 2.0, angie jones says referrals put a sticker by your name in the application system that guarantees someone will actually look at your application. out of a thousand applicants for her recent developer advocate role, only two or three were referrals. she says even though a referral does not guarantee you get the job, it means at least somebody will review what you submitted instead of being lost in the pile.

how does angie jones suggest getting noticed when applying to open source companies?

on guidance counselor 2.0, angie jones recommends contributing to open source projects at companies you want to work for, even if it is just fixing a typo in the documentation. she says when your name shows up on a contribution and then again on an application, hiring managers will notice and look at your resume again. angie works on open source at tbd, so candidates can join the discord or contribute to stand out before applying.

what does angie jones say about using chatgpt for cover letters?

on guidance counselor 2.0, angie jones says do not use chatgpt to write your cover letter because hiring managers can tell when something is generated. she says chatgpt writes in very structured english rather than conversational speak, and for a developer relations role where authenticity is a key skill, a fake cover letter is especially obvious. angie recommends making your cover letter as personal as possible even if you use chatgpt for other tasks.
