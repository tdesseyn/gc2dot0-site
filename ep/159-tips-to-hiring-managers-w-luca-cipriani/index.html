ep 159 · jul 25, 2022

tips to hiring managers w/ luca cipriani

guest: luca cipriani, engineering manager at jimdo

  • as a hiring manager, look for candidates through github contributions, twitter, and open source activity, not just resumes
  • job seekers should put themselves out there online since a strong public presence can make up for a weak resume or cover letter
  • the real difference between mid and senior engineers is honest pushback and negotiation, not just technical skill
  • build diverse teams with a mix of junior and senior people since mentoring junior talent helps senior engineers grow too
  • use okrs to align business and engineering, keep objectives few and clear, and communicate them constantly across the company

questions this episode answers

what does luca cipriani say about how hiring managers should find candidates?

on guidance counselor 2.0, luca cipriani says hiring managers should actively recruit by looking at github contributions, open source projects, and social media presence rather than waiting for applications. he often contacts developers directly after seeing interesting work they've done or questions they've asked in the community. he emphasizes that the best candidates are usually not actively job searching, so managers need to reach out first and start conversations.

according to luca cipriani, what is the real difference between mid-level and senior engineers?

on guidance counselor 2.0, luca cipriani says the biggest difference between mid and senior engineers is not technical skill but honest pushback and negotiation. a mid-level engineer will say they'll try to meet a deadline, while a senior will say no and explain it's impossible by friday but feasible in three weeks. senior engineers are confident enough to push back on unrealistic demands and negotiate alternatives rather than just agreeing to everything. he values this honesty and willingness to negotiate over raw technical ability.

why does luca cipriani hire junior developers alongside senior ones?

on guidance counselor 2.0, luca cipriani says he actively looks for junior engineers because diverse teams with mixed experience levels benefit everyone. mentoring junior developers helps senior engineers grow in their own careers and become more senior. he prefers a less experienced engineer with strong communication skills over a highly technical developer who can't communicate well. building teams with juniors, mids, and seniors creates better balance than only hiring experienced people.
