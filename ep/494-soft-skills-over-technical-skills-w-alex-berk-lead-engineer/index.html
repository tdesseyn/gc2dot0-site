ep 494 · oct 13, 2025

soft skills over technical skills w/ alex berk, lead engineer

guest: alex berk, lead engineer at shipium

  • when you don't have much experience yet, lead with your network and your willingness to ask for help
  • research the person interviewing you ahead of time and look for a real connection like hometown, school, or shared interests
  • you cannot skip technical skills entirely, you still need enough to get past the first screening round
  • keep building your own small projects between jobs, like an app or tool, so you always have something recent to show and talk about
  • cold dming people on linkedin or twitter about roles or questions can lead directly to interviews, even without a warm intro

questions this episode answers

what does alex berk say about getting hired without technical interviews?

on guidance counselor 2.0, alex berk says he landed multiple jobs primarily through soft skills and personal connections rather than technical ability alone. at michelin he did no technical interviews and was hired because they liked him as a person and believed he could communicate across teams. when he interviewed in seattle, one principal engineer voted no after alex bombed an algorithm question, but every other interviewer overrode that vote because alex connected so well with them personally, including bonding over shared michigan roots.

how did alex berk get his first job out of college?

on guidance counselor 2.0, alex berk says he walked up to a ceo after a guest lecture at clemson when everyone else left the room, shook his hand, and told him directly he was graduating soon and looking for work in the area. they talked for fifteen or twenty minutes about alex's coursework and projects, the ceo called him the next day, alex interviewed that friday, and he had an offer the following week before he even graduated.

what does alex berk recommend researching before an interview?

on guidance counselor 2.0, alex berk says he stalks every interviewer beforehand by looking at their linkedin, facebook if public, instagram, personal portfolios, and work history to find personal connections ahead of time. he looks for shared geography, schools, or interests he can naturally work into conversation to disarm people. for example, connecting over michigan roots has helped him in multiple interviews, and he says finding these connections is more important than just solving the technical problem.
