questions this episode answers

what does aaron harmon say about applying to jobs you don't qualify for?

on guidance counselor 2.0, aaron harmon says you should not apply to roles where you clearly don't meet the stated requirements unless you explain the gap in a cover letter. he notes that when job descriptions have explicit requirements and you don't meet them or work in a non-adjacent industry, the desire to apply shouldn't be there. if you are changing industries and have cross-relatable skills, write a cover letter to explain yourself because without context the recruiter won't understand why you applied.

how does aaron harmon recommend organizing keywords on a resume?

on guidance counselor 2.0, aaron harmon says if you have must-have experience like a specific programming language, put it near the top of your resume so it's easy to find. he explains that most recruiters use control f to search for keywords, so if a job requires c plus plus and you only list it once at the bottom, you should move it to the top. the control f search gets him started with the right section of the resume, then he looks through everything else.

what does aaron harmon say about explaining gaps or contract work on a resume?

on guidance counselor 2.0, aaron harmon recommends answering questions before they have to be asked on your resume. if you did contract work, write the word contract so recruiters don't think you're a job hopper based on tenure alone. if you were laid off due to budget cuts or a reduction in force, write that directly on the resume. he explains that recruiters make mistakes and have lean teams with many candidates, so owning your story upfront helps you avoid being misunderstood or overlooked.
