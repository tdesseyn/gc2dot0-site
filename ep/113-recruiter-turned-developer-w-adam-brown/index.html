ep 113 · feb 14, 2022

recruiter turned developer w/adam brown

guest: adam brown, software engineer at twitch, former recruiter

  • focus on one technology first instead of jumping between languages so you actually build depth
  • pick your first technology based on what naturally extends into high demand skills like react after javascript
  • ask multiple recruiters for market insight since they talk to candidates, hiring managers and each other all day
  • pairing a brand new hire with someone just a year into the role can speed up ramp up for both people
  • frame questions you feel you should already know the answer to with self aware humor so you come across confident, not clueless

questions this episode answers

what does adam brown say about which technology to focus on when starting out?

on guidance counselor 2.0, adam brown says to focus on one technology when you're getting into development instead of jumping between languages. he saw people learn javascript at a bootcamp then immediately start learning python, trying to learn everything shiny, but spreading yourself thin means you master nothing. pick one technology, get the fundamentals down, then venture into closely related extensions like learning react after javascript.

how does adam brown suggest finding out which programming languages are in demand?

on guidance counselor 2.0, adam brown and taylor desseyn agree that you should ask recruiters about market demand for technologies. adam knew javascript would let him jump into react because the market was demanding it, information the average person doesn't have. taylor adds that you don't even have to use recruiters to apply, just ask them like ask jeeves about what's hot, then ask another recruiter and put the information together.

what does adam brown recommend about pairing junior developers with mentors?

on guidance counselor 2.0, adam brown says pairing a brand new bootcamp graduate with someone who has been in the role for just a year can create magical results. the person one year in knows enough to help onboard someone new but still has perspective on the fundamentals, and mentoring reinforces those basics for them. adam says his own mentoring is selfish because it levels him up and keeps him humble while helping the new hire ramp up fast.
