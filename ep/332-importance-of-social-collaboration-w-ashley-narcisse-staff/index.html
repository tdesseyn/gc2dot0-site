ep 332 · feb 14, 2024

importance of social collaboration w/ashley narcisse, staff swe @ apollo

guest: ashley narcisse, staff software engineer at apollo

  • hackathons are a fast way to build real skills because the time pressure forces you to learn and apply new tools quickly
  • the most successful hackathon teams mix skill sets like design, business, and engineering instead of only technical people
  • going to the same hackathons and conferences repeatedly builds a network effect that pays off later in referrals and job leads
  • many companies hire based on relationships and referrals, so being visible in community spaces lowers the barrier to getting in
  • wearing or doing something distinctive at events gives people an easy reason to start a conversation with you

questions this episode answers

what team composition does ashley narcisse say works best at hackathons?

on guidance counselor 2.0, ashley narcisse says the most successful hackathon teams he saw were the most diverse ones, mixing an art student, a design student, a business-minded person, a solid engineer, and a ui engineer. this composition mirrors what you see in real workplaces and is something you learn by your third hackathon. having a network to build this kind of cross-functional team before the event starts gives you a major advantage.

how does ashley narcisse say going to the same events repeatedly helps your career?

on guidance counselor 2.0, ashley narcisse explains that when you attend hackathons or conferences repeatedly, you start meeting the same people over and over, creating a massive network effect. he attended over 65 hackathons in college and saw this pattern clearly. years later, when he mentioned wanting to apply to major tech companies, friends he had made at those events offered to put in recommendations and skip the early application hurdles, connecting him directly to hiring managers and directors.
