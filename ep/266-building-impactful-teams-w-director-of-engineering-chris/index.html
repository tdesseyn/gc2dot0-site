building impactful teams w/director of engineering, chris holland

guest: chris holland, director of engineering

  • ask better questions and truly listen to your team before assuming you know their challenges
  • adding scrum or agile ceremonies wont fix delivery problems if the root engineering practices are broken
  • focus on patterns, clean code, and test driven development to reduce technical debt and move faster long term
  • test driven development can speed up delivery by cutting out manual steps used to verify code works
  • moving into leadership means your success depends on your team's success, not just your own technical skill

questions this episode answers

what does chris holland say about why scrum or agile ceremonies fail to fix delivery problems?

on guidance counselor 2.0, chris holland says that throwing scrum at a team already struggling to deliver software only adds arbitrary deadlines without solving the real problem. the root issue is that 99 percent of delivery problems come from poor engineering practices like lack of patterns, clean code, and test driven development. scrum only works if you fix the underlying engineering fundamentals first.

what does chris holland say is the biggest mental shift when moving from engineer to leader?

on guidance counselor 2.0, chris holland says the biggest shift is that your personal engineering skill becomes almost irrelevant because you are now held accountable for your team's success, not your own. as an individual contributor you control your own performance, but as a leader it does not matter how good you are if your team is not delivering. you must have a drive to put other people in a position to succeed and solve people problems, not just technical ones.
