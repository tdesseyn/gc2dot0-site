how to contribute to open source the right way w/ bekah weigel

guest: bekah weigel, developer experience lead at opensauce

  • start contributing to open source by checking the project's github and looking for a contributing.md file before asking questions
  • read the docs and readme first, since answering questions you could have solved yourself burns out maintainers
  • ask questions in the open issue or pr instead of dming maintainers directly, since transparency is the norm in open source
  • pick open source issues that target skills missing from your resume, like documentation or a specific framework, to grow and get noticed
  • leadership, clear communication, and mentoring others make people stand out and get recommended faster when job searching

questions this episode answers

what does bekah weigel say about asking maintainers questions in open source?

on guidance counselor 2.0, bekah weigel says contributors should read the readme and contributing.md file before asking questions, because every question pulls a maintainer's focus away from their work. she emphasizes asking questions publicly in the issue or pull request rather than sending direct messages, since transparency is the nature of open source. contributors should try to solve problems themselves first and only ask after genuine effort, not the moment they get stuck.

how should job seekers choose open source issues according to bekah weigel?

on guidance counselor 2.0, bekah weigel recommends identifying the skills missing from your resume and then picking open source issues that target those gaps. if you need more experience with a specific framework like tailwind, look for issues using that technology. if communication is a weakness, pick issues requiring lots of communication or documentation work. this approach lets you develop missing skills while simultaneously building visibility and growing your network through contributions.

what does bekah weigel notice about people who get jobs faster after layoffs?

on guidance counselor 2.0, bekah weigel says the people she knows who find jobs much more quickly after layoffs are those who exemplify leadership skills, communicate well, and mentor others. she believes anyone at any career stage can be a mentor by helping someone understand a problem. these leadership qualities make people in her network more likely to recommend someone for a job, even if the recommendation does not lead to a direct hire.
