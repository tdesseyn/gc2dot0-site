ep 257 · aug 9, 2023

tips and tricks of the job search from a meta engineer w/andronica klaas

guest: andronica klaas, lead quality assurance engineer at meta

  • you don't have to be a developer to work in tech: andronica moved from writing code into quality assurance because it fits her strength for finding what's wrong and thinking up edge cases
  • internships teach you a company's culture and the skills a role really needs before you commit, and interns give companies a fresh view from the next generation of users
  • know your intention before you network (a sponsor, a mentor or just being known), look people up ahead of time, and think about what you can offer them
  • if networking makes you nervous, keep a list of go-to questions, listen with curiosity, and ask people afterward what you could have done better
  • keep a relationship with at least one recruiter even when you're happy, and tailor your resume to the skills in the job description, translating what you've done into those skills

questions this episode answers

why did andronica klaas move from software development into quality assurance?

on guidance counselor 2.0, andronica klaas, a lead quality assurance engineer at meta, says she interned as a developer in college but did not love writing code all day. after about six months in software development at bank of america, she asked her manager what other opportunities there were and landed in quality assurance. she says qa fits her because she is good at finding what is wrong and coming up with edge cases, while still working with the code and the product.

how does andronica klaas approach networking when it makes her nervous?

andronica klaas says she starts by knowing her intention, whether she wants a sponsor, a mentor or just to be known in a community, and she thinks about what she can offer the other person. before a conference she looks up the speakers so her conversations show real interest. because networking intimidates her, she keeps a list of go-to questions, listens with curiosity, and sometimes asks the person afterward what she could have done better.

how does andronica klaas use recruiters and job descriptions to prepare for her next role?

even when she is happy in her job, andronica klaas keeps relationships with recruiters on linkedin so she knows her market value and has someone to reach out to when the day comes. for her resume, she reads the description of the job she wants, notes the skills it asks for, and works on closing the gap. she also recommends translating your experience into skills, for example running a student organization shows project management skills.
