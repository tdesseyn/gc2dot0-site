how to use linkedin to find a job (solo show)

with taylor desseyn (solo)

  • make your linkedin title specific like "senior .net developer" since recruiters search and filter by exact job titles
  • search linkedin jobs filtered to the past 24 hours daily to catch new postings before the pile of applicants grows
  • before messaging a hiring manager, check your connections and school alumni at that company for a natural way to reach out
  • personalize every connection request with a short human note naming the role and your top matching skills instead of a generic pitch
  • like and comment on posts consistently so your name becomes recognizable to people who might later hire or refer you

questions this episode answers

what does taylor say about the linkedin profile title section for job seekers?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says your linkedin title portion is incredibly underrated because recruiters search by exact job titles and filter results by keywords. if you are a senior .net developer, put exactly that in your title rather than something vague, because recruiters do not have time to click through 538 profiles and will only look at titles that match their search. he demonstrates how he searches for senior .net developer in nashville and immediately filters to profiles with those exact words in the title.

how does taylor recommend filtering linkedin job searches?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn recommends always filtering linkedin job searches to the past 24 hours and running that search every single day. he explains that with the current market, if you are not immediately going to the newest job postings, you will not have a chance because you need to catch them before the pile of applicants grows. he emphasizes that this daily 24 hour filter gives you the most narrow results and the best shot at standing out.

what does taylor say about messaging hiring managers on linkedin?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says to personalize every connection request with a note instead of sending a generic message, because people have dm fatigue from spam. he recommends humanizing your message by saying something like hey i know this is random, then stating you are looking for a job, showing you have passion for their space with a specific example like personal projects, and mentioning your top three matching skills. he insists you must be intentional with every single message and cannot copy and paste, because hiring managers do not have time to go back and forth.
