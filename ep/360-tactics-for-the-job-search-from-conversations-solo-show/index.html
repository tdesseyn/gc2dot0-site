tactics for the job search from conversations - solo show

with taylor desseyn (solo)

  • stop mass applying and instead dm hiring managers directly with why you are a good fit for the specific role
  • build a short mission statement that explains what you bring to the table so you can answer what do you do clearly
  • give yourself time to breathe after a layoff since its a traumatic event and deciding things on pure emotion rarely goes well
  • use a monthly bcc email to your favorite recruiters reminding them who you are and what you are looking for
  • when a recruiter cant place you still ask for a resume tip, how you stack up on skills and salary, and other recruiter contacts

questions this episode answers

what does taylor desseyn say about mass applying to jobs versus direct messaging?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn shares advice from a senior engineering manager who stopped mass applying and instead started messaging hiring managers directly on linkedin. the manager explained exactly why he was a good fit for each specific role in the message. his response rate jumped from zero percent to around 50 percent just by switching to this targeted approach with clear, specific information about his value.

how does taylor desseyn suggest following up with recruiters during a job search?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn recommends the monthly bcc email trick for staying in touch with recruiters. every month, put all the recruiters you like on one bcc email and send a brief reminder of who you are, your key skills, what type of role you want, and your salary minimum. taylor says this consistent monthly check-in helps you stay top of mind since recruiters and hiring managers are overwhelmed and need reminders.

what questions should you ask a recruiter who can't place you, according to taylor desseyn?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn lists five questions to ask any recruiter even if they cannot help you find a job right now. ask for one tip to improve your resume, one tip to improve your linkedin, where you stack up on skills compared to other candidates, where your salary expectations sit in the current market, and whether they have other recruiter friends you should talk to. taylor says most agency recruiters can only help 10 percent of people they talk to, but you can still crowdsource valuable advice from each conversation.
