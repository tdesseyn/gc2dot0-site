building tech culture from the ground up w/ brian hogan

guest: brian hogan, director of education at temporal

  • push for sponsorship, not just mentorship, by actively opening doors and advocating for junior developers in your network
  • on resumes, show accomplishments with quantifiable results instead of listing responsibilities to stand out from hundreds of applicants
  • good leaders build psychological safety and trust over time, modeling behaviors like taking time off so their team feels safe doing the same
  • research company culture through back channel conversations with current or former employees rather than relying only on interview answers
  • focus on people skills alongside technical skills since much of software work involves communication, feedback, and pitching ideas

questions this episode answers

how should a resume stand out when hundreds of people apply, according to brian hogan?

on guidance counselor 2.0, brian hogan says to show accomplishments with quantifiable results instead of listing responsibilities. hiring managers have only 60 seconds or less to scan your resume, so tell them what you achieved, like growing revenue by a specific amount or reducing load times by a percentage. your resume should show what you can bring to their company, not just what you did at your last job.

what does brian hogan say good tech leaders must do to build trust and culture?

on guidance counselor 2.0, brian hogan says good leaders create psychological safety and build trust over time by being people first. it takes months of consistent one on one meetings and defending your team to earn their trust. he emphasizes leading by example, like taking a mental health day or a week of vacation so your team feels safe doing the same, because people look at how their boss behaves to learn the culture.
