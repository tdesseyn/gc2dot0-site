the importance of trust w/ ben kettle, vp of revenue at gun.io

guest: ben kettle, vp of revenue at gun.io

  • strong vetting builds trust, which shows up as rising client satisfaction scores and clients returning without reposting jobs
  • companies often stick with the same hiring sources out of habit, so looking beyond the usual job boards can surface senior talent who are not actively job hunting
  • years of experience matters less than being able to talk clearly and confidently about what you have actually built
  • when interviewing, aim for a peer to peer conversation instead of a formal interrogation, the best interviews feel like two experts talking shop
  • early career professionals should get good at articulating business problems and reasoning through tradeoffs, not just citing technical skills

questions this episode answers

what does ben kettle say makes gun.io different from typical recruiting agencies?

on guidance counselor 2.0, ben kettle, vp of revenue at gun.io, says gun.io has a technical team (dev rel) that vets engineers for the specific skills clients need, rather than having recruiters pretend to be technical experts. after gathering business requirements, the sales team passes them to engineers who actually understand the technology stack and can find the right people. clients then receive only candidates who have been technically vetted by real engineers, not just screened by recruiters reading resumes.

how does ben kettle recommend junior engineers approach job interviews?

on guidance counselor 2.0, ben kettle says the best interviews feel like two peers meeting to talk shop, not a formal interrogation with a power dynamic. he encourages younger people to stop being super formal and tense, get to the real substance faster, and be natural and curious. he says people should be mature and know their material, but they do not need to act robotic or overly formal to make a good impression.

what does ben kettle say matters more than years of experience when hiring engineers?

on guidance counselor 2.0, ben kettle says he does not have much faith in years of experience as a metric. he says if a candidate can build and talk intelligently about things they have actually built, they will probably have a place at gun.io regardless of tenure. the ability to articulate your work clearly and confidently matters more than time spent in roles.
