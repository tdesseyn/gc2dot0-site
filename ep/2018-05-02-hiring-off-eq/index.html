hiring off eq

guest: brad miller and andrew may, brad miller, director of operations and product ownership, and andrew may, ceo and lead architect, code tank labs

  • for junior hires, watch how they react when stuck, saying i need help is a better sign than silently struggling for days
  • build in a tough or unsolvable problem during interviews to see how candidates handle failure, not just success
  • for senior and architect roles, let recruiters vet the technical skills first, then focus the in person interview on leadership
  • ask about hobbies and life outside work, leading a kids sports team or community group shows leadership that is hard to fake
  • if you are an introvert who codes alone, join a team or attend meetups regularly to build the people skills hiring managers look for

questions this episode answers

what does andrew may say about hiring junior developers at code tank labs?

on guidance counselor 2.0, andrew may says code tank labs gives candidates three coding tests, including one that is deliberately unsolvable to see how they handle failure. he wants junior developers to speak up and ask for help when stuck rather than silently struggling for days. the goal is to see if candidates will raise their hand and admit they need assistance, which shows better character than riding a dead horse for a week.

how does code tank labs interview senior developers differently from juniors?

on guidance counselor 2.0, brad miller and andrew may explain that for senior and architect roles, they let recruiters vet the technical skills first. then the in-person interview focuses entirely on personality and leadership ability rather than coding tests. they ask about what candidates do outside work, like coaching kids sports teams or leading community groups, because those activities show leadership qualities that are harder to fake than a nine to five job.

what does brad miller recommend for introverted developers who want to improve their people skills?

on guidance counselor 2.0, brad miller says introverted developers should first change their environment by joining a team instead of working remotely alone. second, they should attend meetups at least once a month to socialize with other people in their field. being around peers who do the same work breaks down the intimidation factor and helps build the emotional intelligence that hiring managers look for.
