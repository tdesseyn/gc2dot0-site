which ai assistant should you actually trust? w/ david pawlan

what david pawlan covers in this episode

david pawlan runs growth at merit systems, which he describes as an agentic commerce lab, and he built assistant benchmark, a head to head test of personal ai assistants. he says he's fully tested more than 30 of them. he started with poke, three days after it launched in september 2025, and got hooked watching a text-based agent negotiate prices from his inbox. this episode is a practical guide to what a personal ai agent actually is, which ones to try, and what it all means for people looking for work.

an executive assistant that lives in your texts

david's simplest framing: a personal ai agent is an executive assistant that happens to live in your text messages. it sees a meeting request in your email, checks your calendar and offers times. you ask it to book a flight to san francisco, find a hotel and grab a dinner reservation for a client. it learns more about you the more you use it. is it perfect today? he says absolutely not, but the industry is only months old.

the first layer of value is the small admin work everyone says only takes five seconds. it adds up, he calls it death by a thousand cuts, and once an agent takes it over, going back feels painful. the next layer is household stuff like reordering groceries and coordinating schedules. his mom, who he calls a technophobe, uses one to track his flights home to chicago so she can surprise him at the airport.

just pick one

when taylor asks about juggling several assistants, david's answer is direct: just pick one. managing six different agents will burn you out, and nothing is perfect yet. the focus right now should be learning the habits of letting one agent become a net asset in your life. he compares the moment to the first e-commerce sales in 1994. saying agents are bad today is like saying online shopping was bad that december.

text-based or app-based

david splits assistants into text-based and app-based. muse performed best across his benchmark, but he still uses instinct daily because he prefers the text message interface, that's where he already lives. for some jobs he'd use a specialist. for travel he points to miso and soar, and says soar found him a hotel for a week in san francisco that was $5,000 cheaper than booking online, because it had access to a deal that wasn't public.

what this means for the job search

asked about the market, david says the number one thing he sees is that he has yet to meet someone who got a job purely by applying, or by having an agent apply for them. when agents can send applications, applications stop meaning much. networking and in-person relationships matter more than ever, so show up, meet people and show your value face to face. from his growth lens, he adds that your brand matters too: can you bring people together, and get in touch with the right person?

his advice to his younger self: stay obsessed with what you're doing and let the results compound. don't be impatient, don't try to win in one day, and don't chase what's trendy. follow what you're genuinely curious about.

questions this episode answers

what is a personal ai assistant, according to david pawlan?

on guidance counselor 2.0, david pawlan, who runs growth at merit systems and built assistant benchmark, says to think of a personal ai agent like hiring an executive assistant that happens to live in your text messages. it can see a meeting request in your email, check your calendar and offer times, book a flight and hotel for a trip, grab a dinner reservation, or research what to do in a city. it learns about you the more you use it. he says it isn't perfect today, but the industry is only months old and the progress has been remarkable.

should you use one ai assistant or several?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 episode, david pawlan, who has fully tested about 32 to 34 assistants, says to just pick one. managing six different agents will burn you out, nothing is perfect yet, and your focus today should be learning the habits of letting an agent become a net asset in your life. the exception is specialized tasks: for travel he'd use miso or soar, and soar found him a hotel for a week in san francisco that was $5,000 cheaper than booking online. he still uses instinct most day to day because he prefers the imessage interface, even though muse performed best on his benchmark.

what does david pawlan see in the job market right now?

on guidance counselor 2.0, david pawlan says he has yet to meet someone who got a job purely by applying or by having an agent apply for them. networking and in-person relationships matter more than ever, because applications no longer mean much when agents can send them. he sees hiring centered on engineers and growth people, and says employers want intellectual curiosity, taste and real opinions, since ai is creative but not innovative. his career advice: stay obsessed with what you're doing and let the fruits of that obsession compound over time.
