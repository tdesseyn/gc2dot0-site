ep 305 · dec 11, 2023

the power of community w/head of devrel, domitrius clerk

guest: domitrius clerk, head of developer relations at clerk

  • stop building your community in slack since it is a business communication tool, not a community tool
  • message history and real discussion threads are what make a community valuable, so pick tools that keep that searchable and indexable
  • growth numbers mean nothing without engagement, so aim for a smaller group that actually talks to each other
  • build trust by giving people honest answers even when it means pointing them to a different product or solution
  • when starting out, prove your value to a community first through things like meetups or workshops before expecting anything back

questions this episode answers

what does domitrius clerk say about building communities in slack?

on guidance counselor 2.0, domitrius clerk says to stop building communities in slack because it is a business communication tool, not a community tool. message history in slack requires paying five to ten dollars per user, and that history is where the real value of a community lives. the company owner also has access to all messages in slack, which creates privacy concerns for community members.

how does domitrius clerk say to measure community success?

on guidance counselor 2.0, domitrius clerk says engagement matters far more than growth numbers. he would rather have fifty people with fifty people talking than three thousand people with only fifty engaged members. he uses orbit to track engagement across different channels because it shows how people actually interact, not just vanity metrics like member counts.

what does domitrius clerk recommend when your product is not right for someone?

on guidance counselor 2.0, domitrius clerk says to tell people honestly when your product is not the right fit and help them find a better solution. he learned this from watching someone at aws who would recommend other products when aws was not right for a specific scenario. this builds trust because people remember you gave them reality instead of trying to sell them something wrong, and they will come back to you later.
