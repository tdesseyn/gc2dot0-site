ep 110 · feb 7, 2022

w/barret blake, practice area manager

guest: barret blake, practice area manager

  • networking in college or past jobs often leads directly to future job offers, so build those relationships early
  • long drawn out interview processes with many rounds can signal poor company culture at a prospective employer
  • hiring managers often value communication skills and culture fit as much as or more than raw technical skills
  • keep resumes to a single page with a short bullet list of top skills and brief paragraphs per job
  • practice talking about your accomplishments with family so you can confidently brag about yourself in interviews

questions this episode answers

what does barret blake say about long interview processes with many rounds?

on guidance counselor 2.0, barret blake says that if it takes more than an hour and a half to two hours of interviews to figure out if someone is a fit, that is an indication of really bad company culture. he believes that many interview rounds show the company does not really know what they are doing. he warns that if they do not care about you during the interview, they will not care about you after they hire you.

what does barret blake prioritize when evaluating candidates during interviews?

on guidance counselor 2.0, barret blake says communication skills and culture fit are more important than specific technical skills because skills can be learned. he looks for how candidates interact, present themselves, and whether they show willingness and ability to learn what they do not know. he emphasizes that being able to fit with the team is far more important than the exact skill set at the time of the interview.

what does barret blake recommend for resume length and format?

on guidance counselor 2.0, barret blake says he prefers a single page resume and generally does not look beyond the first page. he recommends putting a short bullet list of your top five to ten skills at the very top, then no more than a paragraph describing what you did for each job. he warns that if you take five or six paragraphs to outline a job, he will not read it because he only has thirty to sixty seconds to scan each resume.
