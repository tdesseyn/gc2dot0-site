ep 181 · nov 2, 2022

advice to hiring managers w/ stephen white

guest: stephen white, avp of software engineering at healthtrust performance group

  • focus on missionaries over mercenaries by helping people understand the problem, not just handing them a ticket
  • build culture around shared values and how people actually work day to day, not ping pong tables or free beer
  • let teams decide how they want to work and give them only the tools they need, then get out of the way
  • as a new manager put your organization and team ahead of your own career ambitions and be patient about moving up
  • as a candidate turn interview questions back on the employer and ask how they live out their values day to day

questions this episode answers

what does stephen white say about missionaries versus mercenaries when hiring?

on guidance counselor 2.0, stephen white says to focus on missionaries over mercenaries by helping people understand the problem they're solving, not just handing them a ticket. a missionary wants to know the customer and the problem to make the experience better, while a mercenary just takes the top ticket and does the work. giving engineers meaningful impact on the business future is a huge way to retain and attract talent.

what does stephen white recommend for aspiring engineering managers?

on guidance counselor 2.0, stephen white tells aspiring managers to have patience and not rush the transition, because he was impatient early on and barely succeeded. he says to understand that your priority order is the organization first, then your team, then yourself last. white also learned that he became much more successful when he stopped focusing on his own promotions and instead focused on making his team more successful.

what interview questions does stephen white say candidates should ask hiring managers?

on guidance counselor 2.0, stephen white says candidates should turn behavioral questions back on employers and ask things like tell me about a time when you empowered your team to be autonomous or tell me about a time when you celebrated failure. he recommends asking what the company's core values are and, more importantly, how those values show up in daily conversations and work. white also suggests asking how the organization focuses on outcomes instead of outputs like lines of code.
