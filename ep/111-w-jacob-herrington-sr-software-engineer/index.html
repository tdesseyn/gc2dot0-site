ep 111 · feb 9, 2022

w/jacob herrington, sr. software engineer

guest: jacob herrington, sr. software engineer

  • rank companies into tiers like a, b, c, and absolutely not to stay organized during a busy search
  • ask companies directly how they ensure pay bands are fair so people doing the same work aren't paid unequally
  • be wary of recruiters who ask for a long call right away since that can signal a long drawn out process
  • put yourself out there on social media and content creation to become discoverable instead of relying only on applications
  • don't be intimidated by job postings since most requirements are a wish list and no candidate checks every box

questions this episode answers

what does jacob herrington recommend for staying organized during a busy job search?

on guidance counselor 2.0, jacob herrington says he ranked companies into tiers using a whiteboard after a few weeks of chaos. he created an a, b, c, and absolutely not list, then continually moved the bottom three companies from each tier down as he learned more. this let him focus energy on the top five or six companies where he actually wanted to work.

how does jacob herrington suggest asking about pay equity in interviews?

on guidance counselor 2.0, jacob herrington says he directly asked companies in interviews what they had done to make sure pay bands were fair. he wanted to ensure that a woman of color with more experience wouldn't be paid less than him, after seeing that happen at a previous job. some companies glossed over the question or just recited diversity statistics instead of answering how they prevented pay inequality.

what does jacob herrington say about recruiter calls that ask for too much time upfront?

on guidance counselor 2.0, jacob herrington says that when a recruiter asks for an hour or 45 minute call right at the start, before he has even spoken to a hiring manager, it signals the company's process is probably very long. he considers that a waste of time because even if he does well with the recruiter, someone else could veto the decision later. he uses that as a metric to filter out companies with drawn-out processes.
