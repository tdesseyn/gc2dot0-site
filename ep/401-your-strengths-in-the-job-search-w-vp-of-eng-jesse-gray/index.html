leveraging your strengths in the job search w/ vp of eng jesse gray

guest: jesse gray, vp of engineering

  • stop blasting applications everywhere and focus your energy on roles you actually want
  • double down on your strengths instead of only trying to fix your weaknesses
  • in interviews talk about what you personally did on a project, not just what the team did
  • build a small real project, even something silly, to show practical skills beyond bootcamp coursework
  • be easy to work with and stay engaged with your team, since that often matters more than raw output

questions this episode answers

what does jesse gray say about applying to hundreds of jobs?

on guidance counselor 2.0, jesse gray says job seekers should stop blasting hundreds of applications everywhere and instead focus their energy only on roles they actually want. he suggests putting effort into creating opportunities rather than mass applying. gray recommends juniors consider entrepreneurial projects with peers instead of endless applications that go nowhere.

how should engineers talk about team projects in interviews according to jesse gray?

on guidance counselor 2.0, jesse gray says candidates must explain what they personally did on a project, not just what the team accomplished. in interviews he asks for specifics about who was on the project, what role the candidate played, and what they individually delivered. gray and taylor agree that separating personal impact from team results is what moves someone to the top one percent of candidates.

what does jesse gray recommend junior developers build to stand out?

on guidance counselor 2.0, jesse gray says junior developers should build a small real project with practical application, even something silly, to stand out from bootcamp coursework. taylor and gray discuss an example of creating an app to track stray cats in a neighborhood with 50 users. gray says showing a deployed project with actual usage would be super impressive compared to theoretical bootcamp projects.
