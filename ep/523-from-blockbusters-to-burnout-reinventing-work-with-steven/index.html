from blockbusters to burnout - reinventing work with steven puri

guest: steven puri, founder of suka, former studio executive at fox and dreamworks

  • find the best person in your field and get close to them, even if it means starting at the bottom just to watch and learn
  • in interviews, research the company's actual gap and show how you fill it instead of just talking about your background
  • don't trust free signals like waitlists or praise from friends, the only real proof is whether someone will pay

questions this episode answers

what does steven puri say about getting into a field you want to work in?

on guidance counselor 2.0, steven puri says find the best person in the field you want to enter and do whatever it takes to get around them, even if that means taking a low-level job just to watch them work. he took jobs on film sets doing grunt work like recording film output at night so he could shadow great directors like james cameron and david fincher. that exposure to greatness, not the paycheck, is what shaped his career and led to producing the digital effects for independence day which won an academy award.

how does steven puri say you should approach job interviews?

on guidance counselor 2.0, steven puri says the interview is not about you or your background, it's about showing the company you understand their gap and can fill it. he uses the analogy of making chocolate chip cookies: if they have chocolate chips, flour, and butter but are missing salt, you walk in and say you specialize in salt. research what the company actually needs before the interview and position yourself as the missing ingredient, not just someone reciting their resume.

what does steven puri say is the only real validation when starting a business?

on guidance counselor 2.0, steven puri says the only signal that matters when testing a startup idea is whether someone will actually pay for it, not free signals like waitlists or positive feedback from friends and family. he learned this the hard way with his first startup after leaving fox, where they had 60,000 monthly active users but no one was paying. don't be proud of waitlist numbers or praise, he warns, because everyone lies to you until they have to put in a credit card.
