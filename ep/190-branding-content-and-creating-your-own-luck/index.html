branding, content, and creating your own luck

guest: christina aldan, brand strategy consultant and fractional cmo

  • start networking before you need a job, since your personal brand already exists whether you shape it or not
  • pick one or two content formats and stay consistent instead of trying to be everywhere at once
  • if you can't share work details, write about your own journey, struggles, or lessons instead
  • summarizing a talk or article you learned from counts as content and speakers appreciate it
  • at conferences, pick one or two people to connect with and offer to volunteer or live tweet to attend cheaply

what christina aldan covers in this episode

christina aldan is a brand strategy consultant based in las vegas, plus a speaker, trainer and longtime mentor. most of her work is as a fractional cmo, building the chief marketing officer role part-time for small companies and individuals who can't afford a full-time one. online, many people know her by her handle, lucky girly girl, before they know her name. this episode covers three things: personal branding, emotional intelligence, and how to get real value out of tech conferences.

you already have a personal brand

christina's starting point: your personal brand exists whether you shape it or not. if you don't engage, you might be known as the quiet person in the corner nobody can describe. that's still a brand. she encourages people, introverts included, to take control of that story through content and networking, and to start before they need a job.

she and taylor both call themselves introverts. christina likes the term social sprinter: she can be on in front of a big room, then needs days to recover. so she gets in front of as many people at once as she can. for quieter options, she suggests writing a 600 word article on linkedin and replying to comments on your own time, or mentoring, since people you help often become ambassadors for you.

pick one or two things and stay consistent

you don't need to be everywhere. pick one or two formats and share a consistent message about how you work and what it's like to work with you. you also don't need a leadership title to post about leadership. taylor goes further: stay consistent with leadership content and he'd almost promise you'll end up in a leadership role, because you can't do something every day without getting better at it.

when you can't talk about your work

a listener asked how to make content when you can't share much about the tech you work on. christina says to go back to fundamentals, because there are always newbies learning your stack, and experienced people forget how steep that early curve was. write about how you got into your career, what it was like to navigate office politics, or going back to the office. taylor's advice: talk about yourself. what are you struggling with, what are you seeing in the market, what do you want to learn? a summary of a talk or article you learned from counts as content too.

getting the most out of conferences

after a talk, go up to the speaker and just say good talk, even if you're nervous. christina says, as a speaker, she can take it from there, and many speakers will offer some free time to work through a problem. pick one or two people to connect with, rather than trying to meet everyone.

if cost is the barrier, ask about financial assistance or scholarships. conferences also desperately need volunteers and will often waive your ticket if you work registration or a workshop day table. another idea: offer to live tweet talks, since most conferences don't have someone in every room. taylor adds that networking on social media and then meeting in person is a game changer.

her advice to her younger self: maybe focus more, but also own a zigzag path. sometimes you have to make the wrong choice to find the right one, so forgive yourself for it.

questions this episode answers

what does christina aldan say about creating content when you can't share work details?

on guidance counselor 2.0, christina aldan says you can still talk about fundamental topics because there are always newbies learning your stack. you can also write about your own journey navigating office politics, going back to the office, or struggles you faced early in your career. another approach is to summarize talks or articles you learned from, which counts as valuable content and speakers will often retweet it.

what does christina aldan suggest for attending conferences on a budget?

on guidance counselor 2.0, christina aldan says conferences desperately need volunteers and will typically waive your ticket if you volunteer the whole time or even just one day. you can offer to work registration or sit at the workshop day table in exchange for attending the conference days. another option is to offer to live tweet talks, since conferences don't have social media people in every room and would really appreciate that help.
