ep 190 · dec 7, 2022

branding, content, and creating your own luck

guest: christina aldan, brand strategy consultant and fractional cmo

  • start networking before you need a job, since your personal brand already exists whether you shape it or not
  • pick one or two content formats and stay consistent instead of trying to be everywhere at once
  • if you can't share work details, write about your own journey, struggles, or lessons instead
  • summarizing a talk or article you learned from counts as content and speakers appreciate it
  • at conferences, pick one or two people to connect with and offer to volunteer or live tweet to attend cheaply

who christina aldan is

christina aldan is a brand strategy consultant based in las vegas, plus a speaker, trainer and longtime mentor who was working on her first book at the time. most of her work is as a fractional cmo, building the chief marketing officer role part-time for small companies and individuals who can't afford a full-time one. her talks center on emotional intelligence and leadership, and online many people know her by her handle, lucky girly girl, before they know her name. this episode covers three things: personal branding, emotional intelligence, and how to get real value out of tech conferences.

key takeaways

1. you already have a personal brand

christina's starting point: your brand exists whether you shape it or not. if you don't engage, you might be known as the quiet person in the corner nobody can describe. that's still a brand. so is being the team lead who launches on fridays and then rants all week. she encourages people, introverts included, to take control of that story through content and networking, and to start before they need a job. her line: how we do one thing is how we do anything. taylor's version: branding and content are just your reputation.

2. introverts can sprint

both of them call themselves introverts. christina likes the term social sprinter: she can be on in front of 2,000 people, then needs days to recover. so she gets in front of as many people at once as she can. quieter options: write a 600 word article on linkedin and reply to comments on your own time, or mentor, since people you help often become ambassadors for you. mentoring is just sharing experience, like negotiating a raise or switching careers.

3. a solid brand buys you grace

christina shares that the rug was pulled out from under her about four years before this episode, and she went through a period of deep grief while restructuring her business. because her brand was built on consistency, integrity and showing up, clients, vendors and colleagues were far more forgiving while she healed. taylor makes a similar point: people who already know you'll assume you're having a bad day, not that you're a grump.

4. pick one or two things and stay consistent

you don't need to be everywhere. pick one or two formats and share a consistent message about how you work. you don't need a leadership title to post about leadership, either. taylor would almost promise that consistent leadership content gets you a leadership role, because you can't do something every day without getting better at it. if you can only record eight videos, spread them out instead of posting all eight at once. and keep it short: taylor says even his 90 second clips were losing people.

5. when you can't talk about your work

a listener asked how to make content when you can't share much about your tech. christina says go back to fundamentals, since there are always newbies learning your stack: how you got into your career, office politics, going back to the office. taylor says talk about yourself, what you're struggling with and seeing in the market, or summarize someone else's talk. one person's tweet thread summarizing his session with danny thompson got around 900 likes.

6. emotional intelligence wins the tie

emotional intelligence has four parts: awareness of self, awareness of others, management of self and management of others, with 26 facets underneath. her advice is to pick one facet, like communication or integrity, and study it for three months. her go-to book is emotional intelligence 2.0, which includes a quiz pointing you to what to work on. she says hiring managers tell her that between two candidates with the same stack, the one with higher emotional intelligence gets the job.

7. work a conference without burning out

after a talk, just go up and say good talk, even if you're nervous. pick one or two people to stick near, without clinging, and check in a few times. join the conference slack or group chat, and if you camp in a hallway corner, keep the same corner so people find you. on a budget, look local, ask about scholarships, volunteer for a free ticket, or offer to live tweet talks.

what to do this week

  • write down how a coworker would describe working with you today. that is your brand right now.
  • choose one or two places to post and set a schedule you can keep.
  • write a short post on something basic you wish you'd known early in your career.
  • ask someone who knows you well where your blind spots are, and be ready for the answer.
  • find one local or low-cost conference and email the organizers about volunteering.
