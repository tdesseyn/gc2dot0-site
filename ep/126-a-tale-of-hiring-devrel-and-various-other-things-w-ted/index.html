questions this episode answers

what does ted neward say about hiring managers who only look at exact tech stack matches?

on guidance counselor 2.0, ted neward says hiring managers should look at a candidate's depth and transferable skills instead of just checklist requirements. he argues that he would hire a java developer with 15 years of experience for a c-sharp role in a heartbeat because the languages are close enough that the person can migrate quickly. that 15 years of distributed systems thinking and different perspectives is more valuable than an exact tech match.

how does ted neward recommend building a professional network without speaking at conferences?

on guidance counselor 2.0, ted neward says networking does not require speaking at conferences and your brand does not have to be global to be effective. he recommends going to local user groups, volunteering to assist organizers at local code camps or community coding events, and volunteering at local coding boot camps even if you are not from an underrepresented group. he emphasizes that teaching is the best way to learn and all of these activities help you build connections locally.

what does ted neward say about hiring and managing junior developers effectively?

on guidance counselor 2.0, ted neward says hiring juniors is easy because they are begging for a chance, but the hard part is making them productive. he recommends pairing them with a strong team lead from inside the company, creating psychological safety where anyone can ask any question without judgment, and investing time upfront before expecting productivity. at smartsheet he ran a weekly book club for new junior developers and made sure they sat together so they could easily ask each other questions.
