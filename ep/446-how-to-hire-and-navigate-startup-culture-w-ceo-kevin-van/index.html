how to hire and navigate startup culture w/ ceo, kevin van gundy

guest: kevin van gundy, ceo and founder of hypermode

  • startups often hire from their open source communities, so small contributions like fixing a typo or improving a how-to guide can put you on their radar
  • look at integration layers and how-to guides for open source contributions since core project areas usually require deep insider context
  • de-risking a hire matters more for leadership roles since a bad vp can take months to replace and months more to undo the damage
  • show genuine interest in a company by using their product or asking how the team works instead of only focusing on technical questions
  • startups tend to favor people comfortable with ambiguity who can set their own goals and manage up rather than wait for direction

questions this episode answers

what does kevin van gundy say about getting hired at a startup without inside connections?

on guidance counselor 2.0, kevin van gundy says startups often hire from their open source communities, so making contributions to their projects puts you on their radar. he recommends focusing on integration layers and how-to guides rather than core code, since those areas don't require deep insider knowledge. even small contributions like fixing typos or improving documentation can help you stand out above the hundreds of linkedin applicants.

why does kevin van gundy say startups worry about de-risking hires?

on guidance counselor 2.0, kevin van gundy says de-risking matters more for leadership roles because a bad vp can take three to six months to identify, then creates bad structures in the organization that take another six months to undo after you replace them. even for individual contributors at a 10 or 20 person startup, one hire changes 5 percent of the culture, so founders feel anxiety about making sure each person fits.
