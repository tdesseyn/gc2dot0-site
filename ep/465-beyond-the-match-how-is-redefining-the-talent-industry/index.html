beyond the match: how torc is redefining the talent industry

guest: mike morris, ceo and co-founder

  • identify your mentors early and set up regular check-ins with them so the relationship lasts for years
  • build a profile that pulls live data from places like linkedin and github instead of relying on a static resume
  • ai should give recruiters a sidekick for manual work, not replace the human relationship with candidates
  • specializing in a specific skill set makes it easier to get matched to the right opportunities than staying a generalist

questions this episode answers

what does mike morris say about finding mentors for your career?

on guidance counselor 2.0, mike morris says you need to identify mentors early in your career and set up regular check-ins with them that never stop. he mentions meeting with his mentor chris barbin every six weeks for twelve years straight. the hard part is identifying good mentors and then never letting them go, even as you move through different stages of your career.

how does mike morris say tech profiles should work instead of resumes?

on guidance counselor 2.0, mike morris says profiles should pull live data from places you already use instead of requiring manual updates. if you keep your work history on linkedin, the profile should pull from there; if you code on github, it should pull your stats and contributions automatically. he calls these living and breathing profiles that evolve like your social media feeds, not static documents you have to constantly rewrite.

what does mike morris say about ai replacing recruiters in hiring?

on guidance counselor 2.0, mike morris says ai should act as a sidekick to recruiters, not a replacement for them. the ai handles manual processes and data tasks so recruiters can spend more time advising talent on the best job fit and advising clients on the best candidates. he compares it to a doctor who uses data and tools but still sits down with you to explain things and build a relationship you can trust.
