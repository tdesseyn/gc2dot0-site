ep 399 · jan 6, 2025

from sr. ic to sr. leader w/ vp of eng, matt burch

guest: matt burch, vp of engineering

  • be proactive with mentors instead of reactive, meet regularly so you go into new situations warned, not surprised
  • practice concise communication so you are not forcing busy founders or executives to read through a wall of text
  • give feedback early and often even when it feels risky, since avoiding it to protect morale is a bigger risk
  • as a new leader avoid spending more than half your time on ic work, your job is empowering the team not just building
  • do not assume your new boss needs the same things past managers did, ask questions and get expectations explicit early

questions this episode answers

what does matt burch say about finding and using a mentor when moving into leadership?

on guidance counselor 2.0, matt burch says you need to be proactive rather than reactive with a mentor, meeting ideally once a week in your first couple months. the mentor should be a neutral third party outside your job who can warn you about upcoming situations before you hit them. you should tell them what's coming and get their input so you go in warm instead of being surprised and reacting afterward.

how much time should a new engineering leader spend on individual contributor work, according to matt burch?

on guidance counselor 2.0, matt burch says try not to spend more than 50 percent of your time on individual contributor work when you move into leadership. your job success is measured by your team's ability to build product with excellence, not by what you personally code. you have to fight the desire to lean back on building things you know you can do well, because your real job is empowering the team.

what does matt burch mean by keeping communication brief in a startup?

on guidance counselor 2.0, matt burch says you must learn to say a lot with just a few words because founders and executives are either selling or raising money all the time. you cannot force them to read through a wall of text. practice being concise so you can keep them updated and get what you need without being disruptive to their overloaded schedule.
