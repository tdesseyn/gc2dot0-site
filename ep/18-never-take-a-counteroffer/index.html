ep 18 · feb 12, 2021

never take a counteroffer

with taylor desseyn (solo)

  • write down why you are job searching before you talk to your manager so counteroffers don't shake your resolve
  • once your employer knows you are looking, they often plan to replace you at a lower cost later
  • treat a new job offer like it matches your current salary unless money is truly the reason you are leaving
  • research your salary band and talk to peers so you know what to counter with before negotiating
  • one round of asking for more after an offer is fine, but asking repeatedly risks losing the offer

questions this episode answers

why does taylor desseyn say you should never take a counteroffer?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says once your employer knows you are looking for another job, they will likely plan to replace you later at a lower cost than the raise they gave you. he compares accepting a counteroffer to threatening to leave a relationship to force change, which is not a healthy foundation. he advises writing down your reasons for leaving before talking to your manager so you can stick to your decision.

what does taylor desseyn say about evaluating a new job offer compared to your current salary?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says you should approach each job offer as if the salaries are the same unless money is truly the only reason you are leaving. he explains that if culture or management is the problem, more money at your current job will not fix it. he encourages job seekers to be confident about their non-financial reasons for leaving rather than letting a salary increase shake their resolve.

how does taylor desseyn say you should prepare to counter a job offer?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says you need to research your salary band and know where you stand in the market before entering negotiations. he recommends asking friends and peers in your field what they earn so everyone can negotiate fairly. he says going back to ask for more one time is acceptable, but asking two, three, or four times will likely cause you to lose the offer.
