ep 41 · jun 14, 2021

recruiting ama

with taylor desseyn (solo)

  • ask a recruiter for contract vs direct hire, remote status, and salary range upfront instead of waiting for them to volunteer it
  • ask a recruiter if the client is an ats or vms and whether they have direct contact with the hiring manager before investing time
  • keep your resume to two or three pages and load it with the specific tech stack for the job you want
  • use the correct technical terminology on your resume even if the job description has it wrong, since you are the professional
  • to reach someone at a company you want to join, dm an employee directly and ask for a few minutes of their time instead of cold emailing

questions this episode answers

what does taylor desseyn say about why recruiters don't include salary information upfront in messages?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says recruiters withhold salary and other details in initial messages because their metrics are based on unique conversations, not emails sent. they intentionally keep information away to force candidates onto the phone. he recommends having a template message that asks three questions upfront: is it contract or direct hire, what is the remote status, and what is the salary range.

what does taylor desseyn say about whether to match incorrect terminology in a job description on your resume?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says to use the appropriate technical terminology on your resume even if the job description contains incorrect terms. he explains that you are the professional, not the human resources person who wrote the job description, so you need to keep terminology consistent and look like you know what you're talking about. he adds that the job description may simply contain a misspelling.

what does taylor desseyn recommend asking a recruiter about ats and vms clients?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn recommends asking a recruiter whether the client uses an applicant tracking system or vendor management system. he also says to ask if the recruiter has direct contact with the hiring manager, because if they answer no, you should know you are in for an uphill battle with that job opportunity. these systems have different degrees of strictness and can create barriers between you and the actual decision maker.
