what to do if you were laid off today w/ will king, design engineer

guest: will king, design engineer at snowflake

  • if you get laid off, pick a few target companies and build something that shows you can do the job day one instead of mass applying
  • hiring is a trust exercise so your goal is to make yourself the least risky choice a company can make
  • use ai to speed up research and prep, not to generate your resume or project for you since that is easy to spot
  • contributing real fixes to open source tools you actually use heavily can be a stronger signal than a cold application
  • say yes to ambitious side projects while still employed since those unexpected efforts tend to open the most career doors

questions this episode answers

what does will king say you should do if you get laid off instead of mass applying?

on guidance counselor 2.0, will king says if you get laid off, identify three companies max that you want to work for and build something specific for them that proves you can do the job day one. hiring is a trust exercise, so your goal is to make yourself the least risky choice by showing you understand their technology and can solve their problems. this approach is more effective than sending out hundreds of generic applications.

how does will king say you should use ai in your job search?

on guidance counselor 2.0, will king says use ai to speed up research and preparation, not to generate your resume or projects for you. use it to do domain research on companies, understand common problems in their market, and gather information faster, then make your own decisions based on that research. companies can easily spot when someone has let ai do all the thinking, and you need to be able to have a knowledgeable conversation about what you built.

what does will king say about contributing to open source to get a job?

on guidance counselor 2.0, will king says contributing real fixes to open source projects you actually use heavily can be a stronger signal than a cold application. his friend dax at opencode said every single hire came from people who contributed to their open source projects, because the company already knew they could trust the quality of their work. you need to be a heavy user to identify real problems, not just surface level contributions using ai.
