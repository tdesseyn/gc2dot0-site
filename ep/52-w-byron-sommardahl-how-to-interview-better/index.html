ep 52 · jul 9, 2021

w/byron sommardahl & how to interview better

guest: byron sommardahl, cto at acorn avenue and hero unit

  • algorithmic tests mostly show what someone recently studied, not real job skill for most developer roles
  • pair programming through a coding kata with test driven development reveals how someone actually thinks and solves problems
  • being able to teach or explain your code during an interview signals strong communication skills
  • developers need emotional intelligence since poor communication can lead to solving the wrong problem for a client
  • if being yourself hurts you in interviews, work on your emotional intelligence instead of just your technical skills

questions this episode answers

what does byron sommardahl say about algorithmic coding tests in interviews?

on guidance counselor 2.0, byron sommardahl says algorithmic tests mostly show what someone recently studied, like a recent college graduate or bootcamp grad. he argues that 99 percent of developer jobs do not require deep algorithm knowledge because developers simply need to solve problems, not squeeze every bit of efficiency from code. after 30 years of development, he cannot pass those tests because he has never needed those algorithms in actual work.

what interviewing method does byron sommardahl recommend for hiring developers?

on guidance counselor 2.0, byron sommardahl recommends pair programming through a coding kata with test driven development. he has the candidate write unit tests first, then implementation code, which reveals how someone thinks and solves unfamiliar problems. during the kata, byron asks the candidate to teach him, which shows how well they can verbalize their code and communicate technical concepts to non-technical people.

what does byron sommardahl say developers should work on besides technical skills?

on guidance counselor 2.0, byron sommardahl says developers need to focus on emotional intelligence. he explains that the nerdy pocket protector developer cannot exist anymore in the current market. poor communication leads to solving the wrong problem or building something too robust or too brittle because the developer did not communicate enough with the client. he recommends taking an emotional intelligence test to identify areas for improvement.
