ep 182 · nov 4, 2022

importance of soft skills and hiring w/ jm huret

guest: jm huret, head of software engineering for vaco built

  • show genuine interest in life outside work during interviews since it signals comfort connecting as a real person
  • expect interviews to mirror day to day work instead of grilling candidates with algorithms they will never use
  • prepare notes or a written summary of your projects before talking to recruiters so you can speak clearly under pressure
  • when you lack experience in something, explain how you approached a similar problem rather than giving a flat no
  • pay attention early in your career to whether you actually enjoy the work itself, not just the money it can bring

questions this episode answers

what does jm huret say about showing personality in tech interviews?

on guidance counselor 2.0, jm huret says interviewers want to see you as a real person, not just a task deliverer. he asks candidates about hobbies and items visible in their background because those details would naturally come up in an office setting. candidates often avoid talking about their lives outside work, but opening up about instruments, projects or other interests signals you can connect as a human being on the team.

how does jm huret recommend answering when you lack experience in something?

on guidance counselor 2.0, jm huret says do not answer with a flat no when asked if you have done something. instead, explain how you approached a similar problem in the past and how long it took you to get up to speed. he emphasizes that a huge part of engineering is learning new things, so hiring managers expect you to articulate how you would tackle unfamiliar challenges rather than shutting down the conversation.

what does taylor desseyn recommend bringing to an interview to speak clearly about your work?

on guidance counselor 2.0, taylor desseyn recommends writing notes or a summary of your projects before talking to a recruiter so you have talking points under pressure. he suggests keeping your resume, a list of success stories and tasks you have accomplished, and any independent notes in front of you during the call. even experienced professionals do not remember everything they have done, so preparing a written reference helps you respond clearly when questions catch you off guard.
