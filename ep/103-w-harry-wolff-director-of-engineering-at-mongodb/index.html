guest: harry wolff, director of engineering at mongodb

  • skill gets you in the door but timing and a little luck often decide if you land the role

questions this episode answers

what does harry wolff say about timing when applying for jobs?

on guidance counselor 2.0, harry wolff, director of engineering at mongodb, says the timeline of a job posting matters more than candidates realize. when a role just opened, the hiring team often has impossibly high or poorly calibrated expectations because they haven't interviewed enough people yet. applying to older postings can sometimes mean the bar has been adjusted to reality.

why do companies still filter candidates by react experience according to harry wolff?

on guidance counselor 2.0, harry wolff, director of engineering at mongodb, says hiring managers use tech stacks like react as an easy discriminator because evaluating soft skills at scale is hard. with a huge influx of applicants, requiring specific tools is the simplest way to narrow the pool to something a human can review, even if it filters out good candidates.

what should engineers know before moving into management, according to harry wolff?

on guidance counselor 2.0, harry wolff, director of engineering at mongodb, says prepare to solve problems you have no training for. as an individual contributor you learn react or other technical skills, but nobody teaches you how to handle interpersonal conflict on a team. management requires communication and information sharing across the organization, skills completely different from writing code.
