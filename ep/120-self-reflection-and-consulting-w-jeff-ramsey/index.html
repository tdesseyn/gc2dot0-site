ep 120 · mar 2, 2022

self reflection and consulting w/ jeff ramsey

guest: jeff ramsey, leads accounting and finance consulting practice nationally at vaco

  • use consulting projects to test a company's culture before committing to a full time role
  • expect most contract roles to require around 40 hours a week even though pay is often hourly
  • ask any recruiting or consulting firm what your long term career goals are and see if they actually listen
  • use a contract role at a company on the fringes of an industry as a stepping stone into that industry
  • lean on recruiters to learn the market instead of only relying on job boards and easy apply

questions this episode answers

what does jeff ramsey say about using consulting to evaluate a company's culture?

on guidance counselor 2.0, jeff ramsey says consulting is a great way to evaluate a company's culture and the job before committing full-time. you can see day-to-day how they treat people and whether they back up what they say in interviews. consulting lets you try different companies until you find a culture that really fits you, and you can jump off at any point or convert to full-time when you find the right match.

what does jeff ramsey say about using consulting to break into a new industry?

on guidance counselor 2.0, jeff ramsey says consulting is an ideal way to try new industries that are hard to break into directly. if you want to get into healthcare but are in manufacturing, a consulting firm can help you get a contract opportunity at a healthcare services company on the fringes of the industry. this gives you a chance to use your existing skillset while learning the new industry and using it as a stepping stone to where you want to go.

what does jeff ramsey say about working hours and benefits in consulting roles?

on guidance counselor 2.0, jeff ramsey says most consulting and contract roles are 40-hour-a-week jobs, though sometimes more, because clients typically need someone full-time for a period. regarding benefits, consultants are w-2 employees with access to health insurance, 401k, and other benefits, not 1099 contractors left on their own. he emphasizes that people should not assume they will be without benefits or support when consulting.
