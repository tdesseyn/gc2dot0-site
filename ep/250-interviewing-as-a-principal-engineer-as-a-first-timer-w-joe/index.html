ep 250 · may 5, 2023

interviewing as a principal engineer as a first timer w/joe flateau, technical lead/staff engineer at density

guest: joe flateau, technical lead/staff engineer at density

  • when moving to a new job after many years in one place, practice talking about what you personally did, not just what your team did
  • trim resume debt by cutting skills that aren't front and center or relevant to the job you're applying for
  • message a referral contact before you submit your application so they can actually advocate for you with hr
  • keep cover letters short, around four to five sentences, each tied directly to something in the job description
  • treat yourself like a business you run, not just a dependent of your employer, and keep learning and selling your skills

questions this episode answers

what does joe flateau say about talking about your work in interviews after staying at one company for many years?

on guidance counselor 2.0, joe flateau, technical lead at density, says that after 12 years at one company he had to learn to sell his individual accomplishments instead of defaulting to team language. he explains that his natural tendency was to say the team did this or that, but in interviews you have to focus on what you personally did. the interviewer doesn't care what the team accomplished, they want to know your specific contributions.

what does joe flateau recommend about asking for referrals when job searching?

on guidance counselor 2.0, joe flateau, technical lead at density, says you must message your referral contact before you submit your application to the company. once you submit your information to the system, the referrer can no longer carry your resume directly to hr and advocate for you. he explains that referrers are often financially incentivized through referral bonuses, so you are actually doing them a favor by asking, and it only takes them five to ten minutes.
