ep 350 · apr 5, 2024

the benefits of changing companies w/ james q quick, content creator

guest: james q quick, content creator

  • when someone offers to help or says reach out, actually follow up since most people never do
  • invest in your community through meetups, livestreams, and conferences instead of thinking of it as networking
  • research salary ranges, required skills, and experience levels for target roles, then work to fill the gaps
  • staying at one company can cap your salary and growth, so compare offers and be open to moving
  • after a layoff give yourself grace and a day to breathe before starting to research and apply again

what james q quick covers in this episode

james q quick is a developer content creator and speaker who does a lot of panels, podcasts and bootcamp talks, including at vanderbilt, where he went to school. he's been on guidance counselor 2.0 several times, and taylor credits a single dm to james, sent years earlier while feeling intimidated, with opening doors like an introduction that led to a conference keynote. this episode covers following up, investing in your community, when to change companies, and what to do right after a layoff.

when someone says reach out, reach out

james opens with a rant. everyone in his position ends a talk with if you have questions, reach out. some say it to be polite, but he means it. yet only about 2 percent of people actually do. it's an easy missed opportunity. he can't promise to answer every dm, but the people who participate, comment and follow up are the ones he's most excited to help. if you're not reaching out, he says, that's on you, and it's easy to fix. taylor adds that networking is just a lot of small conversations. most go nowhere, and that's fine, because one of them can land you a job.

investing in your community

james doesn't like the word networking. he calls it investing in your community: showing up to linkedin live streams, twitter, discord, local meetups and conferences. and it starts before you need it. you build the network for when you need it, not when you need it.

do your research, then fill the gaps

his biggest piece of advice: research. look at the jobs you're interested in, their salary ranges, the skills they ask for, the years of experience and the kinds of portfolio projects. then work backwards. if you know javascript but every role you want lists typescript, that's your cue to learn typescript. even if you're happy where you are, he says there's almost no reason not to take a call about an interesting opportunity. he's done google's full day of final interviews twice without getting the job, and still found value in doing well enough to be remembered.

the limits of staying put

a listener asked about the pros and cons of moving up within one company. james is clear he's not telling anyone to jump every two years, but there are almost always limits to staying. you may plateau at the top level a company offers, which often caps your salary. or you may feel ready for a promotion that isn't coming. his advice is to ask your manager directly: here's why i think i'm ready, what are the gaps? if you don't get real investment back, that's a sign to look elsewhere. companies also simply pay different amounts.

after a layoff, breathe first

for someone starting a job search on day one after a layoff, the advice from both is to breathe and take the day. james says getting let go in this economy isn't necessarily a reflection on you. companies are making hard decisions every day. give yourself grace, then prioritize: research, find your gaps, and get involved in your community. taylor warns against angry interviewing while you're still mad at your old company.

his advice to his younger self: you're in control. stop spending time on what you could be doing if you aren't ready to put energy into it, and turn your research into a checklist.

questions this episode answers

what does james q quick say about reaching out to people who offer help?

on guidance counselor 2.0, james q quick says that when speakers at panels, podcasts or boot camps tell you to reach out if you have questions, only about 2 percent of people actually do it. he emphasizes that he sincerely means it when he says reach out, and that people who do follow up are the ones who get him most excited to help. this small effort of reaching out after someone offers can create real opportunities, but almost nobody does it.

how does james q quick define networking?

on guidance counselor 2.0, james q quick says he doesn't like the word networking and prefers calling it investing in your community. he explains this means showing up to linkedin live streams, twitter, discord, local meetups in person, and conferences. the key is networking starts before you need it, not when you need a job. you build relationships by just being present and being part of the conversation over time.

what does james q quick recommend doing when you feel stuck in your current job?

on guidance counselor 2.0, james q quick advises doing research on job opportunities that interest you, including salary ranges, required skill sets, years of experience and portfolio projects employers want. then work backwards to identify and fill the gaps in your own skills. he emphasizes this doesn't happen overnight but you need to start investing energy into getting to that next point. he also says there's almost always another job where you'd be just as happy and make more money.
