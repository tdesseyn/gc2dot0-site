recruiter ama cont.

with taylor desseyn (solo)

  • recruiters keep outreach messages vague on purpose so you reply and get pulled into their system toward a metric
  • use a short list of screening questions when a recruiter dms you so you can weed out ones who know nothing about the role
  • ask your recruiter if they have the manager or intake call notes to confirm the role was actually spec'd with the hiring manager
  • if a recruiter is just reading prefixed questions off a script instead of having a conversation, that is a red flag
  • check in with your usual recruiter about local openings even if they have not called you, they may already be working on it

questions this episode answers

why do recruiters keep their initial messages vague according to taylor desseyn?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says recruiters intentionally keep direct messages and emails vague because they want candidates to respond and ask questions. once a candidate replies, the recruiter can get them on the phone, obtain their resume, enter them into the system, and hit specific metrics and goals. taylor admits he used to be scared to share company names, salaries, or contract versus full-time details upfront because he wanted so badly to talk to people on the phone.

what does taylor desseyn recommend asking a recruiter to confirm they know the role?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn recommends asking the recruiter if they have the intake call notes or manager notes from the hiring manager. he says these notes are incredibly important because if the recruiter is doing it the right way, they should have notes directly from the hiring manager rather than just a long human resources generated job description. taylor's team always jumps on a call with the hiring manager to spec out the role and take notes, even if those notes are only two to three sentences.

why do recruiters ask where else you are interviewing according to taylor desseyn?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says recruiters ask where else you are interviewing for two main reasons: to gather market intelligence and to send that lead to their salespeople so they can go get that business. he explains that if a company is hiring and the recruiting firm has a relationship with them but the manager just didn't call them this time, they need to know so they can reach out. taylor clarifies that asking this question does not disqualify candidates and that job seekers should also check in with their favorite recruiter if they see a local job opening the recruiter hasn't called them about yet.
