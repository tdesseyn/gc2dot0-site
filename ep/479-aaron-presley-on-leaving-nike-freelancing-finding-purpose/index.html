aaron presley on leaving nike, freelancing & finding purpose beyond code

guest: aaron presley, independent consultant, localization and globalization expert, former nike localization architect

  • before going independent get your budgeting in order since income will be inconsistent for a while
  • consider starting small and local instead of chasing a unicorn startup idea
  • networking is a muscle that gets easier the more you choose to show up and be social
  • coding skill alone wont be your edge as ai makes writing code easier, so find what else sets you apart
  • try contract to hire arrangements since short interview processes rarely reveal how someone will really work out

questions this episode answers

what does aaron presley say to consider before going independent as a freelancer?

on guidance counselor 2.0, aaron presley says the number one thing is to get your budgeting in order because you need to be prepared to not make much money for a while. he also recommends thinking smaller and locally rather than chasing unicorn startup ideas, noting that even a neighborhood power washing company pays bills. aaron suggests looking for small niches in your local area where technology can help solve problems and generate steady income.

what does aaron presley say about coding skills as a career advantage going forward?

on guidance counselor 2.0, aaron presley says the ability to code is no longer the skill that gives developers an edge because writing code will become easier and easier for everyone. he asks developers to think about what they have left when you remove the ability to write functions and use cloud services, suggesting they focus on whether they care about people, want to find a niche, or enjoy networking. aaron warns that holding onto coding ability as your main edge is not a winning recipe for the future.

what does aaron presley recommend instead of traditional interview processes?

on guidance counselor 2.0, aaron presley recommends using contract-to-hire arrangements where you pay someone for a three-month contract on a real project before deciding whether to bring them on full time. he says this approach lets both sides assess if the fit is working and is the least bad experience compared to traditional interviews. aaron went through this process himself with a scheduling company and appreciated being able to evaluate the opportunity while getting paid, ultimately choosing not to continue so he could pursue his passion for localization.
