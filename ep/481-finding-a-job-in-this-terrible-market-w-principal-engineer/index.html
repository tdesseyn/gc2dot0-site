ep 481 · sep 5, 2025

finding a job in this terrible market w/ principal engineer, jeff blankenburg

guest: jeff blankenburg, principal developer advocate at dynatrace

  • expect a real senior-level job search to take around six months, even with a strong network and reputation
  • skip the apply button and go straight to leadership or peers on linkedin with a short customized note before applying
  • when reaching out, ask for a specific small amount of time like fifteen or thirty minutes so busy people can say yes
  • in interviews ask what success looks like a year from now and get the interviewer talking about themselves to build real connection
  • do not take rejection personally, it is usually business and fit, not a judgment on your worth or skill

questions this episode answers

what does jeff blankenburg say about messaging people on linkedin before applying to a job?

on guidance counselor 2.0, jeff blankenburg says to skip the apply button and instead go straight to the hiring manager or a peer on linkedin with a short customized note before you submit your application. he found that people were surprisingly willing to chat, and many responded with offers to schedule fifteen or thirty minute calls. this approach helped him bypass automated screening systems and got him into seven interview loops at once.

how long should a senior engineer expect a job search to take, according to jeff blankenburg?

on guidance counselor 2.0, jeff blankenburg says his job search took exactly six months from layoff to new job offer, and he considers that typical for a senior-level search in the current market. he had twenty-five years of experience, a strong public reputation, and a solid network, yet still needed half a year. he notes that he had five and a half months of severance which made the timeline more manageable.

what interview question does jeff blankenburg recommend asking to understand what a company really wants?

on guidance counselor 2.0, jeff blankenburg says to ask the interviewer what success looks like a year from now and what would make them glad they hired this person. he also recommends asking what their least favorite part of working at the company is, which always catches people off guard but helps build real connection. he emphasizes getting the interviewer to talk about themselves as much as possible, since people feel an interview went well when they get to share their own experiences.
