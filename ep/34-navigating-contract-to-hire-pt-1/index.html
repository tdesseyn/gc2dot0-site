navigating contract to hire pt. 1

with taylor desseyn (solo)

  • ask recruiters how many people they have placed with this specific manager before taking a contract role
  • ask if the contract position has a history of converting to full time or has that opportunity
  • work with a recruiting firm that has been around for at least two years since turnover creates bad handoffs
  • confirm you are a w2 employee of the recruiting firm, not a 1099 freelancer, when contracting

questions this episode answers

what does taylor desseyn say to ask recruiters before taking a contract job?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says to ask the recruiter how many people they have placed with that specific manager as a contractor. this question gives you confidence about the recruiter's experience and relationship with the client. taylor also recommends asking whether the contract position has converted to full time before or has that opportunity.

according to taylor desseyn, what is the difference between contracting through a recruiting firm and freelancing?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says when you contract through a recruiting firm, you are a w2 employee of that firm, not a 1099 freelancer. the recruiting firm takes out your taxes and the work is typically 40 hours per week. the recruiting firm should offer benefits or provide other options to find them.

why does taylor desseyn say to work with a recruiting firm that has been around for two years?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says the recruiting industry has very high turnover. if a recruiter is new and leaves halfway through your contract, there will be a terrible handoff and you will have no context about what happens next. working with a recruiter who has been around at least two years reduces the risk of them leaving mid-contract.
