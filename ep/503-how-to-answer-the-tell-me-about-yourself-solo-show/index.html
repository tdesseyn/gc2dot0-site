how to answer the 'tell me about yourself' - solo show

with taylor desseyn (solo)

  • keep your tell me about yourself answer to about forty five seconds instead of rambling into your life story
  • end your answer by asking if there is anything else they would like you to expound on so you land the plane instead of trailing off
  • build multiple about me statements tailored to networking, a specific job, or general introductions
  • research the company and interviewer so you can tie in relevant details even if you don't know the exact role
  • practice on video by recording yourself answering the question to catch filler words and nervous habits

questions this episode answers

what does taylor desseyn say about how long a tell me about yourself answer should be?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says to keep your tell me about yourself answer to about forty five seconds instead of giving your entire life story. he explains that the biggest mistake people make is being too long winded and rambling through their whole career history. you should be clear and concise, focusing only on what's relevant to the role you're interviewing for.

how should you practice answering tell me about yourself according to taylor desseyn?

on guidance counselor 2.0, taylor desseyn says you should open up your phone, turn on video and record yourself answering the question over and over. he explains that rewatching yourself will be painful but you'll pick up on ticks and filler words like ums, ands and things like that. he specifically warns against just winging it in an interview without practicing first, unless you naturally have the gift of gab.

what does taylor desseyn recommend about personalizing the tell me about yourself answer?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says you should research who you're interviewing with and pull in a fun fact or shared experience to break the ice. he explains that people bond over shared suffering and similar experiences like college football, kids or hobbies. he also recommends having multiple versions of your about me statement tailored to networking, specific jobs or general introductions rather than one generic answer.
