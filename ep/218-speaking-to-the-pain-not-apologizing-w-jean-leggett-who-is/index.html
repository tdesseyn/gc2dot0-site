questions this episode answers

what does jean leggett mean by speaking to the pain on a resume?

on guidance counselor 2.0, jean leggett says hiring managers have specific pain points listed in job descriptions, and candidates should pull language directly from those postings to show they understand the problems. she calls this the hoo-ah approach: heard, understood, and acknowledged. by mirroring the job description's language, your resume will match what the applicant tracking software is scanning for and show the hiring manager you took time to read what they need.

what are jean leggett's vip pillars for organizing a resume?

on guidance counselor 2.0, jean leggett explains her vip framework stands for validation, integration, and people. instead of a chronological list, you group all your career history under these three pillars: validation is your defining expertise (like engineering leadership), integration is the day-to-day execution (like product strategy), and people is your soft skills and collaboration. this non-chronological structure lets hiring managers quickly see your key strengths without digging through every job title.

what does jean leggett recommend for writing a cover letter?

on guidance counselor 2.0, jean leggett breaks cover letters into four paragraphs: a validation paragraph with your years of experience, an impact paragraph with your most relevant work, a pain point paragraph that picks three to five things from the job posting and addresses them directly, and a personal paragraph about why you like the company. she says the cover letter probably will not win you the job, but it may win you an interview because it shows you took time to personalize your application.
