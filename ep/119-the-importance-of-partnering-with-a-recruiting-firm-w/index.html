ep 119 · feb 28, 2022

the importance of partnering with a recruiting firm w/ jessica kashyap

guest: jessica kashyap, recruiting leader focused on accounting and finance hiring initiatives at vaco

  • partnering exclusively with one recruiting firm creates accountability since there is no one else to point fingers at if hiring goes wrong
  • mixed messaging across multiple agencies confuses candidates and makes it hard to control the narrative in the marketplace
  • candidates have options and will only commit to two or so opportunities that sound most enticing, so slow processes lose talent
  • speed matters, some hiring cycles moved from friday interview to offer within about five business days
  • a good recruiter will tell you honestly when they are not the right fit instead of forcing a square peg into a round hole

what jessica kashyap covers in this episode

jessica kashyap is a recruiting leader at vaco focused on accounting and finance hiring. she's a cpa with a master's in accounting from vanderbilt, started her career at a consulting firm, and had about eight or nine years in recruiting at the time of this episode. she and taylor worked at the same firm but hadn't talked in about a year and a half, and taylor is upfront that the conversation wasn't scripted. the topic is how companies and job seekers can get the most out of working with a recruiter.

one firm, one message

jessica traces her approach back to 2017, when her team ran its first large hiring project for a company. to act as a true extension of a company's hr or talent acquisition team, the messaging had to be the same everywhere, because once a message is out in the market it spreads like wildfire and is hard to contain. when several agencies, linkedin and direct applicants all feed the same role, a hiring manager can't tell what each candidate was told.

her answer is working exclusively with one firm, so all of the responsibility sits in one place. as taylor puts it, too many hands in the cookie jar and nobody eats cookies. jessica says if something goes wrong in her setup, there's no one else to point at. it's her and her team. she counts success as the right people hired and still there a year later.

speed wins talent

candidates have options, and their time is valuable. jessica says most will commit to the two opportunities that sound most enticing and run with those, so a slow process misses the window. her example of how fast it can go: interviews on a friday, a second round the next week, an offer within about five business days.

they also cover the recruiter's side of speed. a firm has to be willing to walk away from companies that drag it along, because recruiters are commission based and will spend their time on the companies that know what they want and decide quickly.

a good recruiter will tell you no

both say the best sign in a recruiting firm is one willing to tell you it's not the right fit, instead of forcing a square peg into a round hole. taylor says if a firm is open to walking away from the conversation, lean in even more. jessica notes the industry has often been seen as used car salespeople, and that's what she wants to push against.

advice for job seekers

jessica's message to job seekers is to treat a good recruiter like a friend and a guide. bad recruiters corner you and push you to take a job just for the placement. if that happens, she says, run the other direction. in her exclusive setup, a bad fit comes back to her anyway when the company asks for a replacement in 90 days. she describes her role as showing you opportunities, not playing god. you decide. find recruiters in your field, use them as a sounding board even when they aren't presenting you for a role, and let them translate the jargon on job postings. relationships like that last, and people often come back when they're hiring or looking again.

questions this episode answers

how fast do hiring processes need to move in 2022 according to jessica kashyap?

on guidance counselor 2.0, jessica kashyap says candidates will only commit to the two most enticing opportunities because their time is valuable, so slow processes lose talent. she has seen successful hires happen in five business days, with companies interviewing on a friday and extending an offer by the following week. candidates have so many options that if a company cannot move quickly within about one week maximum, they will lose strong people to faster-moving employers.

what does jessica kashyap say about recruiters who force bad job fits?

on guidance counselor 2.0, jessica kashyap says a good recruiter will honestly tell you when they are not the right fit for your needs instead of forcing a square peg into a round hole. she advises job seekers to run away from recruiters who corner them and pressure them to take a position, because those recruiters only care about the placement fee. recruiters who are willing to walk away from a bad fit actually demonstrate self-awareness and are worth leaning into more.
