about me!

with taylor desseyn (solo)

  • treat your network like capital, posting consistently on linkedin can unexpectedly reach thousands of people
  • starting in high volume cold calling teaches resilience even if you have no idea what you are selling at first
  • specializing in one niche, like software development recruiting, helps you build real credibility over generalist recruiting
  • speaking at local user groups and code camps is a low pressure way to start building a public reputation
  • remember that behind every job search is a real person who is scared, not just a number to move fast past

questions this episode answers

what does taylor desseyn say about learning to recruit for tech when you have no experience?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says he started by doing high volume cold calling for fortune 100 clients with no idea what he was talking about. he would call developers and ask questions to learn, eventually realizing he liked tech after getting hung up on repeatedly. after two years of calling into companies like yahoo and sony, he moved to specialize only in software development recruiting.

how did taylor desseyn start speaking at tech conferences?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says he got involved with the dot net user group in raleigh and the members invited him to speak at their code camp about recruiting, not technical topics. his first talk was scheduled for an hour and a half before lunch but went three hours because nobody left, which launched his speaking career. he recommends user groups and code camps as a low pressure way to build a public reputation.

why does taylor desseyn create content to help job seekers at scale?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says his dad called him after quitting his restaurant manager job of twenty years and asked how to find a job. that call made taylor realize recruiters can become numb to the fact that people are scared, not just numbers, and he needed to help more people. he says the job search process is terrifying and his goal is to reach people in career transition, whether in tech or not, by sharing information at scale through livestreams and newsletters.
