guest: jerome hardaway, sr. engineer at microsoft and founder of vets who code

  • push for remote first if you want to attract top talent, since people are not moving cities just for a job
  • ask companies to back up in office mandates with real data instead of vague feelings about collaboration
  • build community instead of culture on remote teams by keeping conversations public and minimizing private channels
  • add value before you ask for anything, whether raising money, networking, or trying to get hired

questions this episode answers

what does jerome hardaway say about companies requiring people to work on-site?

on guidance counselor 2.0, jerome hardaway, senior engineer at microsoft and founder of vets who code, says companies pushing on-site work have no data to support that it drives better collaboration or innovation. he argues companies are stuck using feelings instead of facts, often because they have tax deals or building leases that require physical presence. he recommends job seekers ask companies to show the data behind in-office mandates, since those companies use data for every other business decision.

how does jerome hardaway recommend building community instead of culture on remote teams?

on guidance counselor 2.0, jerome hardaway says leaders should minimize private channels and keep as many conversations as possible in public team channels to eliminate cliques. he explains you have to automate social processes by making it easy for people to request one-on-ones and by initiating engagement yourself. hardaway says culture leads to pattern matching for comfort, while community allows people from different backgrounds to bring their whole selves to work.

what does jerome hardaway recommend junior developers build instead of a portfolio?

on guidance counselor 2.0, jerome hardaway says job seekers should build real products that people will actually use instead of building just a portfolio site. he tells students to demonstrate collaboration by showing how they worked with a frontend or backend developer, or to build accessibility audits with actual fixes implemented. hardaway argues that showcasing a real thing you built will get you hired faster than a list of generic projects, because it proves you can deliver value and work with others.
