ep 88 · oct 8, 2021

giving notice

with taylor desseyn (solo)

  • approach giving notice with empathy, thank your employer, then stop talking instead of over explaining
  • be very wary of a counteroffer since it rarely fixes the real reason you want to leave
  • give more notice the higher up or more technical your role is, with four weeks as a common cap
  • offer to document your work or stay on contract part time to help smooth the transition
  • know and be ready to explain your real reasons for leaving, even if you just got a raise or promotion

questions this episode answers

what does taylor desseyn say about how to give notice at work?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says to approach giving notice with empathy by thanking your employer for the opportunity and then stopping instead of running your mouth when you get nervous. he recommends sitting down and saying you've enjoyed your time but you're moving on to another opportunity, then shutting your mouth and seeing what happens. the key is to start with grace and gratitude, then be brief.

why does taylor desseyn say not to accept a counteroffer when you quit?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says a counteroffer is like a bad boyfriend or girlfriend promising they'll get better if you stay, but they won't. he gives the example of someone who was told for years there was no budget for raises, but as soon as they gave notice they got a fifteen to twenty thousand dollar raise, which shows the company was just saving money instead of taking care of them. he warns that the counteroffer rarely fixes the real reason you wanted to leave.

how much notice should you give depending on your level, according to taylor desseyn?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says the higher up you are in an organization or the more technical your role, the more notice you should give. for individual contributors two weeks is fine, but for lead or principal positions he recommends four weeks. he suggests asking your employer what would be ideal for them but says thirty days is the cap and employers don't need more than four weeks. he also recommends offering to stay on contract part time to help with the transition.
