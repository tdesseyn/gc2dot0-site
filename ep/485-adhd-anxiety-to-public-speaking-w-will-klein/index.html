adhd & anxiety to public speaking w/ will klein

guest: will klein, developer experience team at north

  • start small by volunteering to speak at a local meetup instead of waiting until you feel ready
  • if there is no meetup in your area start one with just a handful of people at a coffee shop or library
  • rehearse your opening few minutes heavily since the first moments of a talk are the hardest part to get through
  • use physical movement like pushups or a short walk to work through pre-talk adrenaline and nervous energy
  • apply for roles that feel out of reach since getting rejected still teaches you what you need to learn next

questions this episode answers

what does will klein recommend for someone terrified to speak at meetups?

on guidance counselor 2.0, will klein from north says to start by volunteering to speak at a local meetup in your city and talk to the organizer about what topics they need. organizers are always looking for speakers, especially new ones, and they will help validate something you are learning right now as a good topic. if there are no meetups in your area, start one with just a handful of people at a coffee shop or library.

how does will klein deal with nervousness before speaking at conferences?

on guidance counselor 2.0, will klein from north says the first two to three minutes of a talk are always the hardest, so he rehearses the opening slides heavily to get off to a good start. he does pushups or physical activity right before speaking to work through the adrenaline and get his blood flowing, which helps him feel better and think more clearly. he also overprepares by rehearsing the talk hundreds of times in his head while commuting or lying in bed.

why does will klein say to apply for roles that feel out of reach?

on guidance counselor 2.0, will klein from north says people usually sell themselves short and underestimate what they are capable of achieving. if you get rejected from a dream role, you will learn a lot from the interview and realize what gaps you need to work on. he interviewed at github for a role he wanted for years, did not get it, but learned he needed to understand buffers better and erased potential regret about never trying.
