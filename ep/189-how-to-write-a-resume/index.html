how to write a resume

with taylor desseyn (solo)

  • put only your name and a work specific email at the top of your resume, skip phone number and address
  • write a summary section with four or five bullet points on years of experience and top skills so recruiters can decide fast
  • list each job with company, start and end date by month and year, position, location, and a quick summary of what the company does
  • break out professional experience by project with a short overview, bullet points on your tasks, and the technologies used
  • keep your resume simple with no pictures, colors, or double columns so your experience is easy to scan

questions this episode answers

what does taylor desseyn say belongs at the top of a tech resume?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says to put only your name and a work-specific email at the very top of your resume. he recommends leaving off your phone number because some people have had to change numbers after getting too many calls, and skipping your address since the world is now virtual. a separate work email helps you avoid accidentally replying to a hiring manager when you meant to email family.

how does taylor desseyn recommend writing the summary section of a resume?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says to write a summary section with four or five bullet points that show your years of experience and top technical skills. he explains that if he can open your resume and immediately see ten years of software engineering, six years of javascript, and three years of react, he will call you without even reading the rest of the resume. the summary is so important because it lets recruiters decide fast whether you match the role.

how should you list professional experience on a resume according to taylor desseyn?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says to list each job with the company name, start and end date by month and year, position held, and location at the top. then write a quick summary of what the company does, because if you work at a stealth startup a recruiter never heard of, that context helps them understand the environment. break out your work by project with a short overview, three or four bullet points on your specific tasks, and a final bullet listing all technologies used.
