hiring for potential

with taylor desseyn (solo)

  • be brutally honest with recruiters about what you can and cannot do before entering an interview process
  • practice explaining your thought process out loud since communicating how you solve problems matters more than reaching the right answer
  • get comfortable saying i don't know during interviews instead of pretending to know everything
  • if you are a hiring manager rejecting people for missing a few skills that gap often points to weak training and mentorship in your organization
  • look for companies that let you choose between live coding and take home tests since flexibility helps more candidates succeed

questions this episode answers

what does taylor desseyn say about being honest with recruiters during the hiring process?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says job seekers need to be brutally honest with recruiters about what they can and cannot do from the very start of the process. he gives the example of a ui designer who told him upfront that they do not code heavily, which taylor loved because it set clear expectations. this honesty prevents miscommunication during the interview telephone game and helps everyone understand the candidate's actual skills.

how does taylor desseyn recommend handling interview questions you don't know the answer to?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says candidates need to get comfortable saying i don't know during interviews instead of pretending they know everything. he explains that hiring managers want to hear you say you don't know and be okay communicating that honestly. taylor advises approaching interviews like a conversation with a friend rather than locking up from stress, and being open, honest, and yourself throughout the process.

what does taylor desseyn say about hiring managers who reject candidates missing a few skills?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says when hiring managers reject candidates who have seven out of ten required skills but are missing three, it speaks to a lack of training and mentorship within their organization. he argues that companies need to start interviewing for potential instead of only for exact current needs. if a manager does not feel comfortable hiring someone because they lack a few skills, it means their organization's training structure is broken, which is likely why they keep losing people.
