get promoted w/o being the loudest in the room

guest: jennifer marcou, leadership coach for introverts, author of lead with quiet confidence

what jennifer marcou covers in this episode

jennifer marcou is a leadership coach for introverts and the author of lead with quiet confidence. this episode is a practical playbook for people who don't want to be the loudest person in the room but still want the credit, the promotion or the job. her core idea is that introverts don't need to act like extroverts. they need to use the strengths they already have: curiosity, listening, observation and preparation.

speaking up without being the loudest

you don't have to come up with a brand new idea to contribute in a meeting. jennifer's simplest move is one strategic question, like what does success look like for this meeting? another plays to an introvert's ability to observe: summarize where the group landed, as in it sounds like we're concluding x. people remember that you contributed, even if they don't remember exactly what you said.

showing impact without bragging

taylor asks how someone interviewing right now can show impact without sounding like they're bragging. jennifer's answer has a few parts. watch your verbs, the same way you would on a resume: led says more than managed. include outcomes, because introverts often assume their results speak for themselves, and they don't unless you share them. she cites research on recent women mba grads that found the biggest driver of success wasn't asking for promotions or taking on special projects. it was whether their boss knew what they were doing.

so tell your boss. even if you won't announce a big win, put it in your one-on-one update alongside the issues you want to discuss. she also reframes self-promotion: when colleagues know what you're working on, it helps them too, because they know who to come to.

networking in a ballroom full of strangers

for big events, jennifer has clients set a small goal, like talking to three people, and then they're allowed to leave. you don't have to meet everyone or find the one person who gets you a job. ask questions you actually care about the answer to, and skip the weather. prepare those questions ahead of time, because introverts are better at preparing than improvising. prepare your own intro too, ideally without your title or company, like i help build customer experiences so our customers save time and money. look for people standing alone at the edges, move conversations somewhere quieter, or ask to connect on linkedin and set up a one-on-one. networking, she says, is really just a conversation.

keep a dig list

come review time, most people sit down in december and can't remember what they did in january. jennifer keeps what she calls a dig list, short for damn, i'm good, and checks each week whether anything belongs on it. what you share with your boss can come from that list, focused on outcomes like money saved or a hard design problem solved. taylor's spin: set up a dig list skill in claude, chatgpt or gemini that looks through your email and slack every month and tells you what you accomplished.

her advice to her younger self was short: be confident in yourself, you are enough.
