ep 456 · may 19, 2025

power of engaging on social and how to interview with a startup w/ founder, rae lambert

guest: rae lambert, founder, river

  • treat posting on social media like a long game, small consistent posts compound more than chasing viral hits
  • engineers benefit from learning go to market basics like understanding funnels, tracking, and why marketing work matters
  • when interviewing with a startup, show you understand the founder's problem using a jobs to be done style framing
  • build a small, curated community around shared interests or struggles instead of chasing huge follower counts
  • when applying for jobs, record a short loom video introducing yourself and show side projects you built out of curiosity

questions this episode answers

what does rae lambert say about posting on social media for your career?

on guidance counselor 2.0, rae lambert says every individual post is worthless but the history and sum of the parts is what matters. she emphasizes that no one cares about you at the beginning, so you can delete tweets that don't perform and just keep posting. the compounding effect over time is what brings credibility and connections, not any single viral post.

how does rae lambert recommend interviewing with a startup founder?

on guidance counselor 2.0, rae lambert recommends using the jobs to be done framework to quickly understand the founder's problem during an interview. she also suggests being a user of the product you would help build and giving actionable feedback based on real usage. she requires a loom video introduction from applicants and moves straight to paid test projects at market rate instead of reading resumes.

what does rae lambert say engineers should learn about marketing?

on guidance counselor 2.0, rae lambert says engineers should start caring about go to market work because they often don't understand how hard it is to get people to care about and pay for ideas. she points out that engineers don't know basic marketing tools like utm tracking or how funnels work, and they end up building things from scratch that already exist. she emphasizes that bringing a product to market is completely separate from building it.
