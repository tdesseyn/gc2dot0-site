the power of putting yourself out there w/ aaron francis

guest: aaron francis, co-founder at tryhard studios

  • lower the bar for what you share by posting a novel to you insight instead of waiting for a universal insight
  • frame your posts as i did this instead of you should always do this so you avoid defending a position you cannot back up
  • build content brain by writing down clever moments while you work so you never run out of things to share
  • ask for the opportunity you want since the worst case is the same no but the best case opens a new door
  • do your best work even when the audience is tiny because you never know who is watching or who they will tell later

questions this episode answers

what does aaron francis say about overcoming fear of sharing your work online?

on guidance counselor 2.0, aaron francis says you should lower the bar for what counts as shareable by posting things that are novel to you, not necessarily novel to the universe. he recommends framing posts as i did this instead of you should always do this so you avoid defending a position you cannot back up. this approach makes you more approachable and lets you have conversations instead of arguments with critics.

how does aaron francis suggest building a habit of creating content while working?

on guidance counselor 2.0, aaron francis says he keeps a yellow legal pad next to him while working and writes down any clever moment or insight on a clever list. later, when done working, he reviews the list and turns items into tweets or videos. he calls this developing content brain and promises that once you train your brain to notice shareable moments, you will never run out of things to share.

what does aaron francis say about performing when the audience is small or absent?

on guidance counselor 2.0, aaron francis describes speaking at github universe to only three people in an empty room with no livestream or recording, which he found mortifying. despite the embarrassment, he says your job is to do your best work regardless of circumstances because it is both morally right and strategically optimal. you never know who is watching or who they will tell later, and being known as someone who gives their all no matter what gets around.
