3 things to do when you are laid off

with taylor desseyn (solo)

  • update your resume first and tell a story with project details instead of a long list of bullet points
  • make sure your linkedin has an updated title, banner, and photo since it exists for recruiters and hiring managers to find you
  • collect recommendations on linkedin to help you stand out from other applicants
  • work with recruiters before applying elsewhere since they can do a lot of the heavy lifting for you
  • get your resume uploaded to job boards and consider ones like dice and indeed for tech roles

questions this episode answers

what does taylor desseyn say to do first when you are laid off?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says the first thing to do when laid off is get your resume ready with project details and specific technologies used to solve problems. he emphasizes that resumes should tell a story rather than just listing fifteen bullet points of things you've done. the resume is telling a story, so it needs to show how you solved specific projects.

what does taylor desseyn say about linkedin after a layoff?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says your linkedin needs to be updated with a title, cover picture, banner, and good profile picture. he stresses that your linkedin is not for you, but for recruiters and hiring managers to find you, so it needs specific keywords. he also recommends getting recommendations from others because testimonials are huge for differentiating yourself from other applicants.

what is taylor desseyn's two and two rule for screening recruiters?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn recommends working with recruiters who have been recruiting for at least two years and have been in your city for at least two years. he explains that recruiters who bounce around every eighteen months usually do so to get better base salaries because they are not great at recruiting. he also says if a recruiter has been in a market for less than two years, they do not have good enough market intelligence to educate and guide you.
