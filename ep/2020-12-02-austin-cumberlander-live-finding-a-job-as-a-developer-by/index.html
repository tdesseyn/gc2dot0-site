austin cumberlander live + finding a job as a developer by putting out content

guest: austin cumberlander, junior software developer

  • post real projects with a short description of what you built and what you learned to start building visibility
  • sharing your honest job search journey and feelings often resonates more than polished project updates
  • end posts with a question to invite comments, that engagement signals and conversation help posts travel further
  • reply to comments and engage with other people's posts or your own audience will stop engaging back
  • stay consistent with posting and don't take it too seriously, focus on your goal instead of fear of judgment

questions this episode answers

what does austin cumberlander say about how he got his first developer job through linkedin?

on guidance counselor 2.0, austin cumberlander says he got his first developer job after posting a video on linkedin about not getting an interview, which generated around 20,000 views. a recruiter saw the video, appreciated his positive energy and persistence, and connected him with the company where he was eventually hired. the post focused on his journey and feelings rather than just technical projects.

what does austin cumberlander recommend for developers who don't want to appear on camera?

on guidance counselor 2.0, austin cumberlander recommends that shy developers or those who don't want to be on camera can simply post their completed projects with a description of what they built and what they learned. he says this approach was used by someone who inspired him, and it still generated thousands of views without requiring any video of herself.

what does austin cumberlander say about getting engagement on linkedin posts?

on guidance counselor 2.0, austin cumberlander says to stay as consistent as possible with posting and to end posts with questions or thoughts that would be interesting and encourage people to provide input. he and taylor agree that you need to comment and like other people's posts, because if you aren't engaging back with others, people will stop engaging with your content.
