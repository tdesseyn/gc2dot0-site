ep 205 · jan 11, 2023

resume triage w/alishah novin

guest: alishah novin, hiring manager at microsoft

  • put your technical expertise and summary at the top of your resume instead of burying it under education and certifications
  • write a short summary with specific years of experience and technologies so a hiring manager can place you in seconds
  • quantify your bullet points with real numbers like users, team size, or bugs completed instead of vague statements
  • keep formatting simple and consistent since cluttered layouts, dual columns, and mixed fonts make hiring managers pass even on strong candidates
  • if you lack paid experience, list self-study and side projects with the tech stack and the problem you were solving to show how you think

questions this episode answers

what does alishah novin say should go at the top of a technical resume?

on guidance counselor 2.0, alishah novin says your professional experience should start near the top of the page, not buried under education and certifications. he recommends putting a brief summary with your technical expertise first, then moving straight into your work history. hiring managers want to see your accomplishments immediately, not your degree or course list.

how does taylor desseyn recommend summarizing your experience on a resume?

on guidance counselor 2.0, taylor desseyn says your summary should be a single sentence listing exact years of experience with specific technologies, like ten plus years of overall development, six years of dot net, three years of angular, two years of php, and your degree. this lets a recruiter or hiring manager place you in seconds without doing math from your job dates. if you are early in your career, write an about me statement with your background and what you are targeting instead.

what does alishah novin say about quantifying bullet points on a resume?

on guidance counselor 2.0, alishah novin says you should quantify your accomplishments with real numbers like team size, user count, or even bugs completed per week. he explains that hiring managers want to see you think about impact in measurable terms, not just list tasks you were assigned. even if you are junior and only completed seven bugs a week, stating that number shows how you think and helps him assess your work at scale.
