how to get a job off of x/twitter w/ staff eng, jacob evans

guest: jacob evans, staff engineer at wop.com

  • treat tech twitter as a place for genuine connection, not just memes or drama
  • find mentors by engaging publicly with their posts instead of dming asking for mentorship
  • build your network by finding shared interests outside of tech like hobbies or background
  • when a post gets traction, be ready to say yes to opportunities like flying out to meet a team
  • job boards and easy apply can have a very low response rate, so build real connections elsewhere

questions this episode answers

how does jacob evans recommend finding mentors on tech twitter?

on guidance counselor 2.0, jacob evans says you can have mentors without the traditional concept of them spending dedicated time on you by engaging publicly with them on twitter. instead of dming someone and asking them to mentor you, he recommends engaging with them on the posts they are already willing to share, commenting with your thoughts on why you like or disagree with their ideas. he built a relationship with kent c dodds this way through passive mentorship by consistently engaging with his posts, which eventually led to him helping with kent's discord community.

what does jacob evans say about building genuine connections on tech twitter?

on guidance counselor 2.0, jacob evans says he focused on building genuine connections with people on twitter rather than just posting memes and doing normal social media stuff. he found common ground outside of tech, like when he became friends with ryan carniato, creator of solidjs, by dming him about magic the gathering after ryan mentioned the card game. he also connected with jerome hardway because they both served in the air force as security forces before transitioning to tech, showing that shared interests and backgrounds outside of work create stronger networking bonds.
