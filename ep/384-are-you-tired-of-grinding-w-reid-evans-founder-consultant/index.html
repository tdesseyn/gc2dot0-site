ep 384 · jul 12, 2024

are you tired of grinding? w/reid evans, founder/consultant

guest: reid evans, founder/consultant

  • treat grinding differently from doing deep work you actually enjoy, since nonstop busyness can mask burnout
  • try getting genuinely bored on purpose, since stepping away from your phone can spark better ideas and conversations
  • build financial runway and live below your means before leaving a full time job to go out on your own
  • niche down into a specific skill set to stand out, since generalists get lost in a noisy hiring market
  • use networking as a way to find people you genuinely want to work with, not everyone is your customer

questions this episode answers

what does reid evans say about getting bored on purpose?

on guidance counselor 2.0, reid evans says getting properly bored for a week, not just five minutes, forces you to think about what you actually enjoy doing and what provides value. he found that on days when he takes time to be bored and goes for a walk with no goals, the conversations he has afterward are far better because he is genuinely interested in where they can go. being bored is uncomfortable, but it lets you figure out what you are passionate about instead of staying busy to avoid discomfort.

how does reid evans recommend preparing to go solo in tech?

on guidance counselor 2.0, reid evans says he built financial runway by living below his means and saving almost half his income for years, which gave him options and kept him from making knee-jerk decisions. he also spent years building a network by speaking at community conferences and running meetups, which gave him people to work with once he went solo. he warns against rage quitting and says having that safety net means he only works with people he genuinely wants to work with, not just whoever will pay.

what does reid evans say about niching down as a developer?

on guidance counselor 2.0, reid evans says he had to niche down to maybe 300 companies on the planet that use the specific erp systems and reporting he specializes in. he explains that being specific in something is a way to get your foot in the door, and once you are in, you show every bit of who you are so you are not just a replaceable peg. taylor and reid agree that in today's market companies need a specific developer to solve a specific problem right now, not a generalist.
