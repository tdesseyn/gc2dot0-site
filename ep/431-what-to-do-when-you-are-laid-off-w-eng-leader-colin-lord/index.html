what to do when you are laid off w/ eng leader, colin lord

guest: colin lord, senior manager of e-commerce engineering at carter's

  • when you get laid off, take a day or two to grieve before diving into the job search
  • handle practical items fast after a layoff, like filing for unemployment and signing up for health insurance through the marketplace before coverage gaps hit
  • focus on networking and direct outreach through dms and texts rather than mass applying to job postings
  • don't get emotionally attached to a role until you have a signed offer, since interviews can fall through for reasons outside your control
  • be kind to yourself during a job search since rejection is not a reflection of your worth or skill as a person

questions this episode answers

what does colin lord say to do immediately after getting laid off?

on guidance counselor 2.0, colin lord says to handle two practical items right away even before grieving: file for unemployment because it takes time to process, and sign up for health insurance through the marketplace or another plan. he had to sign up for healthcare.gov by december 15th to have coverage in january after being laid off on december 14th. after handling those two things, then take time to grieve.

how does colin lord recommend applying for jobs after a layoff?

on guidance counselor 2.0, colin lord says he focused on networking and direct outreach through linkedin dms and texts rather than mass applying to job boards. he reached out to people he knew, recruiters, and looked through linkedin to see who worked at companies with openings. he never got to the point of just scrolling job boards and clicking apply until mid-january because he felt those weren't real jobs during the holidays anyway.

what does colin lord say about getting emotionally attached to job opportunities?

on guidance counselor 2.0, colin lord says not to let yourself get emotionally invested in a role until the very end. he learned from past experience that telling friends about opportunities early creates extra burden when you get rejected, because then you have to tell them it didn't work out. even for the job he eventually got at carter's, he didn't let himself get excited until after he received the written offer to sign.
