ep 491 · sep 29, 2025

a very analytical approach to the job search w/ jerome hardaway

guest: jerome hardaway, software engineer and founder at vetswhocode

  • if you are senior keep your resume to the last ten years of experience since most strict background checks and ats systems only look back that far
  • break a broad resume into focused versions by skill area so ats and recruiters can clearly see your expertise instead of a confusing mix
  • use linkedin to talk publicly about what you know so your activity signals expertise and draws the right recruiters and opportunities to you
  • keep your resume active on niche job boards and log in daily since staying visible can get you found by recruiters for roles you never applied to
  • build real working projects you can fully explain in detail since being able to talk through decisions matters more than having a finished template

questions this episode answers

what does jerome hardaway say about how many years of experience to put on a senior resume?

on guidance counselor 2.0, jerome hardaway says to keep your resume to the last ten years of experience if you are senior. he explains that strict background checks in finance only go back ten years, and applicant tracking systems trained on that time frame parse resumes better. he also found that going past ten years triggers ageism concerns while less than five years signals lack of experience.

how does jerome hardaway use linkedin and dice differently in his job search?

on guidance counselor 2.0, jerome hardaway says to use linkedin to talk publicly about what you know so your activity signals expertise to the right recruiters. he posted regularly about data engineering and ai topics to show domain knowledge. for dice, he uploaded his three resumes and logged in daily but did not apply to jobs, because logging in every twenty four hours puts you at the top of recruiter searches. two of his four offers came from dice recruiters finding him, not from applications.
