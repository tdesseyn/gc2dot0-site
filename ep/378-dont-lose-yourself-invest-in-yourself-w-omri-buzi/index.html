ep 378 · jun 28, 2024

don't lose yourself, invest in yourself w/ omri buzi

guest: omri buzi, product manager

  • start the transition to independent work by having conversations with no expectations, not waiting for opportunities to come to you
  • treat going fractional or freelance as a trial, not a tattoo, since full time work will always be there if you go back
  • stop merging your identity with your employer, what you do for a company is their business but what you do with your income is yours
  • find affinity groups like alumni networks, scouting, or hobby communities since people respond more to shared connections
  • if you worry about seeming too salesy when networking, that worry itself means you are unlikely to ever cross that line

questions this episode answers

what does omri buzi say about getting started with freelance or consulting work?

on guidance counselor 2.0, omri buzi says to start by having conversations with no expectations or asks, just learning and seeing where they lead. he emphasizes that no one will come to you, so you must initiate conversations yourself. over time these conversations will reveal how you can deliver value to the market and what modality fits you best.

what does omri buzi say about going back to full-time work after freelancing?

on guidance counselor 2.0, omri buzi says going fractional or freelance is not a tattoo, it is more like a trial you can step back from. full-time jobs will always exist and companies will always be hiring, so trying independent work does not close any doors. if it does not fit, you can return to traditional employment and the experience will make for good interview conversation.

what does omri buzi say about separating your identity from your employer?

on guidance counselor 2.0, omri buzi says you should never merge your identity with your employer because what you do for a company is their business but investing in yourself is yours. he realized he had been reading business books only through the lens of how they could help his employer, rather than how they could develop him as a person. keeping those two things separate means you always invest in yourself even while working full-time.
