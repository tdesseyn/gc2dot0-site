hanging with a friend w/cam goedde, product marketing manager at dell

guest: cam goedde, product marketing manager at dell

  • when switching into a new role, lean into your actual strengths instead of comparing yourself to specialists around you
  • give yourself a few months to step back from day-to-day tasks and figure out what unique value you bring
  • stop waiting for content to be perfect, post the rough version now and let the idea evolve over time
  • build a tangible hobby or physical habit outside of work so you can see real output and avoid burnout
  • when picking communities or projects to join, look for people and teams that are public and transparent rather than anonymous

questions this episode answers

what does cam goedde say about dealing with imposter syndrome in a new job?

on guidance counselor 2.0, cam goedde says it took him two to three months in his new product marketing role to step back from tactical tasks and identify what unique value he brought. he realized he needed to lean into his strengths—his customer-facing and sales mentality—rather than compare himself to peers with finance or mba backgrounds. he recommends giving yourself time to get out of the weeds and focus on what you're actually good at instead of feeling unqualified.

what does cam goedde recommend for getting started with content creation?

on guidance counselor 2.0, cam goedde says you need to post the worst version of your content now instead of waiting for a clear vision or perfection. he admits he spent years planning without shipping because he wanted everything nailed down, but he now realizes the vision will never be perfectly clear. he recommends focusing on what you want to talk about right now and letting the content evolve over time, just like building any other habit.

what does cam goedde say about building habits outside of work to avoid burnout?

on guidance counselor 2.0, cam goedde says getting away from the screen and doing physical work has been critical for him because remote work left him unable to see what he accomplished each day. he started landscaping and building things in his shop because he grew up in a blue-collar family and needs that immediate, tangible output. he says finding hobbies where you can see real results helps balance the abstract nature of knowledge work and prevents burnout.
