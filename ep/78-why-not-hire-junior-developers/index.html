ep 78 · sep 15, 2021

why not hire junior developers?

with taylor desseyn (solo)

  • companies default to hiring senior devs because clients ask for senior talent, not because juniors cant do the job
  • if you dont have time to train new hires you are by definition understaffed
  • juniors who get trained and given a chance tend to stay longer than senior devs who get poached constantly
  • hire for talent and long term fit with the organization, not just for one specific role
  • always hire one person ahead of your current need instead of waiting until youre in a crunch

questions this episode answers

why does taylor desseyn think companies should hire more junior developers?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn argues that junior developers will stay at a company longer than senior developers if the company invests time in training them. he polled his live audience and received overwhelming confirmation from junior developers that they would remain loyal to companies that took a chance on them. taylor believes companies keep losing senior developers because they get poached constantly, while trained juniors become loyal employees who stay three years or more.

what does taylor desseyn mean when he says if you don't have time to train you are understaffed?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says that if a company does not have the time to slow down and appropriately train new employees, that is by definition being understaffed. he argues companies should put more emphasis on onboarding, hiring, and training rather than constantly hiring only senior developers and throwing them at problems. his point is that the inability to invest training time reveals a staffing problem, not a justification for avoiding junior hires.

how does taylor desseyn think companies should evaluate engineering candidates?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says companies need to hire for talent and evaluate how a person could fit within the organization as a whole, not just for one specific position. he recommends finding culture fits with the drive to learn and filling in technical gaps through training. taylor argues that companies should look at whether someone's skills can transfer to another part of the organization if they don't match perfectly with the initial role.
