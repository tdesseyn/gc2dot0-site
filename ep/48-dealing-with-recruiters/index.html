dealing with recruiters

with taylor desseyn (solo)

  • if a recruiter dms you a role that doesn't fit, send screening questions back or just don't respond and move on
  • recruiters get paid by companies through commission, not by candidates, so never pay a recruiter for help finding a job
  • if there's no job posting for an interview, ask the recruiter for the actual intake notes from the hiring manager
  • practice your elevator pitch out loud and record yourself to catch habits and improve how you sound in interviews
  • to find fair pay, research ranges and talk openly with trusted peers in your field instead of relying on vague titles like junior or senior

questions this episode answers

what does taylor desseyn say about dealing with recruiters who send irrelevant job messages?

on guidance counselor 2.0, taylor desseyn says if a recruiter messages you about a role that doesn't fit, you can send them his screening questions from his website or just not respond at all. he emphasizes that recruiters don't care about you personally, they only care about getting responses, so you won't hurt their feelings by ignoring them. he has a free guide with ten questions to stump a recruiter that you can download and send to recruiters who reach out.

what does taylor desseyn recommend when interviewing for a job with no posting?

on guidance counselor 2.0, taylor desseyn says if you're interviewing for a role with no job posting and you're working with a recruiter, ask them for the actual intake notes from the hiring manager instead of a standard job description. if you're not working with a recruiter, ask the interviewer directly for a job description, and if that's not possible, research the company on linkedin to find people in similar roles and learn what skills and technologies they use.
