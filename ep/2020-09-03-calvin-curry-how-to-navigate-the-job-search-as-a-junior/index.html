calvin curry + how to navigate the job search as a junior developer

guest: calvin curry

  • treat your capstone project as the bare minimum, not the finish line, when job hunting as a junior dev
  • take on free work for nonprofits or small businesses to build a real portfolio while job searching
  • list projects on your resume like real jobs, with a summary, tools used, and technologies for each
  • stop mass applying and instead message alumni or employees at target companies to get warm introductions
  • after any networking call always ask for a next step, like an intro to a manager or another developer

questions this episode answers

what does taylor desseyn say about capstone projects for junior developers?

on guidance counselor 2.0, taylor desseyn tells junior developer calvin curry that a capstone project from boot camp is now the bare minimum, not something that will make you stand out. he recommends taking on free work for nonprofits or small businesses to build additional portfolio projects while job hunting. these projects should be listed on your resume like real jobs, with a summary, the tools used, and the technologies for each one.

how does taylor desseyn recommend networking as a boot camp graduate?

on guidance counselor 2.0, taylor desseyn tells calvin curry to search for all alumni from his boot camp on linkedin and message those who currently have their first developer job. instead of asking if they are hiring, he should ask for an introduction to their manager, framing it as wanting to learn how that manager interviews junior developers. taylor emphasizes that having a call where you ask a manager for interview advice is already an interview itself, and you should always end networking calls by asking for a next step.

what does taylor desseyn say about applying to jobs as a junior developer?

on guidance counselor 2.0, taylor desseyn challenges calvin curry to stop mass applying to jobs and instead spend that time connecting with people at target companies on linkedin. he recommends finding employees at companies with openings, messaging them directly about the role, and trying to get warm introductions rather than submitting a resume blindly. taylor says this approach takes more work but will lead to a job faster than spray and pray application methods.
