how to build healthy developer culture w/ matt stauffer, ceo of tighten

guest: matt stauffer, ceo of tighten

  • build healthy dev culture by applying empathy first, treating employees the way you would want to be treated
  • watch for red flags when a company dismisses questions about work-life balance during the interview process
  • try to talk to someone other than the hiring manager before accepting an offer to get a real sense of the culture
  • when job hunting, research the hiring manager and company deeply since a generic resume sent to hundreds of openings gets deleted immediately
  • if you want a promotion, ask your employer to clearly define the next role and the specific steps needed to get there

questions this episode answers

what does matt stauffer say about building healthy developer culture?

on guidance counselor 2.0, matt stauffer says the core of building healthy dev culture is applying the golden rule and empathy in every circumstance. he and his co-founder asked themselves if they could build a consultancy they actually wanted to work for while still being profitable, which became their founding experiment. when hiring managers make decisions, they should ask what would be best for the employee in that situation, balancing care for people with the company's need to stay in business.

how does matt stauffer suggest vetting a company's work-life balance during interviews?

on guidance counselor 2.0, matt stauffer says if a company dismisses questions about work-life balance during the interview process, they are showing you a red flag and saving you from working there. he recommends asking specific questions like how often people work extra hours, weekends, or evenings, and whether employees can step away to pick up their kids. the most helpful tactic is talking to someone who is not the hiring manager, because bosses often say nice things that sound appealing but may not back them up when it gets hard.

what does matt stauffer recommend for getting a promotion?

on guidance counselor 2.0, matt stauffer says it is the employer's job to define the metrics for promotion in the first place, showing exactly what the next role is and the specific steps to get there. if a company has not defined those steps, employees should ask their manager to define the next role and how to reach it, then work on those things in one-on-ones and set goals. at tighten, promotion is based on doing the things the next role requires, like reviewing code, delegating tasks, and presenting to clients, not just being the best programmer.
