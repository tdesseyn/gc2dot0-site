tips and tricks on how to write an effective resume

with taylor desseyn (solo)

  • early career resumes should include more detail, not less, so hiring managers see everything you have done
  • tailor your resume to the specific job you want to get called for instead of leaving in unrelated old experience
  • replace vague bullet points with specific project details and quantitative results like team size or timeline
  • give comparative stats when switching industries so hiring managers can see drive and performance in a new context
  • use clean spacing and reasonable font size instead of bolding everything so your resume is easy to read in seconds

questions this episode answers

what does taylor desseyn say about resume length for junior developers?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says junior developers and early career professionals should actually write more information on their resumes, not less. hiring managers need to see everything a junior person does because they lack extensive experience. taylor advises including detailed information about all tasks and responsibilities so employers can fully evaluate early-career candidates.

how does taylor desseyn recommend tailoring resumes for specific jobs?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says to remove old technology and unrelated experience that causes you to get calls for jobs you don't want. if you worked with outdated tech decades ago, take it out so your resume only shows what you want to be called about. taylor also recommends creating multiple resumes for different skill sets, so a developer with both dot net and angular experience should have separate resumes tailored to each type of role.

what does taylor desseyn say about using numbers and project details on resumes?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says to replace vague bullet points with specific project details including team size, timeline and quantitative results. instead of writing generic tasks, describe actual projects with numbers like how many people you hired over how many weeks. taylor emphasizes giving comparative stats when switching industries, like mentioning hitting sales milestones as a server to show drive and performance that translates to a software role.
