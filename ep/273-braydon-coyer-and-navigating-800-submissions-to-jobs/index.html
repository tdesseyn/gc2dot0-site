ep 273 · sep 15, 2023

braydon coyer and navigating 800 submissions to jobs

guest: braydon coyer, senior front-end developer at logic gate

  • expect the job search to feel slow until networking kicks in, even after hundreds of applications
  • talk to people for a couple minutes, not thirty, networking is just a short genuine conversation
  • juniors can stand out by showing initiative and talking to people even without much experience yet
  • seniors should be adaptable across frameworks and able to find solutions anywhere, not tied to one stack
  • build networking as a skill by practicing in low stakes settings like coffee shops before conferences

questions this episode answers

what does braydon coyer say about networking versus submitting hundreds of applications?

on guidance counselor 2.0, braydon coyer says he submitted over 800 applications in 12 months when entering web development, getting only 20 callbacks, five or six interviews, and one offer. looking back, he would focus more on networking instead of mass applications, because every opportunity after that first job came through networking. he attended a few meetups during the search but wishes he had leaned into networking much more from the start.

how long should a networking conversation be at a conference, according to braydon coyer?

on guidance counselor 2.0, braydon coyer says networking conversations should be short, not scripted 30-minute elevator pitches. he recommends talking to someone for 90 seconds, two minutes, or three minutes maximum. taylor desseyn agrees, noting he and braydon only chatted for six or seven minutes total at a conference but still built a connection.

what does braydon coyer say makes someone a good senior front-end engineer?

on guidance counselor 2.0, braydon coyer says a good senior front-end engineer is not tied to any specific framework, because every job uses different technology. a senior should be able to be dropped into any situation and find the solution to the problem, even if they do not know the answer immediately, by knowing who to ask or where to look. he notes he works with angular daily but previously used java, spring boot, and other frameworks.
