ep 264 · aug 25, 2023

jem young, engineering manager at netflix and the state of tech

guest: jem young, engineering manager at netflix

  • track your wins in a journal, even five or ten minutes every week or two, so you have artifacts ready for reviews or resumes
  • when things get uncertain, be visible, make sure managers and vps know your name and what you deliver
  • put hard numbers behind your work like money saved, projects shipped, or people hired instead of just describing effort
  • junior engineers stand out by showing initiative like fixing outdated docs or pulling threads on problems, not by listing fake metrics
  • ai will likely take over boilerplate and repetitive coding tasks, so get ahead by learning how it fits into your workflow now

questions this episode answers

what does jem young say about standing out during layoffs?

on guidance counselor 2.0, jem young, engineering manager at netflix, says people should make sure their name is known by managers and vps, and they should have concrete artifacts like documents they wrote, projects they shipped, money they saved, or teams they pulled together. he explains that when layoffs happen, managers look at who delivers the most and contributes visibly. young emphasizes putting hard numbers behind your work rather than just describing effort.

how can junior engineers stand out according to jem young?

on guidance counselor 2.0, jem young, engineering manager at netflix, says junior engineers should show leader behavior and initiative even without experience. he looks for juniors who pull threads on problems they discover in the code, update outdated documentation for the next person, or demonstrate a drive to make the business better. young advises against fake metrics and instead recommends telling stories about projects you drove or impact you had on the company.

what does jem young say about ai taking front end developer jobs?

on guidance counselor 2.0, jem young, engineering manager at netflix, says ai will likely handle boilerplate and repetitive coding tasks but cannot solve the hard problems, business nuances, or codebase complexities that require human judgment. he recommends engineers start learning how ai can improve their current workflow, such as writing documentation or generating template code, rather than fighting the future. young believes the menial jobs that don't require much brain power are what ai will replace.
