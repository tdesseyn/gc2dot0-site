content and the recruiting industry w/joel lalgee from hirewell

guest: joel lalgee, hirewell

  • incentivize recruiters to create content instead of expecting it for free on top of their regular work
  • get leadership buy in first, since content efforts feel unpredictable and hard to track without support from the top

questions this episode answers

what does joel lalgee say about how recruiting companies should support content creators?

on guidance counselor 2.0, joel lalgee from hirewell says recruiting companies should pay people to create content instead of expecting it for free on top of their regular work. he explains that hirewell tracks ambassador content and compensates employees for the time they spend producing it. lalgee also emphasizes that leadership buy-in is critical because content feels unpredictable and hard to track, so support from the top makes or breaks adoption.

how does joel lalgee recommend junior recruiters get started with content?

on guidance counselor 2.0, joel lalgee advises junior recruiters to post about their day-to-day experiences and what they are learning rather than trying to act like thought leaders. he says recruiters hear things in one-on-one conversations that hiring managers never hear, so sharing those insights is valuable. lalgee warns against overcomplicating it and says most early posts will get only five or ten likes, but consistency over time builds a following.

what does joel lalgee say about production quality for recruiting content?

on guidance counselor 2.0, joel lalgee says his production quality has always been low and will stay that way because he cares about the message, not the polish. he warns that people often see established creators with crisp video and big followings and assume they need expensive setups, but those creators started with low production too. lalgee advises against spending thousands of dollars on gear before testing whether you even enjoy making content.
