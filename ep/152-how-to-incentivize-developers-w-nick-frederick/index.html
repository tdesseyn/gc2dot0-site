how to incentivize developers w/ nick frederick

guest: nick frederick, ceo of rebar technology

  • share business context and pipeline updates with developers so they feel part of the bigger mission not just their task
  • run regular one on ones even short ones to build rapport and surface honest feedback over time
  • start every new hire with full trust then adjust based on the quality and consistency of their work
  • commission plans for developers sound appealing but are hard to execute since you cant fairly attribute revenue to individual code
  • prioritize cultural fit and the ability to learn over existing technical skills since tools change every few years

questions this episode answers

what does nick frederick say about sharing business context with developers?

on guidance counselor 2.0, nick frederick says leaders should share pipeline updates and business health with developers so they feel part of the bigger mission, not just their task. he has seen developers research potential partners and return with helpful technical insights after hearing about upcoming projects. sharing context helps developers understand what work is coming and prepares them to contribute beyond just their immediate coding assignments.

why does nick frederick think commission plans are hard for developers?

on guidance counselor 2.0, nick frederick says commission plans for developers are difficult because you cannot fairly calculate attribution. for early stage startups without material revenue, there is no clear revenue to base commissions on, and cost accounting to attribute work to specific projects or clients gets messy quickly. he acknowledges the appeal but has not found a workable formula.

what does nick frederick prioritize when hiring developers?

on guidance counselor 2.0, nick frederick says he prioritizes cultural fit and the ability to learn over existing technical skills. he tells his team that the aws services they use today will be different in two or three years, so being able to learn and adjust is just as important as what someone already knows. technical skills matter, but adaptability matters more in a fast-changing environment.
