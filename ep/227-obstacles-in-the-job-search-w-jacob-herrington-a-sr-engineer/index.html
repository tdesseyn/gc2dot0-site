ep 227 · mar 3, 2023

obstacles in the job search w/jacob herrington a sr. engineer

guest: jacob herrington, senior software engineer

  • if you lack a degree or connections, take rough early jobs and prove you can learn fast and work hard
  • list short job stints on your resume if you can explain why and what you delivered in that time
  • when a recruiter wont share full details on a role, ask for an unrealistic rate first to gauge real market pay
  • before doing a lengthy take home test, offer your public code history as proof of skill instead
  • build real relationships with people in your field instead of only reaching out when you need something

questions this episode answers

how does jacob herrington recommend getting into tech without a degree or connections?

on guidance counselor 2.0, jacob herrington says to take really bad early jobs and work extremely hard to prove you can learn and deliver. he took six internships at once, some of which he doesn't talk about because they were low-quality, but they gave him the experience to move forward. the key is making the best of those crappy jobs and moving on once you've proven yourself.

what does jacob herrington say about short job stints on your resume?

on guidance counselor 2.0, jacob herrington and taylor desseyn agree there is nothing wrong with having short stints on your resume if you can speak to why they were short and what you delivered. jacob points out that at startups he has been on hiring calls where he saw someone worked somewhere for 60 days, and if they delivered something valuable at a startup, that is actually a strong thing to discuss.

how does jacob herrington use recruiters to learn market salary rates?

on guidance counselor 2.0, jacob herrington says when a recruiter won't give him full details about a role, he starts by asking for an unrealistic rate and then keeps coming down until the recruiter stops making fun of the number. he knows exactly what software engineers with his level of experience go for in the market because he knows when recruiters stop laughing on phone calls, and that tells him the actual market rate.
