gone are the days of staying at one company forever

with taylor desseyn (solo)

  • its okay to job hop, gone are the days of staying at one company your whole career
  • if you have a short job stint, still put it on your resume and be ready to explain why it ended
  • in interviews, tell a clear story about why you left a job instead of just saying it wasnt good
  • the average job duration people see is around a year and a half to two years
  • staying at one company for many years is fine, but you need to keep innovating and show your title changes and growth on your resume and linkedin

questions this episode answers

what does taylor desseyn say about putting short job stints on your resume?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says you should include short job stints on your resume if you gained relevant skills, and you need to be prepared to discuss what happened in interviews. he emphasizes that saying it just wasn't good creates red flags, but telling a clear story about why it ended - like a management style mismatch where you and your manager decided to part ways - will be received much better by interviewers.

according to taylor desseyn, what is the average job duration he sees in tech?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says the average job duration he sees is around a year and a half to two years. he notes that for contract or project-based jobs, shorter durations of one to three months are normal, and candidates should clearly mark those as contract roles on their resume.

what does taylor desseyn say about staying at one company for many years?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says staying at the same job for ten years does not hurt you, as long as you continue innovating and learning new skills rather than working on outdated technology. he also emphasizes that you must show your career advancement within the company on your resume and linkedin, noting each promotion and title change instead of listing just one title for the entire duration.
