ep 79 · sep 17, 2021

job search thoughts

with taylor desseyn (solo)

  • before an interview write down specific accomplishments tied to the exact role so you do not blank when asked
  • treat interviews as a dialogue and ask your questions during the process instead of saving them for after an offer
  • when asked about salary expectations give a straight answer with a no brainer number and a real minimum instead of dodging
  • skip easy apply and quick apply buttons and instead connect directly with engineers, managers, directors and vps on linkedin
  • when messaging a hiring manager ask for a few minutes to learn about the company instead of asking about a specific open role

questions this episode answers

what does taylor desseyn say about preparing for interviews?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says to write down specific accomplishments from your past that relate directly to the job you are interviewing for before the interview starts. he explains that if you are interviewing for an angular ui role, you should prepare angular-specific accomplishments to discuss because people often blank out during interviews. he also recommends researching the company, having your resume in front of you, and researching the people who will interview you.

how does taylor desseyn recommend answering salary expectation questions?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn recommends giving a straight answer instead of dodging the question. he says to tell the company your no brainer number where you would accept immediately, then also state your real minimum. for example, if you want seventy five thousand dollars, say that is your no brainer acceptance level, but your minimum to consider the role would be seventy thousand dollars, and be ready to accept if they meet your number.

what does taylor desseyn recommend instead of using easy apply on job postings?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says to skip quick apply and easy apply buttons and instead search for the company on linkedin. he recommends connecting with engineers, engineering managers, directors, and vice presidents at the company. when messaging them, he says to ask for four minutes of their time to learn what makes the company special, not to ask about a specific open role, because asking about a specific job comes off as sleazy.
