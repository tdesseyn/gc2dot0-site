everything's broken w/ mike peditto

guest: mike peditto, recruiter, director of talent, and creator known online as realistic recruiting

  • focus applications on roles where you are a true top match instead of mass applying to everything you are loosely qualified for
  • skip the generic resume summary and use a clear headline at the top that instantly shows your role, skills, and industry fit
  • make sure your most important skills and achievements appear in the first third of your resume since recruiters scan fast
  • the ats is not an ai robot auto rejecting people, its a filing system where a human still searches and decides
  • when reaching out to a recruiter or hiring manager, be specific about why you match that exact role instead of sending a generic message

questions this episode answers

what does mike peditto say about how many applications a job posting gets now?

on guidance counselor 2.0, mike peditto says he opened a role on a friday afternoon and by monday morning had five hundred applicants, with many qualified candidates but only one position to fill. he explains that roles that used to get thirty applications now get a hundred, and roles that got a hundred now get a thousand, while hiring teams have shrunk dramatically. this overwhelming volume means four hundred ninety nine applicants will not get hired even when the process works efficiently.

what does mike peditto recommend about which jobs to apply to?

on guidance counselor 2.0, mike peditto advises job seekers to focus only on roles where they are a ninety five to one hundred percent match rather than applying to hundreds of jobs where they are fifty or sixty percent qualified. he explains that in the current market, people getting hired are those in the top five to ten percent of applicants for a specific role, and spending energy on anything less is unlikely to work. he says it is better to apply to fewer jobs where you know you are in that top bucket and can demonstrate why they would be foolish not to interview you.

what does mike peditto say the ats actually does?

on guidance counselor 2.0, mike peditto explains that an ats is a digital filing cabinet that recruiters use to organize applications, similar to how salespeople use salesforce. he says it is not an ai robot automatically rejecting people, but rather a human recruiter uses it to search for keywords like react or angular and then reads the resumes that come up in the search. instant rejections typically come from knockout questions that a recruiter sets up in advance, such as sponsorship requirements, not from ai scanning resumes.
