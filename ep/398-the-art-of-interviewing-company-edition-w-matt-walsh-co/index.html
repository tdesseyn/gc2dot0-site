ep 398 · aug 14, 2024

the art of interviewing (company edition) w/ matt walsh co-founder of talgo.io

guest: matt walsh, co-founder of talgo.io

  • before interviewing anyone, write a document defining the five or six key results the role must achieve and the cultural traits needed to thrive
  • as a candidate expect interviewers to let you talk for minutes without interrupting, so prepare a tight 90 second to three minute story and check in with them
  • network your way into roles instead of relying on mass applications, since a trusted referral lets both sides be honest and saves time
  • treat most requirements in job descriptions as a kitchen sink wishlist and apply even if you only meet about half of them
  • if you want to move teams internally, outperform in your current role and ask both your manager and the target team what results they need to see from you over the next six months

questions this episode answers

what does matt walsh say companies should define before interviewing candidates?

on guidance counselor 2.0, matt walsh says before interviewing anyone you should write a document defining the five or six key results the role must achieve within a certain time frame, since there's a business reason you're hiring. you also need to list the five or six non-obvious cultural competencies that determine whether someone will thrive specifically in your company's culture, not just any generic startup. this document, which he calls a target, replaces relying only on a job description.

how should candidates handle long pauses from interviewers, according to matt walsh?

on guidance counselor 2.0, matt walsh says most interviewers are terrible at interrupting and will sit silent for three or four minutes, which naturally makes candidates ramble. as a candidate you should prepare a tight 90 second to three minute story and then check in with the interviewer by giving them an executive summary and offering to go deeper on any part they want. this helps them interview you better instead of waiting for them to jump in with follow-up questions.

what does matt walsh recommend for moving to a different team internally?

on guidance counselor 2.0, matt walsh says you need to outperform in your current role first, not just do an okay job. then ask both your current boss and the team you want to join to help you build a scorecard of what results and skills you need to demonstrate over the next six months to make the move. when people author a plan with you and you hit those goals, your chance of success is much higher than just doing good work silently and hoping someone notices.
