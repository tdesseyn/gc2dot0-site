how to break into faang w/ genesis bonds

guest: genesis bonds, software engineer at facebook/meta and director of social engagement for text givin

  • treat bootcamps as a two way investment, look for programs that have real skin in the game for your success
  • write out coding problems by hand on paper since real interviews wont let you use an ide or compiler
  • record yourself answering interview questions to catch filler words and awkward pauses before the real thing
  • study the specific company you are interviewing with since interview styles differ, like data structure complexity questions at google
  • dont let one rejection discourage you, a no from one company just means it wasnt the right fit, not a reflection of your worth

questions this episode answers

what does genesis bonds say about choosing a coding bootcamp?

on guidance counselor 2.0, genesis bonds says you should pick a bootcamp that invests in you as much as you invest in them. she attended revature, which required no upfront tuition, paid for housing and certifications, and had you work as a contractor for two years at a set salary. genesis emphasizes finding programs where the bootcamp only succeeds if you get a job, so they have real skin in the game.

how does genesis bonds recommend preparing for faang coding interviews?

on guidance counselor 2.0, genesis bonds recommends writing out coding problems by hand on paper or in a notepad because you will not have access to an ide or compiler during whiteboard interviews. she also suggests recording yourself answering questions to catch filler words like 'um' and 'like' and to identify awkward silences, which helps you practice thinking out loud. genesis used interviewcake.com to practice hard problems with detailed explanations.

what does genesis bonds say about getting rejected by a faang company?

on guidance counselor 2.0, genesis bonds says not getting a role at one company is not a personal slight and may just mean it was not the right fit for you. she warns that you can only apply to companies like facebook once every six months, so you should not go for big companies before you feel truly prepared. genesis also recommends using interviews at companies you are less interested in as practice runs before applying to your top choices.
