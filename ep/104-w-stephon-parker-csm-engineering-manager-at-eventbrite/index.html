ep 104 · jan 21, 2022

w/stephon parker, csm & engineering manager at eventbrite

guest: stephon parker, csm and engineering manager at eventbrite

  • before hiring junior engineers make sure your team has the structure and mentorship to support them or you risk setting them up to fail
  • leaders can build buy in for hiring junior talent by showing it creates loyalty and grows future staff level and leadership talent from within
  • directors and vps should regularly talk directly with individual contributors instead of relying only on managers to relay information
  • ship work more often in smaller pieces and iterate rather than waiting until everything is finished before releasing it
  • a good performance review or termination should never surprise an employee if the manager has been consistently honest about expectations

questions this episode answers

what does stephon parker say about hiring junior engineers?

on guidance counselor 2.0, stephon parker, csm and engineering manager at eventbrite, says before bringing in junior engineers you must have the right structure, mentorship, and experienced team members ready to support them. he warns that without these elements you risk setting juniors up for failure and driving them out of the field entirely. parker says he's seen people leave tech because their first job lacked the support they needed.

how does stephon parker create buy-in for hiring junior talent?

on guidance counselor 2.0, stephon parker tells leadership that investing early in junior engineers creates loyalty, meaning those employees will stay five, seven, or ten years instead of leaving after six months for more money. he explains these juniors eventually become your staff-level, principal-level engineers and future leaders who are already familiar with your product. because you invested in them, they create that same culture of mentorship with their own teams.

what does stephon parker say about directors and vps talking to individual contributors?

on guidance counselor 2.0, stephon parker says directors and vps should absolutely meet directly with individual contributors, not just rely on managers to relay information. he describes a positive experience where a leader two levels up would spend an hour to an hour and a half monthly with whole teams just chatting. parker says this direct contact makes leadership more human and reachable, and prevents the telephone game where messages get distorted.
