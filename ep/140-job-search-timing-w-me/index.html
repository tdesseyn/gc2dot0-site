ep 140 · may 16, 2022

job search timing w/ me

guest: me

  • avoid walking around with an inflated title like vp of engineering or cto if you only have a few years of experience, it can spook hiring managers
  • if your experience justifies a senior title, explain and sell that context clearly in your resume and to your recruiter
  • ask recruiters or hiring managers directly if a position is budgetarily approved before investing time in the interview process
  • ask how many other people are currently in the interview process so you understand your competition
  • if you get rejected, ask for specific quantifiable feedback instead of vague comments like culture fit so you know what to improve

questions this episode answers

what does taylor desseyn say about having inflated job titles early in your career?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says that walking around with a c-suite title like vp of engineering or cto when you only have a few years of experience will hurt your job search. hiring managers get spooked when they see someone with five years of experience claiming a vp or cto title from a startup, because at the end of the day that might just be a dev lead role. taylor advises changing your title on your resume if you want to land a developer job, or if your experience truly justifies the senior title, explain that context clearly in your resume and sell it to the recruiter.

what question should a job seeker ask a recruiter about budgetary approval according to taylor desseyn?

on guidance counselor 2.0, taylor desseyn says the biggest question to ask a recruiter or hiring manager is whether the position is budgetarily approved. he explains that budgetary approval and interview stages are the two biggest things that torpedo deals quickly. taylor recommends asking when the position was approved, because if it was approved four months ago you should confirm it is still active before investing time in the interview process.

how does taylor desseyn recommend asking for interview feedback after rejection?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn advises asking how many people are in the interview process and how many have been rejected, then requesting general feedback themes so you can improve. he tells hiring managers to give quantifiable feedback like scoring a candidate two out of five on react skills instead of vague comments like culture fit. taylor says detailed technical feedback is appropriate, but he understands hiring managers cannot quantify subjective personality concerns.
