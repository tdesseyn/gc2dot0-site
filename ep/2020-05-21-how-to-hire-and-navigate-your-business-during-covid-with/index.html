how to hire and navigate your business during covid with alex belew

guest: alex belew, chef and owner of dalts and jane in murfreesboro

  • look for people with integrity, self-awareness, and a genuine desire to learn over raw talent or experience
  • give candidates a trial shift so both sides can see if the fit and culture actually work
  • handle performance or culture problems with a calm, private conversation instead of public conflict
  • be honest and transparent with your community and staff about business struggles instead of hiding them
  • stay creative and keep pivoting your offerings when your usual business model stops working

questions this episode answers

what does alex belew look for when hiring restaurant staff?

on guidance counselor 2.0, alex belew says he prioritizes integrity, self-awareness, and a genuine desire to learn over raw talent or experience. he believes you can teach cooking skills but cannot teach work ethic, personality, or natural curiosity. he looks for people who are honest about what they don't know and willing to put in the work to improve, rather than those who oversell their abilities.

what is a stage in restaurant hiring and why does alex belew use it?

on guidance counselor 2.0, alex belew explains that a stage is like an internship or shadow shift where a candidate works in the restaurant for one night before being hired. he uses it so both the employer and candidate can see if the fit and culture work, comparing it to trying on clothes at a fitting room. this trial lets the candidate see if they like the food and environment, while the employer assesses whether the person has the right skills and personality without disrupting the existing team culture.

how does alex belew handle employees who don't fit the restaurant culture?

on guidance counselor 2.0, alex belew says he addresses problems with calm, private conversations rather than yelling or public confrontations. when he had an employee spreading drama and showing a bad attitude, he had two conversations and ended the employment with a talk in the parking lot. he remained calm, gave the person a hug, and offered help while explaining it wasn't the right place for them.
