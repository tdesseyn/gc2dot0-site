ep 180 · oct 17, 2022

so you think you're a senior engineer w/ andrew wyborski

guest: andrew wyborski, engineering manager at kroger

  • separate the question of skill level from title and salary band since every company labels seniority differently
  • senior engineers impact a wider group of teams, not just their own, by setting direction and standards
  • know why you call yourself senior and back it up with how you adapted, mentored, and solved harder problems, not just years of experience
  • keep a running log of your wins in cause, action, result format so review time and resume updates are easy
  • ask hiring managers why a requirement like a degree exists so you can judge if it is a real need or just a checkbox

questions this episode answers

what does andrew wyborski say separates a junior, mid-level, and senior engineer?

on guidance counselor 2.0, andrew wyborski says a junior engineer delivers for themselves and impacts their own area, a mid-level engineer impacts their team and sets standards there, and a senior engineer impacts a wider group of teams beyond just their own. he explains that a senior should set direction and influence multiple teams even if they only lead one directly. the key is expanding the scope of impact, not just writing more code.

how should an engineer prove they are senior level according to andrew wyborski?

on guidance counselor 2.0, andrew wyborski says you need to explain why you call yourself senior beyond just years of experience. talk about how you adapted to new technologies, helped your team become more efficient, mentored others in a way they learned, or put processes in place for technical debt. he says seniors lead and solve problems at a higher level, not just write elegant code or close story points.
