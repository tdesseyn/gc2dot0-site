contracting and contract to hire

with taylor desseyn (solo)

  • know your full compensation package including base, bonus, pto, and 401k match before comparing it to a contract hourly rate
  • ask recruiters to build pto and two weeks of bench pay into long term contracts since they wont offer it unless you ask
  • build relationships and create content around the team or department you want to move into for internal transfers
  • bring up your performance reviews and market research on pay when negotiating a contract to hire conversion
  • being kind, communicative, and reliable on a contract job greatly increases your odds of getting converted to full time

questions this episode answers

how does taylor desseyn say to calculate your contract rate from a full-time salary?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says to divide your annual salary by 2000 hours to get your base hourly rate, then add 10 to 15 dollars per hour to account for benefits like paid time off, 401k match, and other compensation. for a 100k salary, that means 50 dollars per hour base plus 10 dollars extra, totaling 60 dollars per hour or about 120k annually. he emphasizes you must know your full compensation package including bonuses and all benefits before making the comparison.

what does taylor desseyn recommend asking for on long-term contract jobs?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says to ask the recruiter to build paid time off into long-term contracts of a year or more, plus two weeks of bench pay. bench pay keeps you on billing for two weeks between projects when you are not actively working. recruiters will not offer these benefits unless you specifically ask for them, so candidates need to request them directly.

how should someone negotiate a contract-to-hire conversion according to taylor desseyn?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says to drive the conversation yourself and bring up any performance reviews you have received, especially if they were positive. he also recommends doing market research on what people in your role typically earn and mentioning those findings. start having the conversion conversation when you are about two-thirds through your contract term so there are no surprises for either side.
