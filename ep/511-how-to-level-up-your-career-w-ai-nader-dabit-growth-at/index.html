how to level up your career w/ ai + nader dabit, growth at cognition

guest: nader dabit, growth at cognition

  • set aside about a hundred dollars a month to try bleeding edge ai tools instead of boxing yourself into one stack
  • stop thinking of yourself as only a front end or back end developer and build a broader skill set including devops and design
  • if your company blocks these tools build side projects on your own and set aside daily time to experiment

questions this episode answers

what does nader dabit recommend developers spend monthly to stay current with ai tools?

on guidance counselor 2.0, nader dabit from cognition recommends setting aside about a hundred dollars a month to experiment with bleeding edge ai tools. he suggests trying multiple tools like codex, claude code, windsurf and others rather than boxing yourself into one stack. he says to treat it as an investment and skip eating out if needed because staying on the bleeding edge will make you more money long term.

how does nader dabit say developers should expand beyond being just front end or back end?

on guidance counselor 2.0, nader dabit says developers can no longer think of themselves as only a front end or back end developer and need a much broader skill set. he recommends leaning into design skills, learning devops and sre practices, and understanding agent orchestration. he emphasizes that the engineering jobs least likely to be automated right now are cloud engineering, devops, sre roles and machine learning development positions.

what does nader dabit recommend about joining startups versus large companies right now?

on guidance counselor 2.0, nader dabit recommends looking for startups with valuations under one billion dollars that are growing fast rather than joining large legacy companies. he points out that companies like meta are reportedly laying off twenty percent of people while fast growing ai startups are hiring aggressively. he says smaller companies offer better job security right now, faster equity opportunities through acquisitions or going public, and more room for growth compared to legacy companies that are downsizing.
