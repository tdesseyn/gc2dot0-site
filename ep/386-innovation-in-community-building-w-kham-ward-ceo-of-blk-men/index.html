innovation in community building w/kham ward, ceo of blk men in tech

guest: kham ward, ceo of blk men in tech

  • start small when building community, kham ward grew black men in tech from a single zoom call with friends checking in on each other
  • use panels instead of solo talks so attendees hear multiple perspectives on the same topic like resumes or job searching
  • build community outside of work topics first, trust and connection often matter more than technical conversation
  • to improve diversity hiring, build a recruiting team that reflects who you want to hire and create representation at every level, not just top or bottom
  • before asking someone for help or mentorship, show up consistently and add value first, then look for ways to collaborate over time

questions this episode answers

what does kham ward say about starting a community or conference?

on guidance counselor 2.0, kham ward, ceo of black men in tech, says to just do it because there is room for everyone to benefit the culture and community. he started black men in tech with eight people on a zoom call in 2020 simply asking how are you doing, and it grew to 50 people in three weeks and 300 a few months later. ward planned his first conference in four months and gave it away for free because it was about access and opportunity, not money.

what does kham ward recommend for improving diversity hiring?

on guidance counselor 2.0, kham ward says companies should build a recruiting team that reflects the people they want to recruit and create representation layered throughout the organization, not just at the top and bottom but through the hiring process and all levels. he also recommends showing up to community events year round, not just conferences, to build authentic relationships rather than being transactional. ward emphasizes being consistent, inclusive, and showing you care, which helps build a pipeline where people want to work for you.

what does kham ward say about asking for help or mentorship?

on guidance counselor 2.0, kham ward says do not walk up to someone and ask them to be your mentor when you don't even know who they are. instead of playing the short game of what you need right now, focus on the long game by showing up consistently, adding value first, and finding ways to collaborate over time. ward recommends getting on a call to discuss what both parties are doing, finding synergy, and starting with something light within six months to build the relationship before making a big ask.
