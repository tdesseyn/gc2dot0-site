talent marketplaces - what are they? - solo show

with taylor desseyn (solo)

  • a talent marketplace gives you access to a pool of already vetted talent you can pick from instead of sourcing from scratch like a staffing agency does
  • talent marketplaces mainly serve senior level roles while staffing agencies are more likely to place junior developers too
  • ask any staffing recruiter if they are showing you every open role or only the ones that are fastest and easiest for them to fill
  • when picking an agency focus on finding a good individual recruiter you vibe with rather than judging the company as a whole
  • fix your resume formatting first since font choices bold text and font size issues cause more rejections than the actual content

questions this episode answers

what does taylor desseyn say is the main difference between a talent marketplace and a staffing agency?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says a talent marketplace has already vetted developers and other talent available on tap that you can pick from immediately. a staffing agency typically has to go out and find that talent when you call them, even if they have high volume needs. talent marketplaces also have technical experts who vet the talent, while most staffing agencies do not have former ctos or vps of engineering doing technical screening.

what does taylor desseyn say to ask a staffing recruiter before working with them?

on guidance counselor 2.0, taylor desseyn says you should ask a staffing recruiter if they are showing you all the opportunities or holding anything back. most staffing companies only show you the roles they can make the most money on and move the fastest, rather than every role that could fit you. he tried to make sure his teams never did that, but admits that is how staffing agencies typically work.

what does taylor desseyn say is the biggest resume problem he sees?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says fifty percent of resumes that are bad fail because of formatting issues, not content. the most consistent problems he sees are poor formatting, wrong font choices, too many bold words, inconsistent font sizes and too many words overall. he reviews resumes daily and finds formatting kills more applications than the actual content does.
