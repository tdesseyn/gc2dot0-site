ep 478 · aug 27, 2025

from yoga teacher to director of engineering — harjis deksnis’ nonlinear tech journey

guest: harjis deksnis, director of engineering

  • your career path does not need to be a straight line from school to a tech job to succeed
  • genuine curiosity about how things work matters more than years of experience when applying for roles
  • being able to learn fast and keep up with new tools matters more than already knowing everything for the role
  • when interviewing, turn short answers into a real conversation and ask the interviewer what kind of depth they want
  • go to industry conferences even if you don't learn much on the spot, since the inspiration and connections pay off later

questions this episode answers

what does harjis deksnis say matters more than years of experience when hiring junior developers?

on guidance counselor 2.0, harjis deksnis says genuine curiosity and interest in how things work matter more than experience when he evaluates junior candidates. he looks for people who tinker with things at home, whether building minecraft mods or trying 3d work, because that spark shows they will figure out what they need to learn. he hired someone with little web experience but a clear curiosity about technology, and that person succeeded in the role.

how does harjis deksnis suggest candidates handle short interview answers?

on guidance counselor 2.0, harjis deksnis advises candidates to turn short answers into real conversations by asking the interviewer what they want. he says it is totally fair to ask how long an answer should be or how deep the interviewer wants you to go on a topic. this helps you understand what the person across the table is looking for and build better rapport during the interview.

what does harjis deksnis say is the real benefit of attending tech conferences?

on guidance counselor 2.0, harjis deksnis says the biggest benefit of conferences is inspiration, not immediate learning. he does not expect to learn something directly from talks because there is too much information, but conferences inspire him to pick up ideas and do his own research afterward. after attending a vue conference in 2018, he spent a weekend learning vue on his own, which is how he actually learned the framework.
