what does recruiting look like post covid-19

guest: chase tinkham, business development at vaco, virginia beach office

  • treat recruiting like a profession and position yourself as a market labor expert instead of apologizing for being a recruiter
  • a video call already builds more connection than a phone call so use that as your starting advantage
  • keep a running list of everyone you talk to and send a regular check in email so people know you did not forget them
  • post one thoughtful piece of content a day so you stay top of mind when candidates are ready to move
  • be upfront when you have nothing to offer but still help with resume or job search advice so the relationship outlasts any single role

questions this episode answers

what does chase tinkham say about building relationships on short zoom calls during remote recruiting?

on guidance counselor 2.0, chase tinkham from vaco says recruiters worry too much about building relationships in seven-minute zoom calls, but the fact that you're doing video already puts you one step ahead of phone-only recruiters. he says the key is consistent follow-up rather than trying to accomplish everything in one call. chase created a bullhorn tear sheet of everyone he talks to and sends a check-in email every other week to show candidates he hasn't forgotten them.

how does taylor desseyn suggest recruiters position themselves instead of apologizing?

on guidance counselor 2.0, taylor desseyn says recruiters need to stop acting like lowly salespeople and instead position themselves as labor market experts. he points out that recruiters see the inner workings of thousands of companies throughout their careers while normal people only see six or seven, giving recruiters unique market knowledge. taylor says recruiters should confidently tell candidates they want a five-year relationship, not just a transactional placement, and offer value through resume reviews and introductions even when they have no immediate job opening.

what does taylor desseyn recommend for staying top of mind with candidates during covid?

on guidance counselor 2.0, taylor desseyn says recruiters need to post one thoughtful piece of content per day on linkedin to stay visible to their network. he also recommends using multiple communication channels including text messaging, email newsletters, and social media to stay top of mind with thousands of people. taylor says he created a text messaging group with about 80 people who receive updates three to five times per week, and he emphasizes that depth matters more than reach when it comes to these touch points.
