ep 442 · apr 16, 2025

building a network & betting on yourself w/ co-founder dee murthy

guest: dee murthy, co-founder of ghost

  • build your network slowly over years by consistently showing up, not by expecting instant payoff from one meeting
  • at conferences introduce yourself, make a quick impression, and move on instead of over-pitching someone
  • when you lose out on a role, how you respond matters since companies may circle back when timing is better
  • do your homework before interviews and be easy to work with during scheduling, since friction signals how you will be to manage
  • treat failure as a learning tool by taking accountability instead of blaming others for what went wrong

questions this episode answers

what does dee murthy say about networking at conferences?

on guidance counselor 2.0, dee murthy says introduce yourself, make a quick impression, and then move on instead of having long conversations. he believes people try to talk too much at conferences when the goal should be to leave an impression without being a burden. murthy recommends reading the room and not getting stuck with one person, since you don't know them well enough yet for deep conversation.

how does dee murthy handle candidates who don't get hired right away?

on guidance counselor 2.0, dee murthy says how a candidate responds when told they are not the right fit right now matters greatly because the company may circle back later. he explains that keeping the door open with a good response leaves you in a pile of resumes for future roles. murthy gives an example of a data scientist who left early in the company but maintained good rapport and is now being considered again because the timing is better.

what does dee murthy look for when hiring for a startup?

on guidance counselor 2.0, dee murthy says he looks for candidates who have worked at companies during growth stages, not just at mature companies. he explains that it matters when someone worked at a company like uber, whether they were there figuring out manual processes or just tweaking automated systems. murthy also says doing your homework before interviews and being easy to schedule with are basics, because if scheduling is painful the company will never hire you.
