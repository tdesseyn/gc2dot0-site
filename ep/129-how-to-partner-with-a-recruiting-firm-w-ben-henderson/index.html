how to partner with a recruiting firm w/ ben henderson

guest: ben henderson, vp of software engineering at celera health

  • interview your recruiting partner like a hire, make sure they understand what you actually need beyond the job description
  • job descriptions should sell the role and team honestly, including real tech stack and work intensity, not just a bulleted list
  • being upfront about challenges and technical debt during interviews builds trust and reduces early quits
  • mix direct hire, contract, contract-to-hire and part-time talent instead of relying on one hiring model
  • carve out projects that do not require full domain expertise so contractors can ramp up fast without slowing the core team

questions this episode answers

what does ben henderson say about interviewing a recruiting partner?

on guidance counselor 2.0, ben henderson says you have to interview your recruiting partner just like you would a hire. not all recruiters are created equal, so you need to find someone who really listens to what you are looking for beyond the job description and understands the key points about your team and culture. the right partner will dig into details and know exactly what drives you so they can send the right candidates.

how does ben henderson write job descriptions to attract candidates?

on guidance counselor 2.0, ben henderson says job descriptions should not be standard bulleted lists anymore. you need to sell the position by being transparent about what the team is really like, including the real tech stack you use like typescript and azure functions, and the work intensity. he also recommends being upfront about technical debt and challenges during interviews because it builds trust and prevents candidates from leaving early when reality does not match expectations.

what does ben henderson say about using contractors versus direct hires?

on guidance counselor 2.0, ben henderson says hiring managers should mix direct hire, contract, contract to hire and part time talent instead of relying on one model. you need direct hires for domain expertise and retention, but contractors can start producing immediately on projects that do not require full domain knowledge. he brought on senior part time contractors who were ramped up and writing code in less than two weeks, which let his team hit year end deliverables without slowing down core staff.
