questions this episode answers

what does julian dasilva say about choosing your first coding project?

on guidance counselor 2.0, julian dasilva, senior software engineering manager, says to tie your first coding project to something you already care about so the lessons stick better. he explains that after you complete a basic tutorial or reddit clone to build a foundation, you should move on to projects that matter to you personally—whether that's tennis, sports stats, gardening, or helping your dad with construction invoices. the practical application makes you more willing to stumble through the learning process.

how does julian dasilva recommend balancing cold applications and networking?

on guidance counselor 2.0, julian dasilva says cold applying still works but you should spend most of your job search time networking instead of only sending applications. he recommends sitting down for an hour or two every day to send cold applications with tailored resumes and cover letters, but spending the majority of your four or five hours a day on networking, reaching out to people, and learning things that set you apart. he notes that a career coach friend told him seventy percent of jobs are now filled by referrals.

what does julian dasilva say about standing out in interviews as a junior or mid-level engineer?

on guidance counselor 2.0, julian dasilva says the biggest thing during an interview is to set your ego aside and focus on being coachable. he explains that he has passed on exceptional candidates from great companies because they came off as too cocky and unpleasant to work with. he emphasizes that the peer interview matters most because those people work with you every day, and their opinion is extremely important to him since he can teach technical skills but cannot teach someone how to be a good person.
