how content can land you a job w/ chantastic

guest: chantastic, developer educator at workos

  • document a layoff publicly if it feels right, since being open and helpful can resonate well beyond your immediate circle
  • treat content as a trust-building exercise, not a highlight reel, since people hire who they trust
  • making a specific video for a company you want to work for can get you noticed faster than a standard application
  • it is okay to tell an interviewer a role is not the right fit for you, even at a company like netflix
  • if you have any interest in video editing, pursue it, since developer content and storytelling skills are increasingly valuable in tech careers

questions this episode answers

what does chantastic say about making content during a layoff?

on guidance counselor 2.0, michael chan (chantastic) says documenting his layoff publicly through video content helped him build trust with potential employers. he explains that content serves as a trust-building machine rather than a highlight reel, and that workos hired him after seeing his layoff videos because they demonstrated he could create video content end-to-end. the content allowed him to have honest conversations and show his ability to take initiative without waiting for direction.

how did chantastic get an interview at netflix using video?

on guidance counselor 2.0, michael chan (chantastic) says he made a video specifically for netflix about why he would be a good fit for a team, then posted it publicly. the video got enough attention and shares that netflix recruiters noticed, and he got an interview even though he would not have otherwise. during the interview he realized the role was not a good fit and told the recruiter so, which had never happened to them before.

what does chantastic say about video editing skills for developers?

on guidance counselor 2.0, michael chan (chantastic) says that if you have any interest in video editing you should pursue it, because his current developer educator role is about ninety percent editing work. taylor and chantastic agree that developers who can edit video may find jobs in developer marketing faster than traditional development roles, especially since companies are moving away from traditional marketing. the ability to tell stories through video is increasingly valuable for technical roles.
