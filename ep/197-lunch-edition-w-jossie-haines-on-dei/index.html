questions this episode answers

what does jossie haines say about diversity versus inclusion in tech hiring?

on guidance counselor 2.0, jossie haines says companies focus too much on the d in dei because it's easy to measure, but that creates a revolving door. she explains that 37 percent of people leave tech due to unfairness and 57 percent of those would have stayed if concerns were addressed. she urges companies to build inclusion and fair management practices alongside diverse hiring, or they will keep losing the talent they recruit.

> source for the 37 percent figure: kapor center tech leavers study, 2017

how does jossie haines recommend writing job descriptions to be more inclusive?

on guidance counselor 2.0, jossie haines recommends reviewing job descriptions for gendered words like aggressive or rock star that can turn off women applicants. she suggests using tools like textio or the free gender decoder to check language. most importantly, she advocates for impact based job descriptions that list 30, 60 and 90 day goals instead of long requirement lists, which help candidates picture themselves in the role and reduce bias throughout hiring and onboarding.

> source for the 100 percent figure: harvard business review, 2014

what does jossie haines say about structured interview feedback and scorecards?

on guidance counselor 2.0, jossie haines says most tech interview feedback forms that ask for a one to four rating and what you liked or disliked are full of bias. instead, she recommends creating a scorecard based on specific competencies from the job description, with defined questions for each competency and clear examples of what good and bad answers look like. this approach evaluates candidates on concrete skills rather than likeability bias, where people favor candidates similar to themselves.
