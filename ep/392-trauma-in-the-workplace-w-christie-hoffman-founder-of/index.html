trauma in the workplace w/ christie hoffman, founder of engagement academy

guest: christie hoffman, founder of engagement academy

  • figure out your personal values so you have a framework for decisions instead of relying on a company that may not have any
  • generic recognition like saying good job means little, teach managers to explain the specific impact an employee's work actually had
  • burnout often comes from feeling you have no control over your goals, so set personal goals outside of work to regain a sense of progress
  • pay attention to how you react when stressed or angry and ask if it mirrors a parent or caretaker, that awareness can change bad leadership habits
  • small daily habits like walking, joining a gym even without a plan, and watching nutrition can meaningfully reduce anxiety and burnout

questions this episode answers

what does christie hoffman say about using company values to make career decisions?

on guidance counselor 2.0, christie hoffman says you need to figure out your personal values to create a framework for decisions, especially if your company doesn't operate by clear values. she explains that when a company isn't living by values that guide how to treat people, set boundaries, or say no, you have to create your own scaffolding. having personal values kept her from situations where she felt taken advantage of and helped her know immediately how to handle boundary violations.

how should managers give recognition according to christie hoffman?

on guidance counselor 2.0, christie hoffman says recognition needs to be specific and explain the actual impact, not just generic praise like good job. she explains that saying thanks for being great at your job is patronizing and means nothing. managers should be trained to give recognition that is specific, timely, tied to a company value, explains how the work went above and beyond, and describes the actual impact on the customer or team so the employee understands why their work mattered.

what does christie hoffman recommend for dealing with burnout at work?

on guidance counselor 2.0, christie hoffman says burnout is a symptom of not feeling in control of your destiny, so you should set personal goals outside of work to regain that sense of control. she explains that company goals are often messy and out of your control, but personal goals for fitness, skills, or the type of person you want to become give you a sense of progress. she also recommends paying attention to how you react when stressed or angry and asking if it reminds you of a parent or caretaker, because that awareness can help you change bad leadership habits.
