how to navigate job postings

with taylor desseyn (solo)

  • before submitting your resume to a job posting, try bcc'ing recruiter contacts and asking if they have connections or are working that role
  • if you lack contacts at a company, go ahead and submit your resume but also reach out to the hr manager or recruiter to ask about next steps
  • remote policies keep shifting, so apply to onsite roles in a city you want to move to and use a remote contract period to slowly research and relocate
  • job descriptions are often stuffed with keywords by hr, so only apply within your actual years of experience and skill level instead of chasing every listed requirement
  • spending an hour messaging people at companies with open roles beats spending that same hour mass submitting resumes around town

questions this episode answers

what does taylor desseyn say about submitting resumes to job postings?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says you should avoid submitting your resume directly to job postings without making human contact first. if you see a job posting, bcc recruiter friends with the posting and ask if they have connections at that company or are working on the role. if you have no contacts at the company, submit your resume but also reach out to hr managers or recruiters to ask about next steps and get on their radar.

what does taylor desseyn say about which job postings to apply to?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says to only submit your resume to job postings within your actual years of experience and skill level. if you are junior level, apply only to junior or associate roles, not architect level positions. he explains that hr stuffs job descriptions with keywords to attract applicants, but that does not mean you need every listed skill, just the core requirement and the right experience level.

what does taylor desseyn say about spending time on job applications versus messaging people?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says that spending one hour messaging people at companies with open roles is more effective than spending that same hour mass submitting your resume around town. he acknowledges that reaching out to individuals takes more time, but it leads to better success in making contact and getting a job than blind resume submissions.
