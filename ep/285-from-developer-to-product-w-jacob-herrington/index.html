ep 285 · oct 13, 2023

from developer to product w/jacob herrington

guest: jacob herrington, product manager, formerly engineer at startups including commsor, now at walmart

  • switching from engineering to product doesn't require giving up strong compensation since product roles can pay just as well
  • if you work closely with engineers, learn the basics of code so you can empathize and communicate about technical tradeoffs
  • to validate an idea, show people a concept and watch their emotional reaction instead of directly asking if it has value
  • moving from a startup to a large company means adjusting your sense of scale since small decisions can carry much bigger consequences
  • learn to identify where value is created in your company and connect your own work directly to that value

questions this episode answers

what does jacob herrington say about compensation when switching from engineering to product?

on guidance counselor 2.0, jacob herrington says engineers get paid really well and product managers also get paid really well, so there is not a huge sacrifice in compensation if you feel burnt out on engineering. he explains that product is a place where you can go and not necessarily give up that high compensation. there are other well-paying roles like data science and ai, but product made more sense for him because strategy is where his head is at.

does jacob herrington think product managers need to know how to code?

on guidance counselor 2.0, jacob herrington says that if you work with engineers you owe it to yourself and them to go do a code academy course or something similar. you should know what a variable is and understand what it means when a programmer says the computer said no so you can empathize and communicate about technical tradeoffs. however, he does not think you need to be technical for the actual job duties of a product manager.

how does jacob herrington say you should validate whether a product idea has value?

on guidance counselor 2.0, jacob herrington recommends putting a concept in front of people in whatever form you can and talking about it, whether it is a sheet of paper describing a wireframe or something else. he says you should not ask directly if it has value because people will not want to hurt your feelings, but instead ask how it would affect their life if this was their new screen. you should gauge their emotional response because people will get excited about value.
