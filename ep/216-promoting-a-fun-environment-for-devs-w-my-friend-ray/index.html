ep 216 · feb 6, 2023

promoting a fun environment for devs w/my friend ray morrison

guest: ray morrison, engineer at oc tanner

  • ask for autonomy in your first weeks so you can research and learn the stack without pressure to perform instantly
  • expect to only know about half the tech stack in a new role, so plan time to fill the gaps
  • use a buddy or pair programming on your first few tickets to learn the codebase before working solo
  • be upfront about your story point comfort level so managers do not overload you early on
  • ask if your company will cover a course or certification tied to your bonus or growth goals

questions this episode answers

what does ray morrison say about learning the tech stack in your first dev job?

on guidance counselor 2.0, ray morrison says you probably only know about half the tech stack when you get hired for any role. he was familiar with react, typescript and material ui but had to learn graphql and apollo on the job. he took courses in those areas and focused on understanding which tooling came from which package so he wouldn't sound confused when talking to senior developers.

how does ray morrison recommend handling your first tickets as a junior developer?

on guidance counselor 2.0, ray morrison recommends asking someone to sit in a zoom call for your first ticket or two in a new repository. he drove the coding while a teammate watched his screen share and explained the file structure, what props were passed where, and how things worked. he says being honest about your story point comfort level is critical so managers don't overload you early on.

what does ray morrison say about asking your company to pay for courses?

on guidance counselor 2.0, ray morrison says he submitted a request for a four hundred dollar css for javascript developers course and his company approved it within a day or two. he then made completing the first four modules a quarterly goal that counted toward his bonus metrics, which his manager agreed was a great idea.
