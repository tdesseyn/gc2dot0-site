investing in your career w/amy dutton who is the director of design at zeal

guest: amy dutton, director of design at zeal

  • build things and do real work because real bugs and problems are what push your skills forward
  • try open source by looking for issues labeled best first issue in well maintained repos on github
  • content you make is for others to discover you, not for you, so do not judge it by your own taste
  • learning how to learn matters more than any single framework since it lets you pick up new tools faster
  • network by genuinely building relationships rather than treating it as a sleazy transactional activity

questions this episode answers

what does amy dutton recommend for someone new to open source contributions?

on guidance counselor 2.0, amy dutton says to look for repositories on github with issues labeled best first issue in well-maintained projects. these issues typically include both the problem and how to solve it, so maintainers just need someone to do the work. she also mentions brian douglas's project opensauced, which connects junior developers and designers with senior people willing to help.

how did content creation help amy dutton get speaking opportunities and her podcast?

on guidance counselor 2.0, amy dutton says her youtube channel led to opportunities to travel around the world speaking at conferences, even though she had not posted in a year. james quick saw her channel and invited her to be on his live stream, which led to them starting the compressed fm podcast together. she also notes that zeal hired her after reading her blog because they could see how she thinks about projects and knew there was alignment.
