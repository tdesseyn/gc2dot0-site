from engineer to product and how to get into tech w/adam murray who is a sr. product manager at cloudflare

guest: adam murray, senior product manager at cloudflare

  • try moving from engineering to product by flexing product skills on a side project before making a full switch
  • get involved in open source to practice working with a community the way product managers work with customers
  • do not limit your tech job search to developer roles, consider product, project management, qa, customer success or business analyst paths
  • be careful accepting inflated titles early since you may later have to walk them back
  • in interviews it is fine to admit you do not know something rather than giving a long answer to dodge it

questions this episode answers

what does adam murray suggest for engineers who want to transition to product management?

on guidance counselor 2.0, adam murray says engineers should flex their product muscles on a side project before making the full switch to product management. he also recommends getting involved in open source projects because working with the community teaches you how to interact the way product managers work with customers. he tried both approaches and found they helped him develop product thinking while still building things.

what tech roles should someone consider besides software developer, according to adam murray?

on guidance counselor 2.0, adam murray tells job seekers not to limit themselves to developer roles when entering tech. he lists product management, program management, project management, customer success engineering, quality assurance, site reliability, devops, marketing and sales as viable paths. adam and taylor agree that business analyst is another strong option that is technical but still involves working with people.

what does adam murray say about accepting inflated job titles too early in your career?

on guidance counselor 2.0, adam murray warns people to be very careful with titles because he has seen people get titles like senior before they are actually ready, especially in startups. he says these premature titles can hurt people later because they may have to walk the title back, and no one wants to tell a future employer they need to drop from senior to mid-level. adam advises being realistic about where you are in your career progression.
