ep 363 · may 6, 2024

tips on how to sell yourself w/ michael hale

guest: michael hale, project manager, office of mobility innovation, city of detroit

  • figure out what you are genuinely good at and lead with that instead of copying someone else's style
  • set a clear goal before walking into a networking event so you know who you are trying to meet
  • let your personal style show up consistently since people remember details like shoes or glasses and it starts conversations
  • find common ground with people by asking about something small they have, like an item or interest, to disarm them
  • after a layoff, take a moment to ground yourself, then reach out to your existing network before searching for new contacts

questions this episode answers

what does michael hale say about finding your style when networking?

on guidance counselor 2.0, michael hale says you should show up as 100 percent yourself, not a version you think people will like. he wears jordan sneakers to professional events because that's what makes him comfortable and perform best, and people remember him for it. he explains that whatever you feel most comfortable in is where you'll show up and perform best, though you still need to be mindful of the environment.

what does michael hale recommend doing first after a layoff?

on guidance counselor 2.0, michael hale says to first take a moment to ground yourself and remember you're still a person regardless of your job status. then look around at who you already know and reach out to existing connections who believe in your work before searching for new contacts. he suggests focusing on allies you've already sold yourself to, and emphasizes that sometimes a layoff gives you a chance to consider fractional work, partnering with someone, or pursuing a project you've been putting off.

how does michael hale say you can disarm people when selling yourself?

on guidance counselor 2.0, michael hale says you disarm people by finding common ground through small details like their keychain, shoes, or glasses. he learned this working at best buy, where he would notice something small about a customer and ask about it to start a conversation. this approach makes people feel like you're not trying to take something from them but are genuinely interested, which opens them up.
