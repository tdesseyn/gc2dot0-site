ep 253 · jul 31, 2023

new beginnings and how to scale w/ ryan burgess

guest: ryan burgess, software engineering manager at netflix

  • when building a new team from existing people, invest the first few months in understanding how they work and what problems they see before proposing solutions
  • pitch new internal projects by writing up the problem and proposal in a doc and asking for input instead of presenting it as already decided
  • demonstrate the value of an idea by doing the work on the side before asking for permission to lead it
  • when a company reorganizes, ask your manager and even skip level leaders why it happened and how you can help, since context matters more than control
  • to judge if a company is scaling responsibly, ask whether an open role is a backfill and why the previous person left

questions this episode answers

what does ryan burgess recommend when taking over a new team built from existing engineers?

on guidance counselor 2.0, ryan burgess says to spend the first few months investigating and learning rather than proposing solutions immediately. he recommends understanding how team members like to work, what problems they see on the ground, and what has worked historically before declaring you have all the answers. nobody likes having someone come in and tell them they have everything solved, because iteration is necessary.

how should you pitch a new internal project or team at your company, according to ryan burgess?

on guidance counselor 2.0, ryan burgess recommends writing a document that explains the problem you see, your proposal, and the pros and cons, then getting input rather than presenting it as a done deal. he suggests keeping people out of the initial pitch and focusing on the problem itself, asking whether others see the opportunity and if a dedicated team would be feasible. get asynchronous comments through a shared doc before scheduling meetings.

what interview questions help determine if a company is hiring responsibly, according to ryan burgess and taylor desseyn?

on guidance counselor 2.0, ryan burgess and taylor desseyn recommend asking if the position is a backfill and, if so, why the previous person left. ryan adds that you should ask why the company is hiring and whether they have gone through layoffs recently. understanding whether someone left for an exciting new opportunity versus other reasons reveals a lot about the role and company stability.
