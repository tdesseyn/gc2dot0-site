ep 178 · oct 12, 2022

better ways for evaluating job seekers w/ josh cypher

guest: josh cypher, senior devops engineer at sonos

  • ask hiring managers for a technical screen instead of a blind code challenge if that format doesn't play to your strengths
  • remember you are interviewing the company just as much as they are interviewing you
  • ask tough questions like what is the top reason you shouldn't work here to expose potential red flags
  • when asking about compensation, get a salary range and ask what drives the top and bottom of that band
  • lean into your non-software background and personal experiences since they can set you apart in interviews

questions this episode answers

what does josh cypher say about asking for a technical screen instead of a code challenge?

on guidance counselor 2.0, josh cypher says if blind code challenges don't play to your strengths, you can simply ask the company for a one-on-one technical interview instead. he explains that many companies are open to this request and that he personally prefers conversational technical screens where he can demonstrate aptitude rather than robotic coding tests. he found that plenty of opportunities exist where candidates can be evaluated in ways more comfortable to them.

what does josh cypher recommend asking about salary ranges during interviews?

on guidance counselor 2.0, josh cypher recommends asking companies for their salary range and then asking what drives the top and bottom of that band. if they give you an extremely wide range, have them explain what factors determine where someone falls within it. if they cannot explain their own compensation structure, that may be a red flag that they don't understand how their benefits work.

what tough question does josh cypher suggest asking to expose company red flags?

on guidance counselor 2.0, josh cypher and taylor desseyn discuss asking what is the top reason you should not work here to expose toxic environments or structural problems. josh adds that you can also ask what is the biggest thing that gets in your way of being successful here. he emphasizes that you are interviewing the company just as much as they are interviewing you, so asking hard questions helps you understand what you are walking into.
