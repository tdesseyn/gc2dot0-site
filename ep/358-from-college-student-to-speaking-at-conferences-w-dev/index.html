from college student to speaking at conferences w/ dev agrawal dev advocate

guest: dev agrawal, dev advocate

  • look for student organizations and university funding to get to conferences you otherwise could not afford
  • volunteering at conferences gets you access to speaker dinners and parties that regular attendees never see
  • post your projects publicly and tell people about them instead of just building quietly in the shadows
  • submitting a conference talk proposal before the talk is finished can be treated as a chance to learn the topic
  • reach out directly when a company shows a product demo you are genuinely excited about, it can lead to a job

questions this episode answers

how does dev agrawal recommend college students afford conferences they can't pay for themselves?

on guidance counselor 2.0, dev agrawal says to look for student organization funding at your university to buy conference tickets. he figured out the university funding process and his student organization got money to send three or four students to react miami who otherwise could not have afforded it. one student who attended react miami through this funding later landed an internship from connections made at the conference.

why does dev agrawal say volunteering at conferences is better for networking than just attending?

on guidance counselor 2.0, dev agrawal explains that volunteering at conferences gives you access to speaker dinners, parties and after-hours hangouts that regular attendees never see. as a volunteer you get to meet speakers who are the cream of the crop, and the barrier is lower than being a speaker yourself. helping out also leaves a good impression because conferences require so much work to run well.

how did dev agrawal land his developer relations job at clerk?

on guidance counselor 2.0, dev agrawal says he attended a ten minute demo of clerk at jamstack con in san francisco and was so impressed he tweeted about it and told the ceo about features he wanted. the clerk devrel person had already seen dev's youtube videos and knew his content style. when dev tweeted about leaving his job in december, the devrel replied offering to chat, which led to a fifteen minute call and then a job offer.
