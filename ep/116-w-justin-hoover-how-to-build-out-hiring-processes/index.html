ep 116 · feb 21, 2022

w/justin hoover + how to build out hiring processes

guest: justin hoover, it professional at alliance bernstein

  • skip pure computer science whiteboard challenges since they rarely reflect real day to day work and add undue stress
  • when screening candidates look for three tells: do they relieve stress, do they show perseverance, and do they show a spark of brilliance in solving new problems
  • pair program with candidates on a realistic business problem instead of arbitrary algorithm puzzles to see how you actually collaborate
  • have a small cross section of the team interview together so they can build on each others questions and judge culture fit
  • be transparent with candidates at every step since a cheap or dishonest interview process damages your reputation and scares off good candidates

questions this episode answers

what does justin hoover say about computer science whiteboard challenges in interviews?

on guidance counselor 2.0, justin hoover says whiteboarding computer science challenges are not beneficial or representative of what people do in their day to day job. he finds them to be gatekeeping that creates undue stress and can lead to monoculture by having hiring managers just look for more of themselves. he has used pure computer science in his career maybe twice in 21 years, one time completely arbitrary and more math-focused.

what three tells does justin hoover look for when screening candidates?

on guidance counselor 2.0, justin hoover says he looks for three tells during phone screens. first, will this person relieve stress or add stress, meaning do they seem capable of doing what they claim. second, do they show perseverance when facing challenges or do they throw their hands up and quit. third, he looks for a spark of brilliance in how they decompose problems they have never seen before into smaller solvable pieces.

what does justin hoover recommend instead of whiteboard interviews?

on guidance counselor 2.0, justin hoover recommends pair programming on a business realistic problem instead of whiteboard challenges. he wants to sit down and work through a realistic problem with the candidate to see how they collaborate, take feedback, and solve problems together, just like they would in the actual job. he includes other team members in this step so it is not just him evaluating.
