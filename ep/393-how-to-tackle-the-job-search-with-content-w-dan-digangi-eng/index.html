ep 393 · aug 2, 2024

how to tackle the job search with content w/ dan digangi, eng manager

guest: dan digangi, engineering manager at postmark (active campaign)

  • during a layoff, double or triple your usual job search effort since timelines are running much longer than before
  • inmail messages tend to feel spammy, so add a short direct note when sending a connection request instead
  • when networking goes cold, try reaching out to people outside your immediate function like sales or product, not just engineering peers
  • build a personal story bank of specific project details and accomplishments so you aren't caught off guard in interviews
  • posting honest content about your layoff and job search can create unexpected connections, so put yourself out there even imperfectly

questions this episode answers

what does dan digangi say about how long a job search takes right now?

on guidance counselor 2.0, dan digangi says if you think a job search used to take three months, you should expect it to take at least double or triple that right now. he easily recommends anyone budget a minimum of six to nine months even if they have their act together, because the timelines are just running longer. he also notes you need to double or triple your effort, multiplying the amount of activity you did before.

what does dan digangi recommend for linkedin connection requests versus inmails?

on guidance counselor 2.0, dan digangi says he will die on the hill that inmails are just very low value products from linkedin and do not work very well. instead, when sending a connection request, he recommends using the add note feature to write a very short, direct message of around 200 characters. he prefers asking directly about a job rather than asking to learn more, and he always keeps messages succinct instead of writing long inmails.

how did dan digangi expand his networking during his job search?

on guidance counselor 2.0, dan digangi says he tried reaching out to people outside of just the engineering function, picking three to five people across different parts of the business like sales, product and management. for example, when targeting home depot, he reached out to people in sales, engineering, product and a couple of bosses. he found that going to other parts of the organization, even as a cold connection, sometimes worked better than only reaching out to engineering peers.
