ep 309 · dec 18, 2023

hiring culture and breaking into tech w/ branden thornton

guest: branden thornton, designer at uber, founder of project nine

  • treat any job as tech adjacent since every industry has the same functions tech companies have, like marketing, finance, and engineering
  • design is judged on what you can show, not just what you can do, so build a strong portfolio with real case studies
  • instead of redesigning a popular app with no context, pick a real problem you notice and design a solution with a full case study
  • networking and referrals matter more than blind applications when trying to break into big tech companies
  • keep making genuine conversations and collecting contacts wherever you work now, since you never know where those relationships lead

questions this episode answers

what does branden thornton say about building a design portfolio to break into tech?

on guidance counselor 2.0, branden thornton says design boot camps often only teach tools like figma but don't prepare people for real company work. instead of redesigning popular apps like netflix or spotify with no context, he recommends picking a real problem you notice in an existing product and designing a solution with a full case study that explains your design decisions and process. companies want to see not just great designs but your ability to explain why you made each choice.

how did branden thornton get hired at twitter and uber?

on guidance counselor 2.0, branden thornton says he applied blindly to twitter twice and got rejected both times. he eventually met the head of design at a conference in atlanta, had a conversation, connected with other designers, and a director reached out saying his work was great and underrated. he emphasizes that networking and referrals go much further than blind applications, and once you have one big tech name on your resume, recruiters constantly reach out.

what does branden thornton say about working outside tech when trying to break into tech?

on guidance counselor 2.0, branden thornton says no job is truly far from tech because every industry has the same functions tech companies have, like marketing, finance, accounting, engineering and design. he started at an apple store, which let him work with products daily, teach creative classes, and have conversations with customers including a startup founder who eventually hired him. he says any experience translates to tech because tech is just a mindset, and startups especially want people who are hungry and passionate.
