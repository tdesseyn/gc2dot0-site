ep 29 · mar 10, 2021

chat w/webflow's quality engineer manager - josh cypher - on hiring

guest: josh cypher, quality engineering manager at webflow

  • global remote hiring means you compete on pay with candidates everywhere, not just your local market
  • location based pay tiers can lose you good candidates once remote work becomes common instead of rare
  • before planning a return to office ask if the job truly requires a body in a seat every day
  • think about succession planning for hires who may only stay a short time during uncertain job markets
  • in interviews explain your reasoning out loud since communicating how you think matters as much as the right answer

questions this episode answers

what does josh cypher say about location-based pay for remote workers?

on guidance counselor 2.0, josh cypher, quality engineering manager at webflow, says location-based compensation tiers worked when remote work was scarce, but now that remote is common you compete globally. he lost a great candidate in rural wisconsin because the tier four pay was 30 to 40 percent lower and simply not competitive with what other remote companies offer. he suggests companies consider offering a single global compensation package instead of tiers.

what does josh cypher recommend asking before planning a return to office?

on guidance counselor 2.0, josh cypher says companies planning a return to office should ask whether the business genuinely needs every person to have a body in a seat. he notes that for 95 percent of startup companies with a digital-only product, spending fifty thousand dollars a month on office space to make people drive half the day is frustrating and unnecessary. forcing a return could be a major attrition risk.

what does josh cypher say about explaining your reasoning in interviews?

on guidance counselor 2.0, josh cypher says candidates need to explain their reasoning and thinking process during interviews because communication is harder to teach than coding. he can teach someone how to code in the company's style, but he needs to see how they reason and articulate their thoughts. he writes down every question a candidate asks because the quality of questions shows how they think.
