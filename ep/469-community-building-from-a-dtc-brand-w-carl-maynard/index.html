community building from a dtc brand w/ carl maynard

guest: carl maynard, lead of creative and community at freaks of nature

  • join communities before you try to build one so you understand how to show up for people
  • the best community leaders stay willing to be the least important person in the room and skip the credit
  • consistency with the same people over time matters more than showing up once and moving on
  • community cant be measured by a clean roi so dont force a data point onto every event
  • let the brand or product sit in the background of the story instead of making it the main focus

questions this episode answers

what does carl maynard say about building community for a brand?

on guidance counselor 2.0, carl maynard says it's important to join communities before you try to create one so you understand how to show up responsibly. he emphasizes consistency with the same people and events over time rather than showing up once for content and leaving. the best community work happens when you're willing to hold the door open without chasing credit or putting your name in the spotlight.

how does carl maynard balance product messaging with community storytelling?

on guidance counselor 2.0, carl maynard says the product should sit in the background of the story instead of being the main focus. he points out that people already know what sunscreen or a job board is, so they don't need education on the basics. storytelling should put the brand in the environment visually but focus on the person's experience, not claim the product is the reason for their success.

what does carl maynard say about measuring roi on community events?

on guidance counselor 2.0, carl maynard says there is no actual data point you can derive from community and the moment you start expecting a clean roi from an event, people will quickly discover you're in community for the wrong reasons. he believes community is a feeling and a space where people want to be, not a measurable transaction. most of their events don't even have a point of sale.
