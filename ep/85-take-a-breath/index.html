ep 85 · oct 1, 2021

take a breath

with taylor desseyn (solo)

  • if you have a current job, narrow your search to a few real-fit opportunities instead of chasing everything at once
  • be upfront with employers if you need more time to decide, asking for a week is reasonable
  • always ask questions in interviews so you know what the job is really like before accepting
  • after interviews send a quick thank you message so you land back at the top of the hiring manager's inbox
  • journal what you think went wrong after each interview so you can track patterns and improve

questions this episode answers

what does taylor desseyn say about how many jobs to focus on at once if you already have a job?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says if you currently have a full-time job, you should focus on only one to three or four job opportunities instead of trying to pursue everything that comes your way. he recommends treating it as first come, first serve and digging deep into the early opportunities that are a genuine fit. this approach helps reduce the overwhelming feeling of having recruiters, friends, and multiple sources sending you jobs all at once.

how does taylor desseyn suggest asking for more time when you get a job offer but are early in your search?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn recommends being absolutely brutally honest with the employer about where you are in your job search. he suggests saying something like thanking them for moving quickly and having confidence in you, then explaining you are at the beginning of your search and would really like a week to decide. he emphasizes that honesty is the best policy when dealing with recruiters, hiring managers, or hr throughout the entire job search process.

what does taylor desseyn recommend doing after every interview to improve?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn suggests starting a journal where you write down or record where you think you messed up immediately after each interview. he recommends reviewing these notes from every interview so you can spot patterns, like realizing you keep missing questions about a specific topic such as variables. this practice helps you identify what to study or prepare better for the next call and track your improvement over time.
