managing your strengths w/engineering manager, tiffany jachja

guest: tiffany jachja, engineering manager at autodesk

  • know how you want to be remembered as a leader before you set goals, it shapes how you communicate and lead
  • build confidence by finding people who will see your work for what it is, starting in spaces where you feel safe
  • ask why you want more money or a bigger title, the real goal is usually what that unlocks for you, not the title itself

questions this episode answers

what does tiffany jachja say engineering managers should do before setting career goals?

on guidance counselor 2.0, tiffany jachja says you should ask yourself how you want to be remembered as a leader before you start setting goals. she recommends defining what words people will use to describe your time at a company, because that shapes how you communicate and make decisions. she found that wanting to be inspirational and fun guided her leadership style even though it sounded different from others who focused on being diligent or hardworking.

how does tiffany jachja recommend building confidence in the workplace?

on guidance counselor 2.0, tiffany jachja says confidence comes from finding people who will see your work for what it is, starting in spaces where you feel safe. she points to the root word confide and says you need community and people you trust who see what you're doing. you don't have to start by showing your work to your manager or people in tech; begin talking about your work wherever you feel comfortable.

what does tiffany jachja say about why people want promotions and more money?

on guidance counselor 2.0, tiffany jachja says you should ask yourself why you want to earn more money or get a bigger title, because the real reason is usually what that money or title unlocks for you. she explains it might mean being more well known in your industry or being able to support people you care about. asking those deeper questions helps you stop sleepwalking through your career and following a path that isn't really yours.
