you belong in tech w/anna mcdougall

guest: anna mcdougall, director of product and engineering, author

  • break your career change into three steps: learn the technical skills, build a network, then apply to jobs with research instead of spray and pray
  • use a structure like scrum sprints to set weekly or monthly learning goals if you are teaching yourself to code
  • when networking, ask questions and listen instead of pitching, then jot down quick notes so you remember people at the next event
  • say yes to opportunities and actually show up, since consistency and time pay off within six months to a year
  • in applications use the names numbers quotes approach, naming people at the company, citing your achievements, and adding real recommendations

questions this episode answers

what does anna mcdougall say about breaking down a career change into tech?

on guidance counselor 2.0, anna mcdougall says there are three broad steps to changing careers into tech. first, learn the technical skills because you cannot have a technical job without them. second, create a network or find a community of people in similar regions or tech stacks. third, approach job applications with research instead of spray and pray, writing targeted cover letters and resumes.

how does anna mcdougall suggest self-taught developers create structure when learning to code?

on guidance counselor 2.0, anna mcdougall recommends using an agile framework like scrum to create structure when learning by yourself. you work in sprints of one week to one month, set goals at the beginning, work through tasks, then review how much you learned at the end. this approach not only gives you structure but also gets you used to how actual development teams work.

what is anna mcdougall's advice for networking at tech events when you have a bad memory?

on guidance counselor 2.0, anna mcdougall says to go to events, ask questions and really listen instead of pitching yourself constantly. then step away to the bathroom or grab coffee and jot down quick notes about who you spoke to and what they said. write down their name, interests, maybe what they look like, so when you see them at the next event you can pick up the conversation and remember the connection.
