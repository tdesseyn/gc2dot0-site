ep 179 · oct 14, 2022

locker room to board room w/ brian dougherty

guest: brian dougherty, chief operating officer slash chief technology officer at jumpstart health investors

  • respect roles on a team and let specialists do their job instead of micromanaging out of your lane
  • good leaders take ownership of failures and let the team own the wins so people can play loose instead of scared
  • use 360 degree reviews and genuinely open door policies to surface honest feedback about leadership
  • in crunch time look to the teammates next to you for motivation instead of just working for the company or the boss
  • when starting out or leading later, volunteer to try other roles outside your usual lane to build context and value

questions this episode answers

what does brian dougherty say about how leaders should handle team failures versus successes?

on guidance counselor 2.0, brian dougherty says good leaders take responsibility for anything that goes wrong while letting the team own the successes. this approach frees people up to play loose instead of operating from fear. when employees know their manager will insulate them from blame as long as they follow procedures and try their hardest, they avoid tunnel vision and perform more creatively.

how does brian dougherty suggest getting honest feedback about poor leadership from employees?

on guidance counselor 2.0, brian dougherty recommends 360 degree reviews where managers are evaluated by their boss, peers and the people who report to them, allowing feedback to come from all directions. he also says good leaders should end every one-on-one by asking what they can do better and what would make the employee's life easier. strong cultures include genuine open door policies where anyone can speak to senior leaders about manager concerns.

why does brian dougherty tell people to volunteer for roles outside their usual lane?

on guidance counselor 2.0, brian dougherty says playing different positions early in your career helps you discover what you really enjoy and makes you more effective in your main role because you understand how other roles work. even as a manager, shadowing other departments like finance or operations gives you context that makes you more valuable to the organization. jumping in when other teams need help builds appreciation for what teammates do and creates better cross-functional collaboration.
