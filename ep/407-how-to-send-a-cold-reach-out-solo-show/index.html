how to send a cold reach out (solo show)

with taylor desseyn (solo)

  • check your current network first before cold messaging strangers since most jobs come through people you know
  • always submit your resume to the job before dming the hiring manager so you can reference it
  • keep cold dms short and specific, state who you are, your fit, and the clear ask
  • research the person's profile for shared schools, interests, or posts to make a genuine connection before messaging
  • follow up multiple times since people are busy and rarely respond on the first try

questions this episode answers

what does taylor desseyn say about cold outreach message length?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says cold messages must be short and specific because attention spans are limited and people don't have time for long exchanges. he recommends telling the person who you are, why you're messaging them, what skills match the role, and making a clear ask in as few words as possible. he warns that most people send walls of text with no real ask, which get ignored.

should you apply before cold messaging a hiring manager according to taylor desseyn?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says you should always submit your resume to the job posting before messaging a hiring manager. he explains that internal recruiters will ask if you applied as their first question, so submitting first lets you reference your application in the message and shows you followed proper steps. in the message you can then say you submitted your resume and explain why you're a fit.

how many times should you follow up on a cold message according to taylor desseyn?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says you should follow up three, four, five, or even six times if someone doesn't respond. he explains that people are busy and rarely have time to respond immediately, so persistence is necessary. he shares that he personally had to follow up with someone three times before getting a response, and that this level of follow-up is what's required in the current market.
