how to hire properly w/ brian holt, staff pm @ neon.tech

guest: brian holt, staff pm at neon.tech

  • making a costly bad hire is worse than missing out on a good candidate, so slow down and be cautious
  • negative signal is hard to spot in interviews because nervousness can look like a bad fit, so create a comfortable climate for candidates
  • give candidates multiple prompts to choose from so they can show off the skills they feel strongest in
  • let candidates use a real code editor and google things during interviews since that mirrors how engineers actually work
  • research the company before an interview and come with real opinions or critiques about the product, not just praise

questions this episode answers

what does brian holt say about making bad hires versus missing good candidates?

on guidance counselor 2.0, brian holt, staff pm at neon.tech, says it is worse to make a costly bad hire than to miss out on a good candidate. he learned this lesson early at reddit and recommends going slower and being more cautious in the hiring process. the goal is to make sure you get the right person rather than rushing to fill a role.

how does brian holt recommend candidates show their skills in interviews?

on guidance counselor 2.0, brian holt, staff pm at neon.tech, says to give candidates multiple prompts or problems to choose from instead of one fixed assignment. this lets people pick the option that matches their strongest skills and gives them the best opportunity to shine. he also recommends letting candidates use a real code editor and google things during technical interviews since that mirrors how engineers actually work day to day.

what does brian holt say about researching a company before an interview?

on guidance counselor 2.0, brian holt, staff pm at neon.tech, says it is a big red flag when candidates know nothing about the company they are interviewing with. he wants candidates to come in with opinions about the product, even critiques, because it shows they evaluated the product and can think critically. when he interviewed at cloudflare, he brought a ten-page google doc of friction points and pull requests on their repos, which only took him about forty-five minutes to prepare.
