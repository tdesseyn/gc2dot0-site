having a savings account and being healthy in 2024 w/ paul sheriff

guest: paul sheriff, consultant, mentor, and former software company owner

  • build a simple savings split, cover living costs, put money into long-term investing, keep an emergency fund, and save some for fun spending
  • use small tricks like round-up savings on bank accounts or automatic 401k contributions to build savings without thinking about it
  • schedule real breaks in your workday for walking or light exercise since consistent movement helps mental clarity and long-term health
  • grow your network by stepping outside your comfort zone through user groups, volunteering, writing, or speaking since those build real relationships
  • stand out in a job search by communicating clearly, answering calls professionally, and always including your full name and contact info in emails

questions this episode answers

what does paul sheriff recommend for building savings as a tech professional?

on guidance counselor 2.0, paul sheriff recommends dividing income into buckets: live on 50 percent of your salary, put 30 percent into long-term investing and savings, keep 10 percent for emergencies, and use 10 percent for fun spending. he suggests automatic savings tricks like bank round-up features that move spare change into savings or 401k contributions that come straight from your paycheck. sheriff also mentions putting physical change into a bucket and depositing it later, which can add up to hundreds of dollars.

how does paul sheriff say physical activity helps with programming and mental clarity?

on guidance counselor 2.0, paul sheriff says walking three to four miles daily and doing light weights every other day keeps him healthy and mentally sharp. he recommends taking at least a 45-minute walk during lunch breaks because stepping away in the middle of the day helps solve programming problems. sheriff notes that when he returns from a walk, problems he struggled with before often become clear, and he emphasizes that never stopping movement is key to longevity.

what networking advice does paul sheriff give to consultants and freelancers?

on guidance counselor 2.0, paul sheriff says the key to networking is getting out of your comfort zone by attending user groups, volunteering for charity work, and speaking or writing about technical topics. he points out that charity work connects you with executives who need help, and becoming an expert through writing or speaking brings clients to you. sheriff emphasizes that explaining topics to others forces you to learn them deeply and positions you as an expert people want to hire.
