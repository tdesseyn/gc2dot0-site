negotiations, career progression, & ensuring fairness w/ ingrid epure

guest: ingrid epure, senior staff engineer at netlify

  • always negotiate, whether it is initial job offers, day to day role decisions, or career progression discussions

  • it is okay for your career path to look different over time, lateral moves and team switches are normal and valuable

questions this episode answers

what does ingrid epure say about negotiating job offers?

on guidance counselor 2.0, ingrid epure says you should always negotiate, including initial job offers, day-to-day role decisions, and career progression discussions. she emphasizes that the best negotiation advice is to never underestimate how much the other person will go to battle for you if they like you and can relate to you personally. she recommends asking questions, listening carefully, and not being afraid to show vulnerability, which is actually a sign of strength and confidence in negotiations.

what does ingrid epure recommend about career paths in tech?

on guidance counselor 2.0, ingrid epure says it is okay for your career to look different throughout the years and that lateral moves and team switches are normal and valuable. she started in testing and quality assurance before moving into backend and distributed systems, showing that your interests can change. she advises setting longer term learning goals tied to industry trends rather than comparing your progress to other people's timelines, and emphasizes that you should focus on what you want and where you want to go instead of comparing yourself to others.

what does ingrid epure say companies should do to ensure fairness for minorities in tech?

on guidance counselor 2.0, ingrid epure says there is nothing more attractive to minorities than seeing other minorities at a company succeeding at all levels, which means more than any diversity campaign or event. she emphasizes that making sure minorities have a fair and equal chance at getting promoted and being represented at all levels is crucial. she also warns against focusing disproportionately on soft skills for minorities instead of valuing their technical skills, and recommends giving minorities challenging, promotable work instead of just glue work.
