ep 249 · may 3, 2023

how to be better w/my colleagues nate walker and joseph jewell

guest: nate walker and joseph jewell, colleagues on taylor's global solutions team

  • shorten your hiring process since time kills deals, decide within the first part of the interview and spend the rest on the real job needs
  • consider contract to hire instead of direct hire if your process is too slow and you risk losing talent
  • recruiters can act as advisors, not just order takers, by sharing honest feedback even when it is not what the client wants to hear
  • when changing careers, network constantly, practice interviewing, and be honest about your real capabilities instead of overselling
  • use time blocking on your calendar to protect focus for prospecting, research, or certifications so you actually get deep work done

questions this episode answers

what does nate walker say hiring managers should do about their interview process?

on guidance counselor 2.0, nate walker says hiring managers should shorten their process because time kills deals. he explains that you know within the first 10 to 15 minutes whether you like a candidate, so spend the rest of the time diving into actual job needs instead of running four or five rounds. if direct hire is slowing things down and you're losing talent, consider contract to hire instead.

how should someone change careers according to joseph jewell?

on guidance counselor 2.0, joseph jewell says you should network constantly, practice interviewing, and prepare to discuss what you've done to gain experience for the change. he recommends doing certifications and side projects and being able to showcase them well. be persistent because the only reason you'll be defeated is if you give up.

what does nate walker say about honesty when interviewing for entry level jobs?

on guidance counselor 2.0, nate walker says be honest about your capabilities instead of overselling. he explains that when people try to oversell and say things that don't make sense, managers are put off. if you go into an interview and honestly share your background, interests, and what you've done to prepare, managers will pick up on that much better than if you try to fluff your way through.
