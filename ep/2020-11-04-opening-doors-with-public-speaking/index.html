opening doors with public speaking

guest: michael richardson, senior software developer at kroger

  • submit your first conference talk even if you expect it to get rejected, just to start the process
  • treat public speaking as a long game, expect rejection and keep improving your submissions each time
  • when picking a topic, think about whether it fits the audience and not just what you personally love
  • talks generally fall into technical deep dives, high level overviews for managers and beginners, or people skills topics
  • speaking and organizing conferences builds a network of organizers and speakers that opens up new opportunities

questions this episode answers

what does michael richardson recommend for someone who wants to start speaking at conferences?

on guidance counselor 2.0, michael richardson says to submit your first conference talk even if you expect it to get rejected, just to start the process. he submitted his first talk knowing it almost certainly wouldn't get accepted, but he told himself he needed to have a first submission to begin that path. richardson submitted half a dozen conference talks before one was accepted, and he emphasizes that public speaking is a long game where you get a little better with each submission.

how should a developer choose a topic for a conference talk according to michael richardson?

on guidance counselor 2.0, michael richardson says to choose something you're passionate about and that is applicable to the actual conference audience. he warns against picking topics that don't fit the event, like talking about collecting funko pops at a developer conference. richardson and taylor desseyn agree that talks generally fall into three buckets: incredibly technical deep dives, high level overviews for managers and beginners, or people skills topics.

what career opportunities does michael richardson say come from conference speaking?

on guidance counselor 2.0, michael richardson says speaking at conferences builds a network of organizers and other speakers that opens up new opportunities. he explains that by making connections through speaking, he got the chance to work with a manager at kroger who was starting a conference, and when that manager moved to portland, richardson took over organizing the conference. he emphasizes that taking advantage of small opportunities leads to bigger things.
