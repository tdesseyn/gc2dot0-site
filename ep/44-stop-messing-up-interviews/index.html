ep 44 · jun 21, 2021

stop. messing. up. interviews.

with taylor desseyn (solo)

  • research the company before an interview, not knowing basic facts about them is a red flag
  • ask questions about the role, including why it is open and who you would be working with
  • try to interview directly with the person you would report to, not just their team members
  • write down your accomplishments and relevant project examples ahead of time so you do not blank under pressure
  • bring a notepad to jot down thoughts during the interview instead of interrupting the interviewer

questions this episode answers

what does taylor desseyn say about researching a company before an interview?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says failing to research the company before an interview is a major red flag. he argues that if you don't care enough to spend time learning about the company you're interviewing with, it shows a lack of commitment. he emphasizes that this basic preparation is non-negotiable and signals whether you're serious about the opportunity.

what questions should you ask during an interview according to taylor desseyn?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn recommends asking why the position is open, whether someone was fired and why, and who you'll be working with. he stresses that you should request to interview with the person you'll directly report to, not just their team members. he also suggests asking to speak with two other people on the team you'll be working with before accepting an offer, noting that this request won't hurt your candidacy.

how should you prepare accomplishments for an interview according to taylor desseyn?

in this guidance counselor 2.0 solo episode, taylor desseyn says you should write down or type up your accomplishments and relevant project examples before the interview and keep them close by. he explains that people often blank when asked about specific technical experiences or projects under pressure. he recommends even chicken scratch notes, not a detailed report, so you have something to reference when the interviewer asks about particular skills or experiences.
