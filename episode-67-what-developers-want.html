what developers want

3 takeaways

  • > offer developers both a leadership track and an individual contributor track so strong coders are not forced into managing people.
  • > let senior individual contributors own a specific product area and keep advancing without becoming a manager.
  • > build mentorship opportunities into career growth since many developers want to teach without taking on full leadership.

about this episode

on this episode of guidance counselor 2.0, hosted by taylor desseyn: how to keep developers engaged through mentorship, individual contributor tracks, and recognition instead of forcing them into leadership roles.

offer mentorship opportunities separate from management so developers can teach and influence without leading teams. recognize and affirm developers regularly, even for small wins, to build retention and engagement. let experienced developers tinker and learn new skills on the job as part of their growth. accept when leaders step back to individual contributor roles after burnout instead of viewing it as failure. give real affirmations and specific guidance instead of vague criticism to help retain your team. offer developers both a leadership track and an individual contributor track so strong coders are not forced into managing people. let senior individual contributors own a specific product area and keep advancing without becoming a manager. build mentorship opportunities into career growth since many developers want to teach without taking on full leadership.
