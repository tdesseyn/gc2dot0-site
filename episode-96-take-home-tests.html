take home tests

oct 27, 2021 · 18 min · hosted by taylor desseyn

3 takeaways

  • > long take home tests hurt retention more than they help, especially when candidates already have jobs and families.
  • > ask better interview questions instead of leaning on hours of take home code tests to evaluate someone.

about this episode

on this episode of guidance counselor 2.0, hosted by taylor desseyn: why take-home coding tests hurt hiring and what to do instead.

three interview rounds in two weeks is enough; seven rounds wastes everyone's time. companies asking for portfolios or github links in job postings would help candidates stand out. volunteer work on real projects builds better experience than take-home test results. keep the process tight, aim for around three rounds within two weeks with fast turnaround on scheduling and feedback. build experience by volunteering a few hours a week on a real project and adding that work to your resume. long take home tests hurt retention more than they help, especially when candidates already have jobs and families. ask better interview questions instead of leaning on hours of take home code tests to evaluate someone. live coding or screen share problem solving together is a fair way to assess skills without a take home burden.
