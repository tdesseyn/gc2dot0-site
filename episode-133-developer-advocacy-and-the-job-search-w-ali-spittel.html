about this episode

on this episode of guidance counselor 2.0, taylor desseyn talks with ali spittel: developer advocacy bridges product and community by earning customer trust at scale through people with deep engineering experience and teaching ability.

build a personal portfolio site showing projects you care about, not generic todo apps, to stand out in hiring. teach what you learn through blogging, videos, or talks to prove deep understanding and show communication skills employers want. do the job before you get the job title by creating content and building community presence in your free time first. developer advocates code daily building demos and dogfooding products, not just marketing fluff, requiring senior-level engineering skills. bootcamp grads especially need portfolio projects that solve real problems you actually want to work on long term. do the job before you get the job by creating content, speaking, or building demos in the space you want to break into. keep team morale up by sharing positive user feedback internally, not just flagging what is broken.
